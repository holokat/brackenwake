# Three ways forward

Written after concluding the current game has no real decisions in it. That
conclusion is correct. This is about what to do with 30,000 lines that are
mostly not the problem.

## The inventory, honestly

```
30,651 lines total
    ~500  the layer that decides what the player chooses    <- this is broken
 ~30,000  world, rendering, content, audio, story engine    <- this is fine
```

Five 3D biomes with weather, day/night and seasons on a wall clock. A hundred
buildings. Forty six recipes. Thirty six fish. An adaptive audio system. And a
story engine with sixty one cards where a hundred and twenty seven of a hundred
and thirty choices do something real, verified by a committed harness.

Rules are the cheapest thing in a game to change. Worlds are the most expensive.
Everything expensive here is done.

## The one genuinely rare asset

The story system. Characters who remember what you did two seasons ago,
promises you can fail, reputation that gates later cards, consequences that
arrive a season late.

No farming game has this. It is currently decoration on a game with no core,
which is exactly the wrong way round.

**Whatever comes next should make that the centre, not the garnish.**

---

# Option A: repoint what exists

Keep the game, change the rules. Two to three weeks.

- A turn is a week. Daylight is the action budget, so winter is fewer decisions.
- Biomes get real modifiers, so the opening choice matters and replaying is a
  different game.
- Land is finite and a committed plot is refused to everything else.
- Spoilage, so preserving is the point of crafting rather than a value ladder.
- The Ledger, once a season.

**Get:** a real game out of what is here, quickly.
**Risk:** it is still a farm game, and you already know how you feel about that.

---

# Option B: the valley is people, not crops

The story engine becomes the game loop rather than an interruption.

You are not farming. **You are the reason a valley works.** People arrive, one
or two a season. Each has a name, one thing they are good at, needs, and an
opinion of you that persists. Land is finite, winter is fixed, and there is
never enough.

Every season you decide who gets land, who gets fed first, who is sent to the
hard work, and who is turned away. **None of those has a correct answer**, which
is Sid's definition, and unlike a shop it never stops being true.

The farm sim becomes the substrate. Crops, animals, buildings, weather and
biomes all stay exactly as they are and become the thing the decisions are
*about* rather than the thing you do.

The ending is not a score. It is whether the valley became a community or your
possession, and the people tell you which.

**Get:** the rarest thing you built becomes the point. Every decision is a real
decision, permanently. It is epic in the way a homestead cannot be: a place full
of people who depend on you.
**Reuse:** near total. The story engine, characters, reputation, pledges and
modifiers are the core rather than a feature.
**Cost:** four to six weeks. Mostly a new decision layer, no new world.

---

# Option C: the greening

The terraforming pitch. A dead world, finite seeds, a thousand years, and the
camera pulling back three times.

**Get:** genuinely epic, and it answers every one of his principles without
contortion.
**Reuse:** terrain, growth, weather, seasons and the story engine port. The
hundred buildings, the animals, the recipes and the fishing largely do not.
**Cost:** it is a new game that borrows an engine. Months.

---

# What I would do

**Option B.**

Not because the others are bad, but because it is the only one where the
question "what is this game about" has an answer that is not about numbers going
up. And because it makes the best thing you built into the point, instead of
throwing it away or leaving it as flavour.

It also fails fast. The core of B is a season screen, a list of people, and a
set of impossible choices. That is prototypable in a few days **without touching
the 3D at all**, on top of the existing save. If it is not gripping as a text
mockup, it will not be gripping in 3D either, and you will have lost a week
rather than a season.

That is the test I would run before committing to anything: build the decision
layer alone, ugly, and see whether you want to press next.
