# D1: the underground, made solid and made big

The user's words: "dungeons have see-through walls, and they need to be much
larger and spacious".

Owned files: `src/world/dungeon_gen.js`, `src/world/dungeon.js`, their
`*.test.mjs`, and this document. Nothing else was touched. `world_runtime.js`,
`monsters.js`, `camera.js` and `main.js` are unchanged, and their suites are
green without an edit.

## 1. Why the walls were see-through

Two causes, both shipped, and the second one was worse than the first.

**A face pointed one way and was drawn the other.** A wall face was built only
toward a neighbouring floor cell, on a `MeshStandardMaterial` at three's default
`side: FrontSide`. So the wall between the camera and the room had its one drawn
side facing away from the camera and was culled.

**And half of them were not even drawn toward the room.** The quad that spans a
wall face used the same corner order whichever way the face pointed, so its
GEOMETRIC winding was `+x` for every x-facing wall and `-z` for every z-facing
wall, regardless of which side the room was on. three culls and flips normals by
the winding, never by the normal attribute. Under `FrontSide` that meant every
`-x` and every `+z` wall face in the game was drawn only from inside the solid
rock, and its lighting normal disagreed with its geometry as well.

**There was no ceiling.** The old comment argued a roof would be all the orbit
camera ever saw. That is true and the answer is to keep the camera under the
roof, not to leave the level open.

### What changed

- Walls are `side: THREE.DoubleSide`.
- `quad()` now reverses its own winding whenever the winding disagrees with the
  normal the caller asked for. This is in the one shared helper, so floors,
  roof, walls, pillars, chests, braziers, bones and rubble are all correct by
  construction. `box()` had every one of its six faces wound backwards too, so
  every chest and brazier would have rendered inside out; that is fixed by the
  same change.
- There is a full ceiling.

### Measured, in `dungeon.test.mjs`

- On a whole dungeon level and a whole cave level, a ray at chest height from
  the floor cell beside each wall face hits it: 844/844 and 666/666 faces, every
  one at the cell boundary and not at some wall further along.
- A ray fired from four metres out in the rock back toward the room finds that
  same wall face: 771/771 and 428/428 wall cells blocked. Sampled one in seven
  over the other 22 levels: 2136/2136 faces inside, 1842/1842 cells outside.
- **The regression runs both ways.** Set the wall material back to `FrontSide`
  and the outside ray sees straight in on 154 of 154 sampled wall cells; put
  `DoubleSide` back and all 154 close again. A test that cannot fail on the bug
  it was written for proves nothing, so this one is driven true and false.
- A winding audit over every triangle of every level: 0 of 323,402 wound against
  their own normal.

## 2. The new layout numbers

`CELL` is still 2 m. Every number below is measured by `dungeon_gen.test.mjs`
over 80 levels and printed when it runs.

| | old | new |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `MAX_GRID` | 48 | **96** |
| dungeon grid, L1 / L2 / L3 | 30..48 | **64..77 / 70..83 / 76..89** cells (128..178 m) |
| cave grid | 26..40 | **62..73** cells (124..146 m) |
| dungeon room side | 3..8 cells | **6..14** cells (12..28 m) |
| cave cavern side | 3..6 cells | **8..18** cells (16..36 m) |
| rooms per level | 5..9 | **7..11** dungeon, **5..7** cave |
| corridor width | 1 cell | **2 cells (4 m)** |
| floor cells per level | a few hundred | **871..2136** |

New layout fields, all additive:

- `layout.corridorW` = 2.
- `room.kind`: `'entry'` (room 0), `'boss'` (the great hall, bottom level only),
  `'hall'` (the far room on the levels above, and any room 11+ cells a side),
  `'room'`. This is the contract `monster_ai.js normalizeDungeonLayout` already
  reads. The labelled boss room is the room that file would have picked by
  distance anyway, so no monster moved; the label only stops the geometry and
  the roster disagreeing about which room is deep.
- `roomAt(layout, gx, gz)` is exported: the room a cell is in, or null for a
  corridor.

Kept exactly: `generateDungeon(seed, site, level)` and
`createDungeonScene(THREE, layout, opts)` signatures; `rooms` as
`{ x, z, w, h, kind, cx, cz }` on the cell grid; `w`, `h`, `cellSize`, `kind`,
`level`, `top`, `entrance`, `stair`, `ore`, `chests`, `id`; `clampToWalkable`
semantics; the exits' hit boxes (2.6 x 3.0 x 2.6, invisible, `userData.exit`) at
the cells `world_runtime.js` picks them from.

Entrance and stair rules are unchanged in substance: the entrance is the centre
of room 0, the stair is the centre of the far room. The far room is now the hall,
so the long walk and the big room are the same walk. On 3 of 120 levels the
grown great hall's centre moves far enough that it is no longer the strictly
furthest room by centre distance; the label wins there, deliberately.

Measured: 0 unreachable rooms, 0 sealed pockets, 0 unsealed rim over 80 levels.
0 one-cell pinches in 29,608 dungeon corridor cells. A cave's passages are
ragged by design and the cross-section metric finds 1,387 single-cell nooks off
wide passages, so the test asks the honest question instead: a body two cells
wide reaches every room and the stair on 80/80 levels.

Generation: **0.41 ms a level** (80 levels in 33 ms). Geometry: **36.8 ms a
level** with a stub canvas.

## 3. The roof, and the look

Ceiling height is a property of the cell: 4 m corridor, 6 m room, 7 m hall, 8 m
great hall in a dungeon (measured and asserted); in a cave a dome from 4 m at the
cavern rim to the peak over its middle, with a hand of hash roughness. Measured
on a cave great cavern: 3.65 m at the rim to 8.76 m at the peak.

The roof is built from **corner** heights, not cell heights: `cornerCeil(i, j)`
is the lowest ceiling of the floor cells touching that corner. Neighbouring
cells share corners, so the surface is continuous with no lintel geometry
anywhere, and the top edge of a wall face reads the same two corners as the roof
quad beside it. Measured: 0 of 49,938 wall vertices above the floor were not
also a roof vertex.

The roof's one drawn side faces **down**. From below it is a roof; from above it
is not there. That is deliberate: a build that has not yet called `cameraClamp`
looks into the level from above exactly as the roofless version did, rather than
at a black lid. Both directions are asserted.

Also new, all in `dungeon.js`:

- Generated 512 px stone sheets, four families: `blocks` (coursed ashlar with
  mortar, dungeon walls), `flags` (dungeon floor and roof), `rock` (cave walls
  and roof), `gravel` (cave floor). Each is an albedo drawn beside a height
  field; the normal map and the roughness map are both read off that height, so
  mortar is recessed and rougher without two drawings to keep in step. Cached
  per family on the module and shared by every level; a level's `dispose()` does
  not take them (asserted).
- `setDungeonCanvasFactory(fn)` and `stubCanvasFactory(w, h)` for node. The
  tests inject the stub, so geometry and placement are exercised with real
  materials and blank pixels.
- Engaged pillars along hall walls, sunk 0.62 m into the rock so a player
  brushing the wall does not walk through a post the grid says is not there.
- Rubble heaps and bones. Chests are proper chests: an oak carcass, a barrel lid
  built as a fan of staves, two iron bands and a lock plate.
- Iron braziers standing in halls and sconces on the walls. Both are light
  stands and both go into the same `torches` array and through the same spacing
  rule, so the light pool sees all of them.
- Dark water planes in cave caverns, never over the entrance or the stair
  (asserted). Measured: 6/6 caves, 399 cells of water.
- Fog widened for the bigger rooms: dungeon 6..46 m (was 5..34), cave 5..40
  (was 4..28). A `HemisphereLight` fill was added so an unlit corner is dark and
  not black; it is not a point light and costs the budget nothing.

Light budget unchanged at 8: six roaming plus entrance plus stair. `TORCH_RANGE`
is now 10 m and `TORCH_SPACING` 6 cells, and the test measures the closest pair
of light stands anywhere at **10.56 m**, which is more than the reach, so no
torch can wink as the pool moves. Per level: **7 or 8 point lights**,
**10,063..19,651 triangles**, 35..77 torches.

## 4. `cameraClamp`, for main.js

```js
import { cameraClamp } from '../world/dungeon.js';

// in the follow-camera step, AFTER camera.js has placed the camera
const L = runtime.dungeonLayout();          // null above ground
if (L) {
  const c = cameraClamp(L, camera.position, player.pos);
  camera.position.set(c.x, c.y, c.z);
  camera.lookAt(target);                    // the same target camera.js used
}
```

Pure: plain objects in, `{ x, y, z, moved }` out, no THREE. A null layout is a
no-op, so it is safe to call unconditionally.

It walks the segment from the player to the camera with an exact grid DDA
(`cellsCrossed`, also exported) and stops the camera `CAM_MARGIN` (0.5 m) short
of the first rock cell, then holds it `CAM_CEIL_GAP` (0.4 m) under the ceiling of
wherever it ended up and `CAM_MIN_Y` (0.9 m) above the floor. The walk is a DDA
and not a march at some step size because a step of any size skips the cells a
segment only clips the corner of.

Measured, both directions:

- A camera with a clear line and headroom comes back untouched, bit for bit,
  with `moved: false`.
- A camera 4 m behind a wall is pulled from 15.00 m to 10.50 m and lands on a
  floor cell.
- 21,168 camera positions across every level at every yaw, at the six distances
  and three pitches `camera.js` can produce: 0 left in the rock, 0 above the
  roof, 0 under the floor, and 7,172 of them left exactly alone.
- A camera at y = 40 drops to 5.60 m under a 6.00 m roof; one at y = -4 rises to
  0.90 m.

`camera.js` is untouched and still has no wall test; this is the wall test, and
it belongs to main.js to call.

## 5. `update(dt, playerPos)`

`createDungeonScene(...).update` now takes `(dt, pos)` and drives a warm flicker
on the six roaming lights and the entrance light. The cold blue light over the
stair is deliberately steady, and the test asserts that too.

The old one-argument `update(pos)` still works and takes its dt off the wall
clock, so `world_runtime.js` needs no change and the flame still moves in a build
that has not been rewired. **When world_runtime.js is next touched, change
`dungeon.scene.update({ x, z })` to `dungeon.scene.update(dt, { x, z })`** so the
flicker is driven by the game clock rather than by `performance.now()`.

`built.parts` is new: `{ floor, ceiling, wallA, wallB, walls: [...], pillars,
rubble, bones, iron, flames }`, named meshes so a test can raycast the walls
without the props in the way. `built.stats` carries the measured counts.

## What is NOT verified

- **Nothing here has been seen on a screen.** There is no browser in this
  session. Every claim above is a number out of `npm test`, not a screenshot.
  Colour, the readability of the stone sheets, whether a 28 m hall lit by six
  10 m torches is atmospheric or simply dark, and whether the domed cave ceiling
  reads as rock or as a lens are all unverified and want Fable's eyes.
- **No pixel of the texture sheets has been looked at.** The test does drive all
  four families through a recording 2D context that throws on a coordinate which
  is not a number, so the calls are real and counted (blocks 1,304 drawing
  calls, flags 2,844, rock 12,346, gravel 14,746, each on four 512 px canvases),
  but a sheet that draws 12,000 correct circles can still look like porridge.
  Nobody has seen one.
- **`cameraClamp` is not called by anything yet.** It is exported, tested and
  documented; until main.js calls it the camera still climbs above the roof, and
  the roof being one-sided-down is what keeps that survivable.
- **Performance in the real renderer is unmeasured.** 10k to 20k triangles and 8
  point lights per level is a reasonable budget on paper; no frame has been
  drawn.
- The monster layer was not re-tuned for the bigger rooms. `groupsForRoom` scales
  a room's group count by its area and caps at `ROOM_GROUPS_MAX` 3, and
  `DUNGEON_CAP` 24 still bounds what is alive, so the bigger rooms mostly hit
  those caps. Whether that is the right density for a 28 m hall is a design
  question for G3, not a bug here.
