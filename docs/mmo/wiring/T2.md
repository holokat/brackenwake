# T2: the castle in every town

Wave B, task T2. Written after the work, with every number this file quotes
measured by `node src/world/town_layout.test.mjs` on world seed 20260904.

The question that started it was "where can I find castles?", and the honest
answer was: five of them, all rolled, the nearest 5 km from the start, because
`structures.js` only rolls a `castle` beside a rolled village. Rule 5 of the
look book asks for a castle with a keep and rule 9 for a tower above the
roofline, and the sheet calls Coldseat a seat, the Last Well a seat and
Cinderport the Legion's own. So all seven authored towns have one now.

## What is there now

Every one of the seven has a castle precinct at its back: a quarter of the
walled ground, closed off by its own wall, with a gate onto the street that
runs to the square, a courtyard inside that gate, a great tower between 19 and
28 m, a great hall, two corner towers, and banners in the realm's colour on the
gate, the tower, the hall and two poles in the court. No two of the seven are
built the same way.

## The decisions worth arguing with

**The keep is a precinct, not a building.** `structures.js` has a rolled
`castle`: a keep behind an eight sided curtain wall 48 m across, with corner
towers and a gatehouse. None of its pieces were reused and two things stopped
them. It builds from `structures.js`'s own `M.stone`, `M.slate`, `M.iron`
material set, which is not in a town's palette, so importing it would have cost
a draw call per colour in every town and made all seven castles the same grey
in seven different realms. And its curtain wall is a free standing ring: dropped
into a town it would have been a wall inside a wall with a street between them.
So the town's castle takes the back quarter of the ground the town wall already
encloses, and the town wall is its outer face. That is also what a capital in
the screenshots looks like: the keep is up against the back of the city, not a
donut in the middle of it.

**A quarter, as wide as the streets allow.** The sector runs from just outside
the ring street (`rIn` = 20.2 m) to the town wall (66 m). Its area is
`half * (rOut^2 - rIn^2)`, so a quarter of the walled ground wants a half angle
of 49.7 degrees. It is then clamped so the castle's flank walls never cross an
avenue: the nearest street, less the angle its own kerb takes at `rIn`. Six of
the seven lose half a degree to that and land at 24.8 to 25.0 per cent.
Cinderport lands at 21.0, because its harbour mouth stands 45 degrees off the
back and the citadel sits in what the two leave it. That number is measured
twice: once from the sector's own arithmetic and once by counting a 0.75 m grid
over the walled ground, and the two agree to a hundredth in all seven.

**The castle is centred in the gap, not dead opposite the main gate.** Straight
opposite would have given Cinderport a keep of 17.2 per cent squeezed against
its own quay. It now sits in the middle of the ground its streets leave at the
back, which is dead opposite for six of the seven and 7.5 degrees off it for
Cinderport.

**The gate a castle keeps for itself.** Where a town gate already points at the
castle's ground, the castle does not fight it: that gate becomes its own outer
gate, the avenue runs through the courtyard, and the great tower stands to one
side of the road rather than across it. Hearthhome's north gate and the Red
Queen's harbour mouth are both that. Everywhere else the town wall closes the
back and a new `keepway` street is cut from the square to the castle's gate.
Measured: every one of the seven gates stands on its own inner wall, and a
street reaches all seven of them with its other end on the square.

**The Red Queen's is a sea fort because of where her gates are, not because it
was told to be.** Her land gates are forced round to 285 and 292 degrees by the
harbour mouth, so the back of her town is the water. The keep's outer arc is
therefore open water for 96 degrees of its 99, and it closes it with its own sea
wall and a water gate on the quay's own axis, 12 m of arc, one hull's beam and
a margin. Every load that lands at the Red Queen's Harbour passes under her
fort. Cinderport's citadel gets 44 degrees of the same sea wall on its harbour
side.

**Two ways a castle can fail, and both of them speak.** A wall smaller than 46 m
has no room behind the ring street, and a precinct that is wide enough but will
not hold the bodies is refused whole rather than built as half a castle. Both
write `plan.keepNote` and both are driven in the test with a shrunken precinct:
at `flatR` 74 the wall is 44 m and the note reads "the wall stands at 44.0 m and
a keep needs 46 m, so there is no castle here"; at `flatR` 76 it is "the keep
could not be stood up, 1 of the two corner towers would not fit inside its
wall". In both the town is still a legal town, with its wall, its four gates and
one of every named building, and not one keep lot or `keepway` is left behind.

**One bug found on the way, and it was a class.** The Last Well grew a palm tree
in the inn's doorway the first time the castle moved anything. `npcs_runtime`
stands its five anchored people 2.0 m out from their own front wall and the
packer leaves 1.6 m of daylight between two lots, so the doorstep was never
anybody's. It is now: `DOOR_CLEAR` blocks 2.2 m in front of every named
building and of the castle's tower and hall, and the test drives it over all 49
doors in the seven. Without the fix `npcs_runtime.test.mjs` failed on "nobody is
standing inside any building at all"; with it, both files pass.

**The Canopy Court's ninth giant moved into the castle.** With a quarter of the
walled ground gone the ninth trunk had nowhere to stand, so the row is eight now
and the Speaker's Tree is the ninth: a bole 45 m to the crown with the hall
built round its foot. It is the one town whose keep is not the tallest thing in
it, because its own trunks stand 34 to 50 m, and the test says so on purpose.

T1 left the seven at 32 or 33 draws. The castle costs five or six more, and five
of those are the gate, which is merged on its own. The budget is 60.

- **The keep shows over the roofline.** From a point 200 m outside the main gate
  at eye height 1.7 m, a ray is cast at the tower against the town's own
  triangles, wall, gates, houses, trunks and all. In all seven the first thing
  the ray meets is the tower. It is aimed a metre UNDER the top of the shaft, so
  the claim covers the top metre of the tower and everything above it and not
  just the tip. Cairnfoot is the one that had to be argued with: the ground 200 m
  outside its gate is 17 m below the town, which flattens the line, and a house
  ridge stopped it at 20 m of tower. Its tower is 25 m now and clears by 2.5 m.
- **And the measurement can fail.** Aimed at the tower's foot instead of its top
  the same line is stopped short in all seven, so the sky it is measuring is not
  an empty one.
- **The castle's ground is the castle's.** 87,746 points, a 0.5 m grid over every
  lot in all seven, asked which side of the castle wall they are on. No lot puts
  a square half metre inside the castle and no part of a castle stands outside
  its own wall. `keepFit` is driven all three ways as well: inside, outside, and
  across the wall, including a 40 m body that steps over the sector's corner
  with all four of its own corners outside it.
- **Nothing else moved.** No two lots overlap anywhere in the seven (7,102 pairs,
  0 overlaps), the square is still empty (nearest corner 19.5 m), every named
  building is still there exactly once, every gate is still on the wall, the
  layout is still deterministic through JSON, and every rolled town still gets
  `null`.
- **The courtyard is open ground the way the square is**, 8 m across, in all
  seven, and no corner of any lot is inside one.
- **The keep is the tallest thing in six of the seven towns**, measured by
  finding the highest vertex in the built town and asking whether it stands
  inside the castle. The Canopy Court is the exception and is meant to be.
- **`userData.keep` survives the merge and does not spread.** The gate is built,
  merged and tagged on its own, exactly as the waystone is. Five or six meshes
  per town carry the flag out of 37 to 39, and every one of them is within 8 m
  of the gate it belongs to. Merged with the town it would have sat on every
  stone body in the place.
- **The one town the roads reach still works.** `site_models.buildSiteMarker`
  reads `opts.field` and hands the gates their real road bearings, and
  `world_runtime` does pass the field, so the running game's Canopy Court has
  two gates and not three. Laid out that way it still gets its castle, all four
  bodies, at 25.0 per cent.

## Wiring Fable has to do

**1. Somebody at the castle gate, in `src/game/npcs_runtime.js`.** No role owns
a castle yet, and one line puts one at its door once `castellan` exists in
`src/mmo/npcs.js` with `appearsIn: ['town']`:

```js
export const TOWN_ANCHOR = {
  innkeeper: 'inn', blacksmith: 'forge', healer: 'healer',
  stablemaster: 'pens', banker: 'bank', castellan: 'greathall',
};
```

`greathall` and not the gate, because `TOWN_ANCHOR` reads lot kinds through
`lotOf(plan, kind)` and the castle's gate is not a lot, and because the hall's
door is the right place to be found: measured, that stand is 8.7 to 13.7 m
inside the castle and 17 to 19 m in from its gate, which is across the
courtyard. A castellan there is seen from the gate and is not standing in it.

If the gate itself is wanted instead, the plan carries it and `streetFor` needs
four lines rather than one:

```js
    if (plan?.keep && role.id === 'castellan') {
      const g = plan.keep.gate;
      spot = { x: g.x + Math.sin(g.yaw) * 2.4, z: g.z + Math.cos(g.yaw) * 2.4, yaw: g.yaw };
    }
```

`g.yaw` already looks back into the town, so that stands the castellan two and a
half metres inside his own arch, facing the street.

Either way `auditNpcSpots` will want `castellan` in `ROLE_TINT`, and
`win_talk.auditTalk` will refuse a role whose panel is nothing but a Talk tab,
which is what caught the Banker in T1. A castellan who trains nothing and buys
nothing needs a service before he can boot.

**2. A click on the castle gate, in `src/game/world_runtime.js`, in `pick()`.**
The gate meshes carry `userData.keep` and `userData.site`, the same shape the
waystone uses, and the pick already finds them. The line beside the waystone's:

```js
          if (site) { cands.push({ d: h.distance, out: { kind: 'site', site, waystone: !!h.object.userData.waystone, keep: !!h.object.userData.keep } }); break; }
```

Then `interact.js` can say the castle's name and what walking in gets you. The
built town also carries `g.userData.keep`, which is the plan's precinct plus
`top`, the world point at the top of the tower, for anything that wants to point
a camera or a compass at it.

## Known, and not mine

`layoutTown` uses whatever `opts.bearings` gives it and nothing more, so a town
the roads reach with one link gets one gate, and T1's promise of two to four is
only kept when nobody passes bearings. Measured on the real field: only the
Canopy Court has links at all, it has two, and its castle is fine with two
gates. If a road is ever added to a second town with a single link, the fallback
belongs in the `bearings` branch of `layoutTown` and not in the castle.
