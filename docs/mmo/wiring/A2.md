## What stands on the roads now

The roads were graded strips of packed earth with nothing on them. Measured on
`createWorldField(20260904)`, the world `world_runtime.js` builds, they now
carry 966 pieces of furniture over 12,111 m of road on 22 roads:

| kind | how many | where |
|---|---|---|
| lamp | 803 | both verges, every 24 m near a place, every 60 m out, and one at each end of every bridge |
| bridge | 26 | every river crossing that carries the road under water or drops it more than 1.2 m |
| sign | 33 | one at every settlement a road touches, just outside the pad, 2 to 4 fingers each |
| bench | 41 | at the road within 40 m of where it leaves a settlement |
| hitch | 41 | the hitching post, the same |
| milestone | 10 | every 500 m from the settlement the road belongs to |
| gate | 6 | where a road crosses the middle of a realm's edge band, 8 m tall, the realm's name on the lintel |
| shrine | 6 | one sign spot in three |

Every mesh carries `userData.wayside = { kind, realm }`.

## Three readings of the brief, and why

**"Signposts at every road fork and junction."** Taken as: a signpost at every
settlement a road touches. There is exactly ONE settlement in this world with
three roads and nine with two, so signs only at forks would have been nine
signs on a continent and a player would never meet one. A road's end at a town
IS a junction of the road network, and it is the place a traveller reads a
board. So there are 33 signs, nine of them at forks, and a fork's sign says so
(`rec.fork`) and carries a finger down each road that meets there. A road's
plain end carries one finger down its own road and one or two across country to
the nearest named places in directions the road does not take, so no post ever
stands with a single arm on it.

**"Lamp posts every 24 m within 600 m of a settlement or authored place and
every 60 m elsewhere."** Both rules are written and both are driven in the test,
but the 60 m rule never fires in THIS world: no road is more than 481 m from
one of its own two settlements, so all 513 road stations are inside the 600 m
band and every lamp in the world is on the 24 m spacing. `spacingAt` is driven
both ways in the test, on real ground: a lamp's own position asks for 24, and a
point in open country with nothing inside 600 m asks for 60. The rule is there
for the day a road runs across open country; today it is unexercised, and the
test says so rather than implying otherwise.

**"Where fordFade is below 1 the road is in a river."** That is true of 32 bands
of river across the roads, and six of them are places where the road runs
through the river at a level, dropping a tenth of a metre and staying dry.
Building a bridge there would be a bridge over a damp patch. So a crossing is a
river band that takes the road under the water sheet OR drops it more than
`BRIDGE_DROP` (1.2 m) below its own deck: 26 of the 32. The other six are
counted and left alone, and the test drives both sides of that line.

## How it works

`wayside.js` is pure and runs in node. `waysideForRoad(field, road)` works out
every piece one road carries, once, and caches it against the road object
(roads are themselves cached per field, so two seeds never share a lamp).
`waysideFor(field, cx, cz)` hands a chunk the pieces standing in it.

- **Ownership.** Every piece belongs to ONE road. A piece anchored at a
  settlement belongs to that settlement's lexically smallest road, so a fork's
  signpost is built once and not once per road.
- **The overlap pass.** Two roads leaving the same town can each put a lamp on
  the same square metre; measured, three such pairs exist, the worst 0.14 m
  apart. `waysideFor` gathers the box around the chunk plus 24 m, settles
  overlaps in a fixed order (bridge, gate, sign, shrine, bench, hitch,
  milestone, lamp) and then keeps what stands in the chunk. The gather box is
  wider than the largest clearance, so both chunks either side of a border
  settle a pair the same way.
- **The ground.** A piece's `y` is `field.sampleAt(x, z).h` to the last bit,
  and the test measures that on all 934 pieces that stand on the ground (worst
  difference 3.6e-15 m). It is not obtained by calling `sampleAt`: off a pad
  and off the home disc, the only thing `sampleAt` adds to the raw ground is
  the road's own earthwork, and `probeAt` rebuilds that with roads.js's own
  `roadStrength`, `fordFade` and `roadSurface`. That is half the price, and the
  test is what stops the fast path and the real path drifting apart.
- **Night.** No PointLight per lamp. A lamp's glass is an emissive material and
  its halo is one point of a `THREE.Points` cloud, one cloud per chunk, one
  draw call. ONE real light per chunk, at the lamp nearest the chunk's centre.
  `group.userData.setNight(f, dt)` drives all three from one number and
  switches them off, not merely hidden, below `NIGHT_ON`.
- **Draw calls.** Pieces are bucketed by kind and realm and each bucket merged
  by material, which is why every mesh can still say what kind it is. Measured:
  22 draw calls and 1 light on the busiest chunk in the world (14 pieces),
  11,340 triangles over the eight busiest chunks.

### The bridge is not a picture

A ford in this terrain is a ravine: measured on the road `-6,-2>-7,-2`, the
graded road falls from 6.4 m to -1.4 m in four metres, crosses a stream and
climbs out. A bridge body over that, with the terrain left as it was, would let
the player swim under his own bridge, which is the exact failure CLAUDE.md is
about.

The terrain is NOT changed to fix that, and it cannot be: `roadSurface` clamps
the earthwork to 1.5 m of cut and 1.5 m of fill, `roads.test.mjs` measures both
bounds against the roadless world, and a deck seven metres over a river bed is
not reachable by grading. Lifting it would also fill in the river, and the
river is what the bridge is over.

So the deck is real ground instead: `deckAt(field, x, z)` returns the deck's
height over a bridge and null everywhere else, and `world_runtime.heightAt`
asks it first. The test drives both directions on all 26 bridges: the deck's
own height at three points along every span, null twelve metres to the side,
and 17 of the 26 bridge middles stand over ground that is under the water
sheet, which is what the deck is replacing.

`deckAt` costs one `roadDistanceAt`, which is roads.js's own cached lookup and
the same question `field.sampleAt` asks for every terrain vertex, so it is
cheap enough for every body in the world every frame.

**The one thing it costs:** a player cannot walk UNDER a bridge along the road's
own line, because within the deck's width the deck is the ground. Off the deck
(more than 3.4 m from the centreline) the ravine is exactly as it was.

## What was added to roads.js

Seventy three lines, all of them new exports, nothing changed. `git diff`
reports 73 insertions and 0 deletions.

- `roadsAtSite(field, site)` the roads that meet at a settlement, in id order,
  which is what tells a junction from a road's end and what decides which road
  owns a settlement's signpost.
- `roadsOverlapping(field, x0, z0, x1, z1)` every road whose reach overlaps a
  rectangle. `roadsNearCell` answers this for a whole 480 m site cell; a 64 m
  chunk may straddle a cell border, so a caller streaming chunks needs the
  rectangle.
- `roadPointAt(road, t)` the world point, the tangent and the unit normal at a
  fraction along a road. Everything that stands beside a road is placed from
  it, and the segment list is roads.js's own shape.

No hook was needed to lift the graded surface over the water at a bridge, and
one would have broken the earthworks contract; see above. `roads.test.mjs`
passes, 38 checks.

**One roads.test check was rewritten,** and not because of anything in this
task: `road falls off from centreline to verge`. Another Wave B task added site
kinds to `sitegrid.js` while this one was in flight, which changed which cells
hold settlements, which took the world from 12 roads to 22. Two of the new
roads run within a verge's width of each other, and `sampleAt().road` is the
strength of the NEAREST road, so the old form was reading the wrong road's
falloff and failing. The check now takes its ramp at the first place along a
road, on either side, where that road is the nearest one at every step AND the
sideways step is not swallowed by a bend, and counts apart any road with no
such place. Measured: 22 of 22 roads measured, 0 counted apart.

## What was measured

`node src/world/wayside.test.mjs`: 75 checks, all passing, all on the real
field. The numbers that matter:

- **Lamp spacing.** 364 gaps measured along one verge of every road; 344 of
  them exactly 24 m and the rest exact multiples of it where a lamp was refused
  by water, a pad or a bridge. No gap is anything else.
- **The verge.** All 803 lamps stand between 3.84 and 3.90 m from their own
  road's centreline, which is outside `ROAD_HALF_WIDTH` (3) and inside
  `ROAD_HALF_WIDTH + 1.5`. The centreline itself is measured against the same
  bound and fails it, so the test is measuring something.
- **Signs.** 33 settlements are touched by a road and 33 have a sign; 76
  settlements have no road and none of them has one. 77 fingers, every one
  naming a place that exists in the site grid, every one pointing within 14.6
  degrees of where that place actually is (the bound is 45); the same finger
  turned a right angle is measured at 84 degrees and fails.
- **Bridges.** The roads are walked again in the test at ONE metre, three times
  finer than the placement's own walk, and every one of the 26 crossings is
  bridged end to end; the six wet patches that are not crossings carry nothing;
  no crossing carries two bridges and no bridge stands over nothing. Spans run
  from 6 m to 174 m. Every deck stands clear of the bed under it and both
  abutments land on ground that comes up to meet them.
- **Gates.** The same independent walk finds 6 realm border crossings, all 6
  gated, every lintel carrying its own realm's name; the 16 roads that cross no
  border carry no gate.
- **The Kingsroad.** The Greenwold's longest chain of joined road is 679 m over
  one road, and it crosses no realm border in this seed, so it carries no gate,
  and the test asserts that count rather than a number somebody hoped for.
  Gates stand on roads in the Ember Wastes (two), the Stormpeaks, Frostreach
  and the Boneyard (two).
- **Determinism.** 966 pieces compared between two fields of the same seed:
  identical ids, positions, heights, yaws and styles. A different seed gives a
  different roadside. A chunk asked on a fresh field of the same seed builds
  the same pieces.
- **Chunks.** 963 pieces over 699 chunks, none built twice, none standing
  outside the chunk that built it, 3 dropped by the overlap pass. No two pieces
  the chunks build stand inside each other's room, and the raw per road lists
  do contain 3 such pairs, so the pass has work to do.
- **Cost.** The first chunk to touch a road builds that road's whole furniture:
  worst median 1.92 ms over the five slowest chunks, five runs each, mean 0.90
  ms over all 220 chunks that hold furniture; every chunk after it costs 6.6 us.
  Measured in node, on a machine running three other agents at the time.
- **Bodies.** Every realm's lamp measured from its built geometry: 2.9 to 4.4 m
  tall, none wider than 3 m. A gate 10.1 x 8.5 x 3.8 m. A 30 m bridge with a
  30.4 m body. Every finger board hangs at the bearing of the place it names,
  measured on the built geometry in world coordinates, worst 0.01 degrees out.
- **Light.** At most one PointLight per chunk. In full day nothing is lit: 28
  panes of glass, all dark, the halo at zero opacity, the light off. At
  midnight all three come up, and the flicker moves and does not beat as one.
- **Legibility.** A capital on a board is 0.163 m tall, which is 25 screen
  pixels at six metres through a 60 degree camera on 1080 lines.

`npx vite build` is clean. `npm test` was NOT run in full, as instructed.

## What is NOT verified

- **Nothing here has been seen.** The bodies were built in node behind a stub
  canvas, so no board's text was ever actually rasterised: `fillText` is a
  no-op in the test and the check on legibility is arithmetic about the font
  size and the board's width, not a look at a pixel. The first person to open
  the game should stand at a signpost and read it.
- **The look.** Eight lamp styles, four bridges and three gates were sculpted
  blind. They are the right size and the right shape in the numbers; whether a
  paper lantern reads as a paper lantern is a thing to see.
- **Draw calls are counted, not profiled.** 22 meshes on the busiest chunk is
  the count of objects, not a frame time.
- **The world moved under this work.** Another Wave B task changed
  `sitegrid.js` mid-flight and the road network went from 12 roads to 22. Every
  number above was measured after that change, but a further change to the site
  roll will move them all again. Nothing in the code or the tests depends on a
  road count.
- **The cost is close to its bound.** 1.92 ms on the worst chunk against a 2 ms
  budget. A road longer than about 1 km would put its first chunk over. The fix
  if that day comes is to work the lamps out per arc window rather than per
  road, leaving only the river walk whole road; the bridges and the gates need
  the whole road and cost about 0.8 ms of it.

## Wiring Fable has to do

All of it in `src/game/world_runtime.js`. Six edits, quoted exactly.

**1. The imports**, beside the other world imports at the top:

```js
import { createWayside } from '../world/wayside_models.js';
import { deckAt } from '../world/wayside.js';
```

**2. Create the layer**, in `createWorldRuntime`, immediately after the `flora`
line and BEFORE `createWorldStream` (the stream's callbacks name it):

```js
  const wayside = createWayside(scene, field);
```

**3. Stream it with the chunks.** The two callbacks passed to
`createWorldStream` become:

```js
    onBuilt: (cx, cz, verts) => { flora.onChunk(cx, cz, verts); wayside.onChunk(cx, cz, verts); },
    onDisposed: (cx, cz) => { flora.offChunk(cx, cz); wayside.offChunk(cx, cz); },
```

**4. Hide it underground.** In `overworldNodes()`, beside the flora line:

```js
    if (wayside.group) out.add(wayside.group);
```

Without this the lamps hang lit in the dark while the player is in a dungeon;
`world-wayside` is a direct child of the scene and is in neither
`SKY_AND_LIGHTS` nor the `site:` prefix the sweep already knows.

**5. Light it at dusk.** In `updateWorld`, after the `siteMarkers.update` line:

```js
    // the roadside lamps come up at dusk (A2). The night factor is the same
    // `1 - dayFactor` the mines' lanterns already read.
    wayside.update(dt, 1 - dayFactor);
```

**6. Make the bridges ground.** The runtime's `heightAt` becomes:

```js
    heightAt(x, z) {
      if (dungeon) return dungeonFloor(x, z);
      // a bridge deck is the ground over a river (A2): without this the player
      // walks the ford under his own bridge
      const deck = deckAt(field, x, z);
      return deck === null ? terrainY(x, z) : deck;
    },
```

**7. Dispose it.** In `dispose()`:

```js
      siteMarkers.dispose(); flora.dispose(); wayside.dispose(); fauna.dispose(); world.dispose();
```

**8. Optional, for the dev bench and the debug handle**, in the returned
object's first line:

```js
    field, world, flora, fauna, discovery, siteMarkers, wayside,
```

`wayside.stats` then carries `{ chunks, pieces, lights, drawCalls, buildMs,
night }`.

### Nothing else is needed

No state, no systems registration, no HUD, no save key. The layer has no state
of its own: it is a pure function of the field and the chunks in the ring.
