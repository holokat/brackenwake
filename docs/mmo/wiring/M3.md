# M3: the twenty one tags, wired

Wave A, M3. Owned files: `src/game/monsters.js`, `src/game/monsters.test.mjs`,
`src/game/actor.js` (one line), `src/game/actor.test.mjs`, this note. One file
was touched outside that list and section 6 says which and why.

M2 put a hundred rows on the roster and twenty one new note tags on them, and
wrote out in its own section 2 the rule each tag needs. This is that section 2,
built. Sixteen of the tags became rules in `src/game/monsters.js`, one became a
resist in `actor.js`, two became entries in the ranged table, and the twelve
authored boss plans went into `BOSS_PLANS` where the four that were already
there live.

Measured on the tree this was written against: `src/game/monsters.test.mjs` went
from 278 checks to **398**, all green; `src/game/actor.test.mjs` from 107 to
**111**, all green; `npx vite build` clean, 134 modules, 736 ms.

## 1. What each tag now does, and the number it does it with

Every constant is exported from `src/game/monsters.js` and every one is named in
a test. Where M2 gave no number the constant's comment says INVENTED and why.

| tag | rule | constants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `shieldWall` | while `WALL_MIN` live monsters of the same row stand within `WALL_M` of each other, each carries `WALL_AR` more armour, lent as a tracked delta | 2, 4 m, +10 AR |
| `healsAllies` | the chaplain's cast lands as health on every hurt ally within `HEAL_ALLIES_M`, herself included, for a roll in her row's own damage range | 8 m |
| `howl` | the first blow it takes calls every monster of its own row within `HOWL_M` that has no target yet, once and never again | 30 m |
| `summons` | a row carrying `summons: { id, count }` calls exactly that; a boss with no such field falls back to the habitat roll, unchanged | `SUMMON_AT` 0.5, `SUMMON_COOLDOWN_S` 30 |
| `dives` | a flyer whose target is past `DIVE_TRIGGER_M` stoops to the floor, lands one blow worth `DIVE_MULT`, and climbs | 8 m, x2, every 6 s |
| `grab` | `GRAB_CHANCE` of a landed blow roots the target for `GRAB_SECONDS` and takes the row's LOW damage each second; killing the holder ends it | 1 in 4, 2.0 s |
| `ambush` | spawns with no body, no plate and no aggro until a target is within `AMBUSH_M`; the first blow is worth `AMBUSH_MULT` | 6 m, x2 |
| `awakens` | visible the whole time and completely inert until a target is within `AWAKEN_M`, then its own aggro radius takes over | 4 m |
| `burrows` | every `BURROW_EVERY_S`, if the target is past `BURROW_MIN_M`: gone for `BURROW_UNDER_S`, then up within `BURROW_OUT_M` **behind** the target | 12 s, 5 m, 2 s, 3 m |
| `dropsFromAbove` | spawns `DROP_HEIGHT_M` up and inert until somebody walks within `DROP_UNDER_M` underneath, then falls and fights, taking no fall damage | 6 m, 2.5 m |
| `stormCall` | a cast that produces a mark on the ground rather than a missile: `STORM_WARN_S` of warning, then the row's damage as energy within `STORM_RADIUS` | 1.5 s, 3 m |
| `powderCharge` | a charge at the target's feet every `POWDER_EVERY_S`: `POWDER_WARN_S` of fuse, then the row's damage as fire within `POWDER_RADIUS` | 10 s, 2 s, 3 m |
| `hex` | the SECOND cast of a row that already casts, on `HEX_EVERY_S`: `HEX_POINTS` off the target's hit for `HEX_SECONDS` | 15 s, 15 points, 10 s |
| `ashCloud` | the same shape on a landed blow rather than a cast | 20 points, 6 s |
| `tailSweep` | every `SWEEP_EVERY`th swing is a cone at `SWEEP_RANGE` and `SWEEP_HALF_ANGLE` instead of a blow, and it costs that swing | 4th, 4 m, 90 degrees |
| `dragonTime` | Malachar's swing timer at `DRAGON_SWING` and his approach at `DRAGON_RUN` while he is above his last threshold; under it they come off and `wantsPlayerSlow()` goes true | 0.5, 1.5x, scale 0.5 |
| `fireImmune` | `actor.js` `NOTE_RESISTS`, fire at `RESIST_CAP` | 70 |

`powderCharge` and `stormCall` also went into `monster_ai.RANGED_TAGS`, as
`thrown` and `cast`, so the Legion Sapper stands off with a bag of powder rather
than walking into sword reach and the reef eel holds a cast that can be broken.
`spellFor` grew one branch for `stormCall`, which returns a spell carrying
`ground: { radius, warn, colour }`; that field is the whole of the difference
between a bolt and a mark on the floor.

### Four rules that share one machine

The boss slam, `stormCall` and `powderCharge` are the same thing with different
radii, warnings, damage types and words: `startMark(mon, at, spec)` and
`stepMarks`. `startSlam` is now a one line wrapper so nothing else in the file
changed. `warnings()` grew `kind` and now reports each mark's own radius rather
than `SLAM_RADIUS` for all three.

`ambush`, `awakens`, `dropsFromAbove` and `burrows` all reduce to one flag,
`ctx.dormant`, which `stepMonster` reads: no aggro, no drift, no wander, holds
exactly where it was put. The runtime owns each trigger, because only the
runtime knows where every body is; the moment it clears the flag the row's own
aggro radius takes over, so there is one aggro rule and not two.

## 2. Everything a rule lends, it gives back

Four of these rules write onto an actor that is not the monster's own, and every
one of them is a tracked delta with a release path, because a player still
carrying fifteen points off his hit after the thing that cursed him is dead is
the silent-effect failure this project has already paid for.

* **The shield wall** is `actor.ar` plus `mon.wall`, and `mon.wall` is
  subtracted again in `stepWall`, in `despawn` and in `died`.
* **The hex and the ash cloud** are a real entry in `target.buffs`, in the list
  `actor.recompute` already reads, because a number written straight onto
  `bonuses.hit` disappears the moment the player equips a ring. When no
  `recompute` was handed in, the points go on the live record instead and are
  added back by hand; both paths are real and both are tested.
  **The buff carries no `until` field, on purpose.** That field is in SECONDS in
  `abilities_runtime.js` and in wall-clock milliseconds in `recompute`, and a
  curse expiring against the wrong clock is the units bug this project has
  already paid for once. The clock is `penalties`, in this file, in the same
  milliseconds every other timer here uses.
* **The grab** is `combat.applyStatus(target, 'root', ...)`, the same root a web
  writes, cleared by `combat.clearStatus` on release, on the holder's death and
  on `releaseAll`.
* **Malachar's own speed** is `bonuses.swingSpeed` and `actor.run`, applied and
  removed by one function so the removal is exact. Measured: 2.40 s becomes
  1.20 s and back, run 8 becomes 12 and back.

`releaseAll()` runs on `clearLayer` (a stair, a teleport, the surface coming
back) and on `dispose`. Tested: after `monsters.dispose()` the player's
`bonuses.hit` is 0 and his `buffs` list is empty.

## 4. `wantsPlayerSlow()`: a hook with no consumer yet

Malachar's last third is the one ability in the game allowed to touch the
player's own clock. **This file does not apply it, and should not.** The world
clock is `app/context.js`'s `ctx.clock`, the wyrmsoul system is the only thing
that sets its scale today, and two systems writing one number is how a dragon
call and a boss phase end up fighting over it with neither looking broken.

So `monsters.wantsPlayerSlow()` is a question, and nothing in the tree asks it
yet. Until something does, Malachar's last phase is his own half-speed swing
coming OFF plus a line saying the room slowed, which is honest and half a boss.

It returns `{ active, scale, source, name }`, `{ active: false, scale: 1 }` when
nothing wants anything.

The consumer belongs in `src/game/app/systems/dragon.js`, which already owns
`ctx.clock` and already calls `setScale` and `catchUp`. The shape it needs:

```js
// in the frame, after monsters.update, alongside the existing
//   if (!W.isActive(soul) && ctx.clock.scale !== 1) ctx.clock.setScale(1);
const boss = ctx.get('combat').monsters.wantsPlayerSlow();
```

and then the honest part, which is a **design decision this note deliberately
does not make**, because `ctx.clock` slows the WORLD (which speeds the player up
relative to it) and Malachar wants the opposite. Two ways out:

1. **Slow the player, not the world.** A negative `bonuses.swingSpeed` on the
   player's actor for as long as `active` holds, applied and removed the way the
   hex is applied and removed above, plus a movement factor. This is small and
   works today, and it stretches exactly what M2 asked for: "the player's own
   swing timer is what stretches".
2. **A second scale on the clock.** `ctx.clock` grows a player-side scale that
   `main.js` divides `dt` by. Bigger, and it would have to be reconciled with
   dragon time so that calling the dragon inside Malachar's last phase does
   something coherent rather than multiplying two scales together.

Whoever takes it should also decide what happens when a player calls the dragon
during that phase. Nothing in the tree stops both being live at once.

## 5. What M2 asked for that this deliberately did not build, or built differently

Written out rather than buried, so each is one constant or one paragraph to
change.

**`grab` is 2.0 s, not 3.** M2 section 2 writes "root the target for 3 s" and
then, two sentences later, "it is `webRoot2` with damage on it and it should
reuse that root". `webRoot2` is two seconds by its own name and by the roster's
own words for it, and the M3 brief asks for two. `GRAB_SECONDS` is the constant.

**`shieldWall` is two, not three.** M2 and `NOTE_TAG_MEANING` both say "two or
more"; the M3 brief says three. The roster is the source of truth for a number
the roster states. `WALL_MIN`.

**`awakens` is 4 m, not 8.** Same reason: `NOTE_TAG_MEANING` says "never aggros
at all until you come inside four metres of it". `AWAKEN_M`.

**`fireImmune` is 70, not 100.** `RESIST_CAP` is 70 and `recompute` clamps every
resist to it, so writing 100 would arrive as 70 while looking like it meant
something more. This is exactly how `immunePoison` and `coldImmune` were already
written.

**`powderCharge` does not move what it hits.** M2 says "moving whatever it hits".
There is no monster-side knockback anywhere in the tree: `abilities_runtime`'s
`doKnockback` is the player's, and writing the player's `actor.pos` from here
would be undone by `player.js` on the next frame. The charge does its damage in
its radius and says so; the shove is unbuilt and is listed below.

**The non-boss summon has a cooldown as well as a "once".** M2 says "does it
once, the first time it drops below half health". A row that flees at 25% and
returns healed can genuinely cross half twice, so `summonReady` re-arms above
half and `SUMMON_COOLDOWN_S` (30 s, INVENTED) stops a second call inside that.
Tested both ways.

## 6. The one file touched outside the owned list

`src/game/monster_ai.js`. M2 section 2 says outright that the twelve boss plans
"belong in `monster_ai.js`" and pastes them, and that `stormCall` "needs an

wiring note claims that file. Three changes, all of them M2's own paste:

* two rows added to `RANGED_TAGS` (`powderCharge`, `stormCall`);
* one branch added to `spellFor` for the ground-mark spell, plus five constants
  (`STORM_WARN_S`, `STORM_RADIUS`, `POWDER_WARN_S`, `POWDER_RADIUS`,

* the twelve `BOSS_PLANS` entries, word for word from M2.

Nothing else in that file moved. `auditRangedRows()` still passes, which is what
proves the two new ranged tags produce a mode and a spell.

## 7. The honest half: what is still carried and read by nothing

`auditTagRules()` classifies all 79 note tags. The counts, printed by the test:

```
monsters.js 24    monster_ai.js 7    actor.js 11    roster 6    descriptive 6    unwired 25
```

`descriptive` means the tag is a true statement about a number already on the
row (`plate`, `shield`, `thickHide`, `swordAndShield`, `lootTwice`,
`purpleFloor`) and there is nothing missing. `unwired` means a row carries it and
**nothing in the tree reads it**. Twenty five of them:

```
erratic, noonOnly, wanders, slow, charges, leadsGoblins, warCry, disease10,
stun, paralyse15, silence3, knockback, groundSlam, webRoot2, roots, frostNova,
burnStopsRegen, regrows, threeHeads, healsInDaylight, lifeLeech30, manaDrain,
phylactery, coinPurse, champion
```

None of these is M3's to build and none was made worse. Four are worth naming
because they are not what they look like:

* **`lifeLeech30`** is read by `makeMonsterActor`, the node stand-in that ships
  in `monsters.js` for the tests, and **not** by `actor.js`'s `spawnMonster`,
  which is what `app/systems/combat.js` passes as the `actorFactory`. So in the
  running game the Vampire Knight, the Drowned Knight, King Caradoc and Malachar
  do not leech at all. One line in `spawnMonster` would fix it; `actor.js` was
  mine for the fireImmune resist and nothing else, so it is reported instead.
* **`webRoot2`** has no writer, even though the root itself now exists and
  `grab` uses it. The Mother of Spiders and the Blossom Spider carry the tag and
  never web anybody. It is four lines against `stepBlows`, the same hook `grab`
  uses.
* **`knockback`** and **`groundSlam`** are carried by nine rows between them and
  are both entirely absent for monsters. The boss slam is a BOSS PHASE and does
  not read `groundSlam` at all, so the Glacier Golem and the Temple Guardian
  carry a tag the Warden's behaviour is not driven by.
* **`champion`** promises "the plate says so"; only `isBoss` gets a name plate,
  so eight tier 5 champions have none.

## 8. Effects: what a later pass should draw properly

`createMonsters` now takes an optional `opts.effects`. **Every call to it is
optional-chained and nothing reads a return value**, so a headless run and the
game as it stands today behave identically; the rules are complete without it.
`app/systems/combat.js` does not pass one. The one line that would, in that
file's `createMonsters` call:

```js
      effects: ctx.get('abilities').effects,
```

With one in hand, six places currently ask for a coloured burst or ring and
should be drawn properly instead:

| where | what it asks for now | what it should be |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `startMark` | `ring(pos, radius, colour, warn)` | a slam ring in dust, a storm mark crackling with the column already forming above it, a charge with a visible fuse and a sputter |
| `stepMarks` on landing | `burst(pos, colour, radius/3)` | a lightning column for the storm (`effects.column` exists and is right for it), a fire bloom and a shockwave ring for the charge |
| `grabHold` | `burst(target.pos, white)` | an arm or a tentacle actually holding the player, and a strain on the rig |
| `chantHeal` | `burst(chaplain.pos, green)` | light travelling from the cup to each ally it reached, one bolt each |
| `surface` | `burst(worm.pos, sand)` | the ground breaking, a spray of it, the worm rising out of the hole |
| `tailSweep` | `burst(mon.pos, white)` | an arc swept through the air at the cone's real angle |
| `summonFor` | `burst(mon.pos, purple)` | something at each of the ring's points, not one at the summoner |
| `hitPenalty` | `combat.float(target, 'hit -15')` | a screen effect for the ash cloud, and a mark over the head for the hex |

The dive, the ambush and the drop want no new effect: they want the models to
have a stoop, a rise out of the reeds and a fall, which is `monster_models.js`'s.

## 9. Two things found while measuring, neither of them M3's

**`src/game/monsters.test.mjs`'s slam block is non-deterministic**, and was
before this wave. `actor.js`'s `spawnMonster` seeds `lastSwingAt` with
`-Math.random() * m.speed * 1000` so a pack does not swing in lockstep, and the
test hands `spawnMonster` no rng. Over three seconds the Warden lands one blow
or two, so "standing in the ring costs you" prints 64 or 128 run to run. The
check is `took > 0`, so it passes either way, and it was verified against
`HEAD`'s own module to make sure this wave did not cause it.

**`src/game/npcs_runtime.test.mjs` was red before this work started and still
is** (`the turn settles facing you and stops   -4.712 against 1.571`). It is not
mine. Five other suites were red at the start of this work and are green now,
fixed by other agents while it ran: `state`, `win_map`, `world/flora`,
`world/mine_models`, `world/sitegrid`.
