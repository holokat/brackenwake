# GW1: the Greenwold sculpted to the painted map

The request, in the user's words:

> do you think you could sculpt the zone with objects that are needed to make
> this map as accurate as possible? Then, I could go through and fix anything I
> dont like with the editor. Try to match correct elevations, vegetation,
> fields, roads, etc...

So this is a FIRST DRAFT OF A COUNTRY, laid by script so there is something to
walk and argue with, and every number in it is meant to be moved in the editor.

## What runs it

```
node scripts/sculpt-greenwold.mjs
```

It is rerunnable and it overwrites. Every file it writes it writes whole:

| file | what |
| --- | --- |
| `public/terrain/greenwold.json` | 4,081 strokes. The header is unchanged: sculpt, a flat 6 m grass world, no sea, `places: false` |
| `src/mmo/spaces/greenwold_*.json` | 40 spaces: the places, the fields, the wood, the copses, the banks and the stretches of road |
| `src/mmo/spaces/list.js` | regenerated by `tools/editor_save.mjs`'s own `writeSpaceIndex` |

It writes through `saveEditorFile`, which is the function the editor's own
`POST /__editor/save` calls, so this is the editor's path and not a second one.
It does not touch the three `tile_*` spaces the user's editor made, and it does
not touch `zones.js`, the guide, the map or the minimap.

**`places: false` is what makes this the only Greenwold.** `field.js` line 793
drops every authored site when a sculpt header says so, so the sheet's own nine
sites do not stand and there is not a second village three kilometres away.

## Where the numbers come from

`src/mmo/greenwold_guide.js` is the painting measured, and it is the one source:
the twelve centres, the ring, the two roads and the river all come off
`GUIDE_ZONES`, `GUIDE_ROADS` and `GUIDE_RIVER`. Nothing in the script
re-derives an image fraction.

**North is minus z.** The top of the sheet is `z = -2200`, the chalk hills are
at negative z, and Coldwake, south west on the painting, is at x -796, z +1291.
Getting this backwards draws the whole country upside down, which is why the
guide says it three times and why this file says it once more.

## The order of the strokes is the picture

Later strokes win, so the list is laid in the order a person would cut it.

1. **The downland skeleton**, 681 plateaus on a 200 m grid at a target height.
   A plateau pulls the ground TO a number where a raise only adds one, so the
   country is a function anyone can read rather than whatever a pile of raises
   summed to. The function is `6 + 14 * north + 8 * west + roll`, faded to the
   flat 6 m world between 2300 m and 2850 m out.
2. **The chalk hills**, 15 mountains along the north and north west with the
   roughness climbing toward the Chalk Pits, and two ridges: the low one along
   the west that the beech wood stands on, and a spine that ties the north
   range into one range. Every amount is scaled by 0.85, because the first run
   stacked two hills and a spine into a 113 m peak where the brief asked for 40
   to 90. Measured now: the highest hilltop is 93 m and the lowest 29 m.
3. **Roughening**, 8 noise strokes over the open south and east.
4. **The rises**: the Standing Hedge's ring on a gentle 5 m dome a kilometre
   across, and the Long Meadow's own rise under its oak.
5. **The river's corridor**, 108 plateaus at the water level plus a bank, cut
   BEFORE the water so the channel has ground to cut into.
6. **The flats**: the village, the water meadows, Coldwake, the Kingsroad camp
   and the nine stones.
7. **The water**: 107 river ribbons, the mill pond, the mere and two meadow
   ponds.
8. **The scar and the hollow**, pit and cliff.
9. **The paint**, 3,136 strokes: the fields' dirt, then the mud and the scar,
   then the roads LAST, because the first run painted the roads first and four
   of the bank path's forty four samples came back "mud".

## The water level is measured, not typed

At every 60 m down the river the ground is asked what it is, the water is put
two and a half metres under it, and the profile is then forced to fall: never
above the line from the head to 2 m at the east edge, and never higher than the
point upstream. So the river runs downhill in a cut on ground nobody guessed at.

Measured: head 54.9 m, mouth 1.8 m, 5,863 m long, 53.0 m of fall, and 59 of 59
points on the centreline carry water.

**The weir is the pond above it, not a hole below it.** Two earlier tries got
this wrong and both were caught by the measurement:

- taking two metres off every target below the mill put the east edge at 0.5 m
  where the brief asks for about two;
- taking it off the ground's own line as well made a real step and dropped the
  whole lower river with it, mouth included.

What a mill weir does is hold water back. So the pond is walked UPSTREAM from
the mill and raised to the weir's crest until it meets the river's own level,
which is a backwater curve and is monotone by construction. Measured: a fall of
1.99 m at the mill, and 480 m of pond above it at a crest of 15.30 m.

## How a plan is put on the water

Three of the nine plans are drawn about water, and the guide's river runs
straight through all three centres, which would put the well, the mill and the
arch in it. So those three spaces are not placed at the guide's point: the part
of the plan that WANTS water is placed on the guide's river, exactly, and the
buildings step back off it.

| place | what happened |
| --- | --- |
| Hearthhome | turned 290.9 deg until the bridge's 10 m span is square to the river, then moved 59 m so the bridge stands on the guide's own waypoint. The turn is chosen from the two that satisfy the right angle: the one that leaves the gate on the same bank the Kingsroad arrives on, so the road reaches the gate without fording anything |
| the Mill Run | turned 339.9 deg until the plan's leat runs with the river, then moved 25 m so the middle of the leat is the river's own line |
| the Old Cellars | moved 45 m onto the north bank, off the channel |

So the river is the guide's polyline to the metre and the plan is what moved.

**The village's wall now meets the river, and nine of its runs came out.**
Hearthhome's flint wall is a closed ring 46 m out and the channel passes 44 m
from the middle, so the water crosses it. `openTheWall` drops the runs the
channel goes through, which leaves a water gate. The user may prefer to move
the village thirty metres instead; that is one number in the script.

**Anything else standing in water is walked ashore.** `dryOff` asks the field
about every piece, tree and rock, and walks the ones that have no business
being wet outward in five metre rings until the sample is dry. It moved one
thing on this run: the village's hay rick, which the plan puts at local 43, -12
and the new channel runs under. Fourteen models are on a list of things that
are only themselves when they are wet (a bridge, a weir, stepping stones, the
sunken chapel and its headstones, a willow), and reed beds and dew ponds among
the rocks.

## What is in the forty spaces

286 pieces, 119 runs, 1,050 trees, 842 rocks, 22 markers, 20 people, 110 spawns.

- **The nine plans** are copied to their painted points. Every PERSON NAME is
  dropped on the way, because `plan_schema` refuses a named villager in a space:
  `PERSON.bram.place` is "hearthhome" and a space stands at a point and has no
  place at all. The ROLE is kept, so the game still stands an innkeeper in the
  inn, and a marker is put beside each of the cast saying who the plan meant.
  Seven of them in Hearthhome, one at the mill, one in the hollow, one at the
  camp.
- **The Standing Hedge is nine spaces**, one per stone, each turned so its
  carved face looks back at the ring's middle. One space with a `repeat` would
  have had a 22 m body at the ring's centre and the streamer would never have
  found a stone from a kilometre away.
- **The Beech Hangar** is the plan's 47 beech pieces with 514 more beeches
  scattered round them on a jittered 9 m grid with a 7 m floor. Measured: the
  closest two trees in the world stand 7.00 m apart.
- **Eight fields**: five of wheat and three of pasture, each a hedged square
  with a gate, a scarecrow or a fold, oaks on the hedge line and dirt painted
  under the wheat. 130 wheat rows and 39 furrows in the big ones.
- **Four stretches of Kingsroad**, placed by walking out from the gate every
  440 m and shrinking each until it clears the camp and everything else. Lamp
  posts every 100 m, milestones every 500 m, which is what a milestone is: the
  brief said "every 100 m" for both and a stone every hundred metres is not a
  milestone.
- **Five copses, three willow banks, the Long Meadow, the Water Meadows and
  Coldwake**, which are the three the spaces doc still listed as to author.

**No space overlaps another by more than a quarter of the smaller radius.** The
ones with give in them (roads, copses, banks) are sized by `fitRadius`, which
asks every space already down. Measured on the finished set: three pairs touch
at all, and all three are the user's own `tile_*` spaces, which this script does
not move.

## The runtime, walked to the village

The script's last measurement is not the field: it is the game. It builds the
same `createWorldRuntime` the browser builds, in node, with the scene mocked
exactly as `world_runtime.test.mjs` mocks it, hands it this file's own stroke
list and walks to the green.

```
the runtime took the file: 4081 strokes, sculpt world true, and rebuilt 3 chunks and 6 sites
walked to the green: Hearthhome is standing: true; 3 site markers live, 86 meshes, 715,786 triangles
the streamed ground, grass, dressing and wayside round it: 18 meshes, 195,180 triangles,
  3 of them instanced carrying 39,698 instances
the 64 m chunk the well stands in: 1 meshes and 2,304 triangles
111 bodies of water are live, and the runtime's own heightAt at the well is 7.24 m
sitesNear within 400 m of the well: greenwold_eastfield, greenwold_greenfield,
  greenwold_hearthhome, greenwold_kingsroad_1
```

## What it cost

`sampleAt` with all 4,081 strokes on the ground, median of five passes:

| where | cost |
| --- | --- |
| anywhere in the realm, 40,000 points | 1.91 us a sample |
| standing on a painted road, 1,416 points | 2.32 us a sample |

The budget was 4 us. One pass is not a measurement: the same list read 2.27 us
and 3.87 us on two consecutive single passes, which is why the script times five
and reports the median, the best and the worst.

## Two bugs this turned up, and both are fixed

### Every ground treatment in the game faced the wrong way

`plan_models.laneMesh` and `plan_models.areaMesh` wound their quads clockwise
seen from above. Three culls the back face of a `FrontSide` material and every
colour in the palette is FrontSide, so **every lane, every wheat field, every
mud patch and every water decal in all nine plans was invisible from anywhere a
player stands.** Nothing had ever asked.

It was found by a ray: dropped straight down onto the Old Cellars' mud, it
passed through all thirty of that decal's triangles. Measured over the plans and
the new spaces, 19,487 of the level triangles faced DOWN. Both builders are
wound the other way now and the same count reads 19,575 facing up, the remaining
8,701 being the undersides of boxes, which are meant to face down.

`plan_models.test.mjs` has the guard: a layout of nothing but ground, one of
each of the five treatments, every triangle of it asked which way it looks and
one in twenty of them asked again with a ray. Driven both ways: 1,752 of 1,752
triangles face down with the old winding and 0 of 1,752 with the new.

### A planned place was never hidden underground

`world_runtime.overworldNodes` gathers the scene children whose name starts with
`site:` and switches them off on the way into a dungeon. A planned place and a
hand laid space both come back from `buildPlan`, whose root group is named
`plan:<id>`, so a village or a wood would have stayed lit over the player's head
underground. Until a named space stood near the spawn nothing noticed.

`createSiteMarkers.update` names every marker for its site now, in one line,
rather than five builders each remembering to. The plan's own name still lives
on every child group, which is what the picker and `spaces.test.mjs` read.

`world_runtime.test.mjs` had the claim already and it was red; it is green.

## What could not be matched

- **The Kingsroad camp stands 2,447 m from the middle**, past the realm's own
  2,200 m circle. That is the guide's own placement and `auditGuide` counts it
  rather than refusing it, because the camp is where the road ENTERS the realm.
  It is left where the guide puts it.
- **The chalk scar and the Beech Hangar are 2.2 km apart.** The spaces doc says
  the scar is "white in the hill ahead, 400 m off" from the ridge above the
  wood. The painting puts the pits at the top left and the wood at the bottom
  left, and the painting wins. The sightline in the doc is not there.
- **The lane the brief describes as one lane is two.** "South west through the
  ring, west past the long meadow to the mill" is two different bearings out of
  one gate on the painting: the guide's own `villagelane` goes west north west
  past the Long Meadow, and the lane to Coldwake goes south. Both cross the
  ring, so "through the ring" is true of either.
- **The village keeps the plan's own trees inside its wall.** The brief says no
  trees inside the village wall; `hearthhome.json` has eight, authored from the
  concept painting, and they are the plan's and not this script's. Nothing new
  was planted inside it.
- **A milestone every 100 m** was asked for and would be absurd. Lamps are every
  100 m and milestones every 500 m.
- **Hearthhome's wall is open at the water** for about 27 m either side of the
  bridge, which is nine dropped runs. It is a water gate rather than a wall in a
  river, and it is the one thing here most likely to want a hand.

## Where to start moving things

Everything in the script is a table near the top of its own section: `HILLS`,
`FIELDS`, `COPSES`, `BANKS`, `ROAD_STOPS`, `LANE_COLDWAKE`, `PATH_BANK`,
`PATH_RIDGE`. Change one and rerun. Or open the editor and move the thing, and
do not rerun, because a rerun overwrites.
