## 1. The Sunken Chapel's water floated

The plan lays a water plane 3.6 m over the ground at the chapel. A pad levels
that ground. So the lake stood 3.6 m over a dry, level table 35.2 m across, and
its edge at 30 m was a wall of water between 1.60 m and 3.91 m high on all
thirty two bearings measured. The chapel was under water to its eaves, which is
right, and the meadow it was supposed to be flooding was not.

`zones.SITE_DISH` gives an authored site a floor under its own rim:

```js
export const SITE_DISH = Object.freeze({ sunkenchapel: 3.6 });
```

`field.js` takes it off by the pad's OWN weight, in the one place the pad is
applied, so the floor is the full depth down across the flat middle of the pad
and comes back up to the rim over the shoulder the pad already blended on. The
side of a dish is therefore exactly as walkable as the side of every pad in the
world, because it is the same shoulder.

Measured in `field.test.mjs` (11 checks, all green):

- **The depth is the plan's number and not a second opinion about it.**
  `SITE_DISH.sunkenchapel` is `sunkenchapel.json`'s `areas[0].y`, checked, so
  the two cannot drift apart.
- **The floor is 3.6000 m under the rim.** Rim 3.842 m, floor 0.242 m.
- **And it is flat.** 144 points out to 17.6 m, spread 0.0e+0 m.
- **The rim is still the meadow.** 160 points from 36 m to 72 m, every one bit
  for bit the ground the seed made.
- **The dish itself lays 0.293 m of rise per metre at its steepest**, against
  the 1.2 the player refuses (`player.MAX_SLOPE`, imported rather than copied)
  and the 0.51 every named climb in the world stands at.
- **The whole pad, dish and hillside together, worst 0.535 m per metre**, 25.8 m
  out on the 330 degree radial.
- **A player walks in and out.** 1280 one metre strides on 32 radials, every one
  accepted by player.js's own rule, both directions.
- **The water lies at the rim** (surface 3.842 m, rim 3.842 m) and **the chapel
  floor is the floor of the dish**, 3.600 m under it, which is what
  `PLANS.md` asks for.
- **Driven the other way:** 57 padded sites carry no dish and every one is still
  a table at its own height, worst 1.8e-15 m off. Exactly one site in the world
  asks for a dish.

`zones.test.mjs` adds the rules a second dish would have to meet: it has to name
a real place, the row has to carry it and every other row a flat 0, it has to
have a pad to be cut into (a dish on a `flatR` of 0 would be silently dead,
because field.js does not run the pad code for one), and its shoulder has to be
long enough to carry the depth at under `RAMP_MAX_STEP`. Driven the wrong way: a
20 m dish on the same 32 m pad is 1.63 m per metre and is refused.

**The heart digest did not move**, and there was no need to re-pin it. The one
site with a dish stands 1759 m from the origin with a 36 m reach, and the digest
is a 1 km square. `field.test.mjs` says so where the digest is pinned, and the
whole suite is green on the number it already had:
`d0f49cbf0a06008faf66d163ba604935ce8827a337ee2159d12593da78bc51f2`. The relief
digest is taken from `raw`, which never asks what stands on the ground, and is
likewise untouched: `d807c78135a47572d0ebcc93609deac2203fd7d509bdc62172a1efa669972494`.
`sampleAt` costs 1.53 us, against 1.55 before.

### What is still wrong out there, and it is not the dish

The flooded meadow is a flat disc of water 60 m across and the meadow it lies in
is not level: the ground falls 3.93 m across the plan's own circle. So the
shoreline, where the ground comes up to the water, sits between 27.5 m and
70.3 m from the chapel depending on the bearing, and on 6 of 32 bearings the
ground never reaches the water level at all inside 72 m. Where the shore is
further out than the water polygon, the polygon's edge stands proud: on 21 of 32
bearings, by at most 1.21 m.

That is a great deal better than the 32 of 32 and 3.91 m it was, and it is not
something a deeper dish fixes, because the ground to the south is simply lower
than the surface. What would fix it is a pad wide enough to hold a LEVEL shore
ring at the water's own radius: `flatR` 56 keeps the pad flat to 30.8 m, which
is the water's 30 m, and the dish would then need its own smaller radius so the
lip stands at the shoreline. That is a change to the shape of the pad and not to
the dish, it moves 60 m of the Greenwold rather than 36, and it was not asked
for, so it is written down here rather than done.

## 2. The Standing Hedge landed you in a field

`edgeOf` lands you at nine tenths of a site's `bodyR` on the side you are
coming from. That gets the DISTANCE right and says nothing about the bearing,
and the Standing Hedge is nine sarsens at nine bearings on a ring 805 m across.
Measured before the fix, from Hearthhome, from the origin and from the far south
west: **112.3 m, 92.0 m and 75.2 m from the nearest stone**, standing in grass
with the hedge a rumour on the skyline.

A plan with a `repeat` already knows where its copies are (`footprints.stopsOf`,
which `plan_models` builds the ring from and `dressing` will read). So `edgeOf`
now takes the stop nearest the player and stands you `RING_LAND` (8 m) beyond it
on the ring's own radial, looking back in: the stone is the first thing in front
of you and the ring is the walk. It is in `edgeOf` and not in `landingFor`
because the Sites list teleport goes through `edgeOf` too and had the same bug.

Measured in `win_dev.test.mjs`:

- From those same three places the landing is **8.000 m from a stone**, and
  outside every footprint of the plan laid at that stop, including the boundary
  stones on their 6.5 m ring.
- It is **the stone nearest the player**, and three different stones for the
  three different sides.
- You stand **813 m out, outside the 805 m ring, facing in**, to 1e-9.
- **Driven the other way, twice.** The Obsidian Bridge carries a `bodyR` and no
  plan, and still lands at 96.3 m, nine tenths of its 107 m reach, on the side
  you came from. Hearthhome carries no `bodyR` and still lands at 128 m, its
  120 m precinct plus the 8 m pad.
- And **the Standing Hedge is the one plan in the world laid more than once**,
  so this branch cannot quietly start catching others.

## 3. Two people in one street answered to the same name

`nameFor(site, roleId, index)` hashes the ROLE'S LENGTH and the index into a
table of twenty four given names. A street of nine draws nine times out of
twenty four, and by the birthday arithmetic two of them come up the same about
as often as not.

Measured over every settlement within 6 km of the origin, before the fix:
**29 of 129 streets held a repeat, 45 people of 440.** Hearthhome stood Pell the
Stablemaster beside Pell the Banker and Udd the Alchemist beside Udd the
Provisioner, which is the pair the browser pass saw. It is not a planned town
problem: 28 of the 29 are villages the world rolled, and Saltwake stood three
Elsys.

`nameFor` now takes an optional `taken` Set. A name already spoken for in that
street walks on to the next in the table, which keeps every name a pure function
of the site, the role and the order of the street, and keeps the name for
whoever asked first. `streetFor` holds one Set per street and **seeds it with
the cast's names before generating anybody**, both the full plate and its first
word, because "Cobb" standing next to "Cobb Ashby" is the same bug in a coat.

Measured in `npcs_runtime.test.mjs`:

- **440 people over 129 streets within 6 km, planned and rolled, not one
  repeat**, comparing first words so a surname cannot hide a clash.
- **The cast keep their names.** Nan Ockley, Cobb Ashby, Alys Fenn, Old Wynn
  Ashby, Bram Haywood and Pip all stand in Hearthhome under the names
  `story.js` gives them, and the street asked twice is the same street.
- Hearthhome now reads: Nan Ockley the Innkeeper, Cobb Ashby the Blacksmith,
  Alys Fenn the Healer, **Pell** the Stablemaster, **Quill** the Banker, Old
  Wynn Ashby the Elder, Bram Haywood the Farmer, Pip the Child, Rook the
  Provisioner, Jem the Tailor, Orrin the Bowyer, **Udd** the Alchemist, Wren the
  Weaponsmaster, **Vess** the Provisioner.
- **Driven the other way:** `nameFor` with no street still gives Hearthhome
  `Tam, Yarrow, Cobb, Pell, Pell, Wren, Kett, Vess, Rook, Jem, Orrin, Udd, Wren,
  Udd`, which is the bug, so the uniqueness is doing the work and the hash has
  not quietly changed under it. A held name is stepped over to exactly the next
  in the table and no further.
- `auditNpcSpots` now refuses a world where the widest street could outgrow the
  table. Today it is 24 names against a street of 14.

## Two things found on the way, not fixed, for whoever owns them

**`SITE_FLAT_R` has `millrun` twice.** Line 658 is P1's `millrun: 40` and line
672 is the older landmark row `millrun: 8`. The later key wins in an object
literal, so the Mill Run stands on an 8 m pad and P1's measured recommendation
of 40 has never taken effect: the mill, the miller's house and the granary sit
across 4.85 m of fall. The value was not changed here because it moves the
ground under a place this task was not about. A duplicate key in an object
literal is invisible at runtime and cannot be caught by reading the object, so
if 40 is the right answer the fix is to delete line 672 and say so.

**Four planned people can never appear.** `streetFor` returns early when the
site's kind is not in `PEOPLED` (`town`, `hamlet`, `ruin`, `tower`), and that
gate stands ABOVE the planned branch. So the Mill Run's miller, the Kingsroad's
officer, the chalk pits' provisioner and Highwayman's Hollow's outlaw are
written into their plans, audited by `plans.test.mjs`, and gated out: their
places are a landmark, a landmark, a mine and a camp. Hearthhome and the Sunken
Chapel are the only two planned places whose kind gets past the gate. Moving the
planned branch above the gate is one line, but it puts four people into the
world who are not there today and `auditNpcSpots` walks `PEOPLED` for ring and
plaza radii, so it is a decision and not a tidy-up.
