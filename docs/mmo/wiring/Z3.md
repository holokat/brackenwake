# Z3: the ground is dressed and every realm has its own sky

Written 2026-09-06. A fifth task beside Wave B's four, on files none of them own.

The Boneyard read as a flat sand plain with pebbles, three cacti and a pond,
under the same blue noon the meadow gets. The sheet says it is a graveyard of
nine dragons under an ash sky. Two things were missing and both are here: the
ground carries the realm's own things every forty metres, and the realm has its
own sky.

## Files

New:

- `src/world/dressing.js` the placement. Pure, no THREE. Nine kits, the gate,
  the two grids, the runs, `auditKits`.
- `src/world/dressing_models.js` the bodies and the streamed layer. Sixty three
  primitive builders, nine colour rows, four materials, `createDressing`,
  `auditBodies`, `nameOf`.
- `src/world/dressing.test.mjs` 76 checks.
- `docs/mmo/wiring/Z3.md` this.

Changed:

  `skyLighting`, `auditSky`, and `skyColours` now takes a realm or a mix.
- `src/game/scene.js` `lightingAt` and `applyLighting` take a tint, `follow`
  keeps the realm blend, `setDay` puts it on the fog and the four lights.
- `src/game/sky.test.mjs` and `src/game/scene.test.mjs` gained the realm halves.

Nothing else was touched. `field.js`, `zones.js`, `flora.js`, `grass.js`,

## Wiring Fable has to do

All of it is in `src/game/world_runtime.js`. The sky needs no wiring at all:
`scene.js`'s `follow(pos)` already runs every frame from
`src/game/app/systems/world.js`, and it now keeps the realm blend, which
`sky.js` reads off `sc.realmMix`. Seven lines, quoted exactly.

**1. the import.** Beside the flora import, after line 28:

```js
import { createDressing } from '../world/dressing_models.js';
```

**2. the layer.** Directly after the line that makes flora, and BEFORE
`createWorldStream`, because the stream's `onBuilt` names it:

```js
  const dressing = createDressing(scene, field, { sitesNear: discovery.sitesNear });
```

**3 and 4. the stream hooks.** The two lines inside `createWorldStream`'s
options become:

```js
    onBuilt: (cx, cz, verts) => { flora.onChunk(cx, cz, verts); dressing.onChunk(cx, cz, verts); },
    onDisposed: (cx, cz) => { flora.offChunk(cx, cz); dressing.offChunk(cx, cz); },
```

**5. the descent.** In `overworldNodes()`, beside the flora line:

```js
    if (dressing.group) out.add(dressing.group);
```

Without this a rib cage stays lit while the player is two levels under the
Skull Lodge.

**6. the frame.** In `updateWorld`, after `flora.update(nowMs, x, z);`:

```js
    dressing.update(nowMs);
```

**7. the handle and the disposal.** The returned object's first line becomes:

```js
    field, world, flora, dressing, fauna, discovery, siteMarkers,
```

and the disposal line becomes:

```js
      siteMarkers.dispose(); flora.dispose(); dressing.dispose(); fauna.dispose(); world.dispose();
```

That is the whole of it. Optional, if the dev bench wants it: `sc.realmMix` is
the blend at the player as `[[realm, weight], ...]`, and `runtime.dressing.stats`
carries `chunks records drawCalls instances tris` for the settings window.

## What the dressing is

Two grids over every chunk, seeded on the chunk and nothing else.

- **ANCHOR, 32 m.** One large thing attempted per cell: a rib cage, a sandstone
  arch, a hedgerow run. Four tries, each rolling a different kind, because the
  ground refuses a KIND and not a PLACE: a cell too steep for a wreck may still
  take a cairn. When all four are refused the cell falls back to whichever of
  the realm's kinds tolerates the most slope. An anchor cell only ever stands
  empty over water, a road, a pad or the home clear. This grid is the promise
  that open country is never bare.
- **SCATTER, 16 m.** Four under every anchor cell, at the realm's density,
  thickened 1.9x where a road is near and 1.6x where a place is.

**Runs.** A kind may run: a hedgerow, a dry stone wall, a reed bed, a post
fence, a fallen column, a buried wall, and the Boneyard's spine, which is ten to
eighteen vertebrae 2.9 m apart, so a dragon's back runs up to fifty metres
across the ash. Each segment is gated on its own, so a run crossing a stream has
a gap in it rather than a vertebra in the water. Every segment carries `ri`, its
step in the run, which is how the test tells a gap from a jump.

**The gate.** One function, `openAt`, called by the placement and by the test,
so the test path is the real path. In order: the home clear, open water, the
surf line, a site's pad and its margin, a road, then the kind's own slope limit.

**Slope, measured and not guessed.** The first draft capped props at 0.2 to 0.35
rise per metre and left every hillside in the world empty. The median slope over
a hundred metres of open ground in the Boneyard's hills is 1.0 and in the
Saltmarch's 0.77. So every realm now keeps one RUGGED kind at 1.4, a loose thing
that lies where it fell: a boulder, a shard, a bone, a knot of driftwood.

**The kits**, from the sheet's `geography` lines, ninety three kinds over nine
realms, eight to twelve each, 0.8 m to 12 m:

| realm | kinds |
|---|---|
| the Greenwold | hedgerows and dry stone walls in runs, hay ricks, wayside shrines, carts, field gates, milestones, signposts, beehives, sheaves, stiles, boundary stones |
| Verdant Deep | fallen giants you can walk along, root arches, giant mushrooms, carved faces, rope bridge stubs, buttress roots, vine curtains, mushroom rings, mossed boulders, Court lanterns |
| the Saltmarch | wrecks, upturned boats, jetties, salt pans as white flats, reed beds in runs, net stakes, drying racks, crab pots, mooring posts, driftwood |
| Ember Wastes | wind carved stacks, sandstone arches and pillars, obelisks, half buried walls in runs, dead trees, sun bleached bones, bleached skulls, trail cairns, broken carts |
| the Stormpeaks | rider cairns, totems, broken columns, eyrie nests on the steep ground, post fences in runs, cairns, prayer stones, slate slabs, scree boulders, banners |
| the Boneyard | rib cages the size of houses, rib arches, dragon skulls, spines running fifty metres, half buried giants, bone trees, bone stakes, ash drifts, old bones, fallen slabs, bone cairns |
| Frostreach | ice shards, frozen pines, wrecks under snow, frozen waterfalls against the steep ground, standing stones in their ice, mammoth ribs, splinters, snow drifts, frozen stakes, rimed cairns |
| the Sunken Kingdom | marble columns standing and fallen, marble arches, drowned statues, broken pediments, coral heads, sea glass, amphorae, mosaic floors, anchor stones |
| the Ashen Throne | obsidian spires and shards, black pillars, brass pipes, Legion banners, slag heaps, cinder cones, glowing lava vents, brass wreckage, charred bones |

**The bodies.** Sixty three generic builders, composed from boxes, cylinders,
cones, spheres, arcs and lumped icosahedra, with the colours baked into a vertex
attribute. That is what lets the whole world's dressing run on four materials
(stone, ice, brass, and one glowing material for the Ashen Throne's vents) and a
chunk full of rib cages cost one draw call. A body is authored in whatever
proportions look right and then scaled so its largest dimension is exactly the
`size` its kit row asks for, so one number in `dressing.js` controls how big a
thing is and the two files cannot drift. `auditBodies` measures every one.

Every mesh carries `userData.dressing = { kind, realm, name }`. The name is the
noun with its article ("a rib cage", "a dragon's skull"), ready for the hover
pass.

## What the sky is

`REALM_SKY` in `sky.js`: nine rows, each the same five stops `SKY_KEYS` has, at
the same day factors, so a realm's whole day is authored and not guessed from
two colours. Beside the stops: the ground by day and by night, fog near and far
in metres, multipliers on the sun's colour and strength, on the hemisphere and
on the ambient, and a haze.

- **the Greenwold** the sky that existed before, unchanged, and it IS
  `SKY_KEYS`. `sky.test.mjs` pins its noon and its midnight to the numbers read
  out of the module before the table was written, every component to the bit.
- **the Boneyard** ash grey and bone white, a low brown horizon, haze 0.6.
- **Ember Wastes** hot and hazy, the longest fog in the world at 536 m.
- **the Stormpeaks** thin blue air, far fog, almost no haze.
- **Frostreach** white by day and blue by night.
- **the Sunken Kingdom** green tinted, and the nearest fog at 250 m.
- **the Ashen Throne** red over black with the horizon on fire.
- **Verdant Deep** green gold light under the canopy, a close fog.
- **the Saltmarch** pale and damp.

**The blend.** `realmMixAt(x, z)` gives `[[realm, weight], ...]` summing to one.
The realms' discs overlap, so on the line between two both weigh one and the sky
is half of each: crossing a border is a fade over the edge band and not a cut,
measured at 0.06 of a colour channel per tenth of the way. Ground claimed by
nobody takes the default sky by the share it has left over, so walking out of
the Boneyard thins the ash out of the air rather than switching it off.

**How it reaches the frame, with no wiring.** `scene.js`'s `follow(pos)` runs
every frame and now also asks `realmMixAt` where the player is. `sky.js`'s
`createSky.update` reads `sc.realmMix`, and `scene.js`'s `setDay` reads it for
the fog and the lights, so the dome, the fog and the four lamps are one blend
and never disagree.

**Why the lights are tinted and not replaced.** The curve in `scene.js` was
measured in the browser against a dark cloak in shade and a meadow at noon.
Replacing it per realm would throw that away, so a realm multiplies it instead,
and the Greenwold's multiplier is 1 in every channel. The ground is the one that
comes from colours: each realm names the ground it stands on by day and by
night, and the multiplier is that colour over the Greenwold's at the same hour.
The Greenwold over itself is 1.

**Why the fog is scaled and not set.** `setFog` is called by
`world_runtime.js` with the streamed ring, by `creation.js` with 26 to 74 and by
`roster.js` with 70 to 260. A realm scales whatever the caller last asked for by
its own reach over the Greenwold's, so the character screen keeps its close fog
and the world keeps its open one, and only the reach moves.

## What was measured

Every number below is printed by `node src/world/dressing.test.mjs`,
`node src/game/sky.test.mjs` and `node src/game/scene.test.mjs`.

**Open country is never empty.** Twenty random open points in each of the nine
realms, the nearest prop measured over the five by five chunks around each:

| realm | mean | worst |
|---|---|---|
| the Greenwold | 7.0 m | 18.4 m |
| Verdant Deep | 9.9 m | 18.6 m |
| the Saltmarch | 7.7 m | 15.2 m |
| Ember Wastes | 11.5 m | 24.3 m |
| the Stormpeaks | 11.0 m | 21.7 m |
| the Boneyard | 8.1 m | 17.0 m |
| Frostreach | 9.3 m | 17.2 m |
| the Sunken Kingdom | 9.4 m | 17.7 m |
| the Ashen Throne | 9.5 m | 23.4 m |

Worst of all nine: 23.4 m, against a promise of 60.

**Nothing stands where it must not.** 3818 props over 441 chunks, seven by seven
around each realm's centre: 0 in water, 0 on a road, 0 on a site's pad, 0 inside
`HOME_RADIUS`, 0 on ground steeper than its own kind takes. Median slope under a
prop 0.12, steepest 1.39. Seven by seven chunks around the origin: 0 props
inside the home clear and 660 outside it.

**Both directions on every gate.** Open ground passes and open water does not;
the home clear refuses and two metres past it does not; a real road at strength
0.71 refuses and sixty metres off it does not; a real site's pad refuses and
outside the pad and its margin it does not; a slope of 1.04 takes the rugged
kind and refuses the fussy one. `auditKits` and `auditBodies` and `auditSky` are
each driven through four or five failing tables as well as the real one.

**Determinism.** The same chunk builds the same props twice, the same after a
fresh field, different under a different seed, and the same whichever neighbour
loaded first.

**Cost.** On a warm field, 0.15 ms a chunk to place and 0.21 ms a chunk to place
and build, against a budget of 3. Worst single chunk 0.53 ms. Twenty five
chunks of the Boneyard: 261 props in 15 draw calls. The whole nineteen by
nineteen streamed ring standing on the Greenwold and Verdant Deep border, both
kits on screen at once: 6582 props, 30 draw calls, 1.26M triangles, 0.30 ms a
chunk. 141 bodies over the whole world, 34196 triangles in total, 243 a body,
built once in 56 ms.

**The sky.** The Greenwold's noon and midnight are exactly the sky the game had.
No two realms share a noon sky. Every realm's palette carries every key. At the
centre of a realm the mix is that realm alone and the sky is its own to the bit;
halfway between the Greenwold and Verdant Deep the mix is half of each and every
one of the twelve colour components lies strictly between the two; crossing the
border moves the zenith by at most 0.06 per tenth of the way.

**The world still reads under every sky.** `litness`, which is what the terrain
material, the water and the grass all take their light from, per realm as a
share of the meadow's, by day and by night: the Greenwold 1.00/1.00, Verdant
Deep 0.95/0.96, the Saltmarch 0.97/0.99, Ember Wastes 1.09/1.08, the Stormpeaks
1.05/1.05, the Boneyard 0.92/0.93, Frostreach 1.06/1.06, the Sunken Kingdom
0.97/0.97, the Ashen Throne 0.96/0.96. No realm is more than a tenth off the
meadow either way, so nothing goes black and nothing blows out.

**Suites.** `dressing.test.mjs` 76 green, `sky.test.mjs` 83 green,
`scene.test.mjs` 54 green, and `flora.test.mjs` 117 and `grass.test.mjs` 89
still green, untouched. `water.test.mjs`, `world_runtime.test.mjs`,
`wiring.test.mjs`, `win_settings.test.mjs` and `chunks.test.mjs` all still green.
`npx vite build` clean.

## What is NOT verified

- **Nothing has been seen in the browser.** Every claim above is a node
  measurement. The dressing is not in the bundle until the seven lines above
  are wired, and the sky's blend runs through `follow`, which only a real frame
  loop calls. The look of ninety three bodies and nine palettes wants an eye on
  it at the dev bench's tour stops.
- **The dressing does not stream until it is wired.** `world_runtime.js` is not
  mine. Until the lines land, `createDressing` is tested and unused.
- **A prop is scenery and not yet a thing.** `userData.dressing` carries the
  kind, the realm and a name, and nothing reads it. Hover, click and collision
  are for whoever comes next. Nothing here blocks the player: a rib cage is
  walked through, not around.
- **`createScene` could not be driven in node** (it wants a GL context), so the
  fog assignment inside `setDay` is proved by computing the same arithmetic in
  `scene.test.mjs` rather than by reading `scene.fog.far` off a live scene.
- **`src/world/field.test.mjs` was failing while this was written**, and the
  failures moved between runs: V1 is mid flight on `field.js` and `zones.js`.
  Every one of them is in V1's own relief work, in files Z3 did not touch, and
  none of them is mine to fix. Worth Fable's eye before Wave B closes.
- **The player is told nothing when the sky changes.** Crossing into the
  Boneyard darkens the air and nobody says so. The zone banner already fires on
  the realm; whether the sky deserves its own word is a decision, not an
  oversight.
