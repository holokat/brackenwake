# R2: the roadside after the coast moved

Written 2026-09-06. Files owned and touched: `src/world/wayside.js`,
`src/world/wayside_models.js`, `src/world/wayside.test.mjs`, this note. Nothing
else was edited. `roads.js`, `field.js`, `zones.js`, `sitegrid.js` and
`flora.js` were read only, and they moved under this work while it ran (R1);
every number below was measured after they settled, on
`createWorldField(20260904)`.

A2 furnished 22 roads over 12 km of a world that was more than half water.
Fable widened the continent mask and the road network grew to 60 roads over
33 km with 103 bridges on it, and seven of `wayside.test.mjs`'s 75 checks fell
over. Three of them were the test being wrong about a bigger world. Four of
them were real, and two of those four were bugs a player would have met.

`node src/world/wayside.test.mjs`: **95 checks, all passing.** `roads.test.mjs`
(38) and `site_models.test.mjs` (55) were run read only and both pass.
`npx vite build` is clean. `npm test` was NOT run in full, as instructed.

## The seven, one at a time

### 1. Two pieces stood in a river. A bug, and it was the same bug as 2

Both were border gates: `4,-1>5,0#gate:greenwold` in water of strength 0.98 and
`7,2>7,3#gate:sunkenkingdom` at 0.03.

Everything else that stands beside a road goes through one gate, `probeAt().ok`,
which asks four questions: is the ground above the shore line, is it out of a
river, is it off every pad, is it off the farm's disc. The border gate did not.
It stands ON the road rather than on the verge, and standing on the road had
been taken for permission to stand anywhere the road went. A ford is a place
where the road goes into the river.

The gate now walks `GATE_STEPS` looking for ground like everything else, and it
is refused for the same four reasons. Measured over the whole network: **0 of
2497 pieces stand in a river**, and the same probe driven the other way refuses
the middle of a town when asked.

### 2. Three pieces stood on a site pad. The same three gates

`-5,-2>-5,-3#gate:greenwold` stood 22 m into Tannerford's 26 m pad,
`4,-1>5,0#gate:greenwold` 16 m into a hamlet's, `4,10>4,11#gate:verdant` 19 m
into another. The pad list was right and always had been; the gate simply never
consulted it. Fixed by the same line. Measured: **0 of 2600 pieces stand on a
pad.**

Two checks were added rather than one, because `sampleAt` was answering a
smaller question than the test was asking. It hands back the site of the
point's own cell, plus the seven town precincts wide enough to reach out of
theirs, so a hamlet's 26 m pad one cell over was invisible to it. Every piece is
now measured against every pad within reach of it, at `PAD_MARGIN` outside the
pad's own edge, which is the stricter bound the placement itself keeps: **1139
piece and pad pairs looked at, 0 inside one.** The other direction is driven on
Hearthhome's 120 m precinct, the widest pad in the world.

**On `bodyR`, and why the pad rule does not read it.** Two authored places carry
a body radius and no pad at all: the Standing Hedge, a ring of stones 811 m
across, and the Obsidian Bridge, a 107 m arch. `bodyR` is how far a BODY reaches
so the site marker stays alive, not how much ground is levelled. Refusing every
lamp post inside it would put eight hundred metres of the Greenwold's road in
the dark for a ring that is empty in the middle. So the refusal reads `flatR`,
and a check counts what actually stands inside a body instead of assuming:
**0 today**, and the day a road reaches a megalith the check says so and
somebody gets to look at it.

### 3. Two settlements a road touched had no signpost. A bug

Tannerford and Lowthorpe. Both are towns whose road leaves the pad and goes
straight onto water.

Tannerford's road is bridged from 42 m past the pad to 162 m past it, and the
post's five fixed offsets ran out at 44 m, all of them on the bridge.
Lowthorpe's leaves into a river bank: the first three offsets were in the water
on one verge, and the fourth stood 5.9 m from a border gate that keeps 9 m to
itself, so `place()` refused it and nothing tried again. The old code took the
first spot the GROUND allowed and never noticed that the spot was then refused
for room.

The post now walks. `SIGN_STEP` is 9 m and it goes on to `SIGN_WALK` (300 m) or
the middle of its own road, whichever comes first, trying both verges at every
step, and a step only counts as found when the piece is actually placed.
Measured: **81 settlements on roads, 81 signs, 0 without one**, and 76
settlements with no road still have none. 77 of the 81 posts stand exactly where
the road leaves the pad; four had to walk, at 9, 18, 27 and 117 m. The 117 is
Tannerford, standing at the far end of its own bridge, which is where a board
belongs.

### 4. `deckAt` answered null in the middle of three bridges. A bug, and a worse one underneath

The three sample points were at the middle of a 182 m span, at a fifth of the
way along it, and at four fifths of a 66 m span. The reason was not the ends and
not a short span: **the deck was drawn as a straight line between the two
abutments, and the road it carries bends.**

Measured on the old records: 7 of the 106 spans crossed a bend in the road, and
on the longest, 182 m over an inlet of the Caldera Sea, the chord ran 12.1 m
away from the road at its worst. `deckAt` starts from `roadDistanceAt`, which
looks no further than 6 m from a centreline, so a point in the middle of that
deck was not on any road at all and the function correctly said there was no
road there. The body was worse than the answer: a straight beam laid twelve
metres to the side of the road it was supposed to be carrying.

So the deck is the road's own line now. `deckPath(road, t0, t1)` returns the
road's corners across the span, the record carries them as `path`, and `deckAt`
answers `roadHeightAt(road, t)`, which IS the profile the ford dives out of.
`wayside_models` builds the body along that path: `legsOf` turns it into one leg
per straight run in the piece's own frame, and the deck, the rails, the
voussoirs, the piles, the planks and the ribs are all placed at an arc length
along it. A span with one leg comes out exactly as it did before.

Measured, on all 103 bridges, walked at thirteen points each including both
abutments: **1339 deck points, 0 missed, 0 at the wrong height**, and 0 of them
nearer another road than the one the deck belongs to. Twelve metres to the side
of a span it is null on all 103. The deck's line lies on the road to 1.3e-12 m;
the chord between the same two abutments is up to 12.1 m off it, on 3 spans.
On the most bent span in the world, 182 m over two legs, every point of the
walkable deck has built geometry within 3.43 m of it, and thirty metres to the
side the nearest built vertex is 27.1 m away.

### 5. One border crossing of 21 read as unmarked. A fixture

The Verdant Deep's road `1,11>2,11` crosses the middle of the realm's edge band
at t 0.3205, and the border falls inside a 66 m bridge. The gate cannot stand on
a bridge, so it walked 26 m along the road and stood on the far bank, which is
where a real one would be. A2 matched a gate to a crossing on the FRACTION along
the road, within 0.04 of it, which is 15 m on a short road and 40 m on a long
one: 24.2 m out on a 365 m road is 0.066, and the check called it unmarked.

The match is done in metres now, and on the crossing the gate itself says it
marks rather than on how near it landed. Every crossing takes one gate and no
gate is taken twice, so a doubled match is a failure and not a pass. Measured:
**21 crossings, 21 gates, 0 unmarked, 0 marking no crossing**; the placement's
3 m walk and the test's 1 m walk agree on where a border is to 2.3 m; the
farthest any gate stands from its own border is 27.5 m against the 44 m it is
allowed to walk. Driven the other way, a gate moved a hundred metres up its own
road is 101 m against a 4 m bound and is not counted.

A second thing came out of this: a road that goes into a realm and out again
crosses twice, and both gates were built with the same id. The crossing's index
is in the id now.

### 6. "The pass has real work to do" had nothing to work on. A fixture

A2 asserted that the raw per road lists held at least one pair of pieces
standing in each other's room, which is what the chunk's overlap pass exists to
settle. That is a fact about a seed and not about the code, and the bigger
network briefly had none.

The check is rewritten to build the case. `settleOverlaps` is exported and is
the function `waysideFor` itself calls, so this is the real pass:

- two lamps from two roads meeting, 0.40 m apart against 1.6 m of room: one is
  dropped, and it is the one later in the order
- the same two 2.00 m apart: both stand
- a signpost and a lamp 1 m apart: the sign wins, handed over in either order
- the same two 4 m apart, outside the sign's 3 m: both stand

The world's own count is reported rather than asserted: 20 such pairs today,
worst 0.11 m of 1.6, every one of them a bridge's abutment lamp landing on the
station lamp beside it.

### 7. The first chunk to touch a road cost 2.39 ms against a 2 ms budget. A bug in the shape of the code

A2 named the fix and it was the right one. A road's furniture was worked out
whole road on the first chunk that touched it, and the worst chunk in the world
touches three roads of about 660 m each. Profiled: **2.31 ms of the 2.40 was one
function**, `crossingsOf`, walking each road end to end at `WALK_STEP` with a
terrain sample at every step. The lamps were 0.35 ms of it and everything else
was noise.

So a road is not laid out all at once any more. Its state is a set of probes
along it, filled on demand, and:

- **the bridges** are found over an arc window, widened either side to a CUT,
  which is two probes running that are neither in a river nor under the deck.
  A river run cannot cross a cut and neither can the walk out to an abutment,
  which only moves while the surface is below the deck, so the spans found by
  scanning from one cut to the next are exactly the spans a walk of the whole
  road would have found. They are cached per road by the probe the span starts
  at, so the same bridge found from two chunks is one bridge.
- **the lamps** are worked out per station, and a station is a point on a walk
  that asks the site grid for the local spacing and never asks the terrain.
- **the spine** is the border gates, the signposts, the shrines, the benches,
  the hitching posts and the milestones. A long road carries about a dozen, each
  of them looking at the terrain in one place, so the spine is still whole road
  and costs almost nothing.

One rule was dropped on the way: two lamps of one road no longer check each
other for room. They cannot clash, and that is a measurement and not a hope: the
stations are 24 m apart along the road and the two verges 7.8 m apart across it,
both far outside the 1.6 m a lamp keeps, and the test measures the closest pair
in the world at 7.80 m. Where a bridge's own abutment lamp does land on a
station lamp the chunk's overlap pass settles it, which is the pass's job.

Measured, cold, on a field that has never been asked anything, with the chunk's
terrain meshed first because that is the order the runtime does it in, three
runs of the whole suite:

| | A2, this network | now |
|---|---|---|
| worst median of the five slowest chunks | 2.39 ms | **1.13 to 1.46 ms** |
| mean over every chunk that holds furniture | 0.956 ms | **0.602 ms** |
| every chunk after the first | 4.1 us | 6.6 us |

`deckAt` got cheaper as well, and it is the one thing in this file that runs
every frame for every body: it no longer lays out a whole road on its first
call. Measured warm, median of nine passes over a hundred points: **0.130 us off
any road, 0.370 us on a road, 0.407 us on a bridge deck.** The first call on a
cold field over a 120 m span is 0.25 ms, which is the probes of that one ravine
and the road's own pad list.

## What holds the windowing honest

Three checks, because a chunk asking for less of a road than the road has is
exactly the shape of bug that hides as a smaller number.

- **A scrambled walk.** A field that has never been asked anything, every chunk
  in the world visited in a fixed shuffled order, so no road on it is ever laid
  out whole, against the same chunks read off a field the sweep walked end to
  end. 2580 pieces over 600 chunks, **0 different.**
- **The books balance.** Every piece the whole road lays down is either built by
  the chunk it stands in or gave way to a piece that was: **2580 built, 20 gave
  way, 0 unaccounted for**, and 2580 + 20 is 2600.
- **The bridges, walked again at a metre.** The roads are walked in the test at
  three times the resolution the placement uses, independently of it: 104
  crossings that carry the road under water or drop it more than `BRIDGE_DROP`,
  **0 left as a ford, 0 doubled**, and the 9 wet patches the road runs level
  through carry nothing.

## The world as it stands

60 roads over 33,173 m, 2600 pieces: 103 bridges, 21 gates, 81 signs, 19
shrines, 110 benches, 104 hitching posts, 26 milestones, 2136 lamps. Spans run
from 6 m to 182 m. Every piece stands on `sampleAt`'s own ground to 1.78e-15 m.
Nineteen draw calls and one light on the busiest chunk.

## What is NOT verified

- **Nothing here has been seen.** As with A2, the bodies were built in node
  behind a stub canvas. The bent bridges in particular are new geometry and have
  never been looked at: the checks say every point of the walkable deck has
  built structure within 3.43 m of it and that thirty metres to the side there
  is none, which is a measurement of where the triangles are and not of whether
  a two legged bridge reads as a bridge. Somebody should stand on the 182 m span
  on the road `3,4>4,4` and walk across it.
- **The join at a bend.** Two deck legs meet at the corner and each is grown by
  0.6 of a deck's width so they overlap rather than leave a gap. That is
  arithmetic, not a look at the seam.
- **The one call that is trusted rather than proved.** `deckAt` takes the road a
  point is nearest to be the road whose deck it is on. That is true of every one
  of the 1339 deck points walked, and the test asserts it rather than assuming
  it, but two roads running within a deck's width of each other over the same
  river would break it and this world has no such pair.
- **The ground moved while this ran.** R1 was editing `field.js`, `roads.js`,
  `zones.js` and `sitegrid.js` throughout. Every number here was taken after
  those settled, and nothing in the code or the tests depends on a road count, a
  bridge count or a coordinate.
- **`npm test` was not run in full.** `wayside.test.mjs`, `roads.test.mjs` and
  `site_models.test.mjs` were, and `npx vite build`.

## Nothing new for Fable to wire

`world_runtime.js` already imports `createWayside` and `deckAt` and already
calls `heightAt` through the deck. Both exports keep their names and their
signatures. The only shape change is inside a bridge record, which nothing
outside these two files reads: it carries `path` now, and its id names the probe
its span starts at rather than its position in a list.
