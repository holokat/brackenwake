# V3: the ground, the seams and the light

Three files and their tests, plus one browser harness.

| file | what it is | tests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `src/world/terrain_material.js` | six layers, generated tileable textures, the blend shader | `terrain_material.test.mjs`, 82 checks |
| `src/world/chunks.js` | field normals, layer weight attributes, the road ramp | `chunks.test.mjs`, 71 checks |
| `src/game/scene.js` | renderer, tone mapping, shadows, the day lighting curve | `scene.test.mjs`, 39 checks |
| `tools/terrain-shader-check.html` | the shader compiled and rendered for real | run by hand, see section 7 |

Nothing else was touched. `flora.js`, `water.js`, `sky.js`, `main.js` and
`world_runtime.js` are other people's; the sky domes and `setDay`'s sky
crossfade in scene.js are left exactly as they were for V1's swap.

## 1. What caused the lines, and how I know

Three separate things, measured before anything was changed.

### The chunk grid: normals from the mesh

`buildChunkGeometry` ended with `geo.computeVertexNormals()`. That averages
every face touching a vertex. A rim vertex touches ground faces on one side and
skirt faces on the other, and the skirt hangs straight down. So the rim of every
chunk got a normal tilted outward by the skirt.

Measured on chunk (0, 0), row j = 16, inside the home disc where the ground is
perfectly flat:

```
i= 0  x=0.0   meshN=(-0.832, 0.555, 0.000)   fieldN=(0.000, 1.000, 0.000)
i= 1  x=2.0   meshN=( 0.000, 1.000, 0.000)   fieldN=(0.000, 1.000, 0.000)
i=16  x=32.0  meshN=( 0.000, 1.000, 0.000)   fieldN=(0.000, 1.000, 0.000)
i=32  x=64.0  meshN=( 0.832, 0.555, 0.000)   fieldN=(0.000, 1.000, 0.000)
```

56.3 degrees off vertical, on ground with no slope in it. Against the sun
scene.js puts at (90, 120, 50), N dot L across one chunk went

```
0.0000   0.7590   0.8948
```

so the -x rim lost the whole of the sun and the +x rim gained 18 per cent of it.
A dark line and a bright line on every chunk edge, every 64 metres. Shading
interpolates from the rim vertex to the next one in, so the line is as wide as
the tier's vertex spacing: 2 m near the player, 4 m in the middle ring, 8 m out.

The same measurement across a shared border, before:

```
same tier, chunk (0,0) | (1,0):  33 shared verts, max normal delta 1.8974
LOD border 33 | 17:              17 shared verts, max normal delta 1.6291
```

A delta of 1.90 between two unit vectors is 143 degrees apart. Colours already
matched to 0.0000 across those borders, because colours were sampled from the
field; only the normals were not.

### The road: a 1.8 m fade sampled every 2 to 8 m

`roadStrength` runs 1 to 0 between 1.2 m and 3 m from the centreline. Walked
across the road at (1680, -720) at each tier's vertex spacing:

```
2 m:  0.00 0.00 0.66 1.00 1.00 0.29 0.00
4 m:  0.00 0.66 1.00 0.00
8 m:  0.00 0.66 0.00
```

At 2 m the colour jumps 66 per cent of the way to road brown in one vertex. At
8 m the entire road is one vertex at 0.66, and which vertex that is moves when
the tier changes. Hard band, and a band that flickers as you walk.

### The biome classifier: hard comparisons on a noise field

Found by the new continuity test, not by looking. `field.js` picks a biome with
a chain of hard comparisons. Walking north at x = 1680:

```
z -576.1  meadow  temp 0.300   grass 0.82  dirt 0.10  rock 0.02
z -576.0  boreal  temp 0.300   grass 0.58  dirt 0.26  rock 0.10
```

0.24 of the grass weight in one tenth of a metre, at temperature 0.30 exactly.
The zero set of a noise threshold is a line, and it wanders across flat meadow.

### Not the cause

- Shadow acne. Terrain meshes are `receiveShadow` only and never
  `castShadow`, so the ground cannot shadow itself. Left that way.
- The skirt's own colour. That had already been fixed: skirt vertices copied
  the rim colour. They did not copy the rim normal, which is the half that was
  still broken.

## 2. The fixes, and what they measure at now

**Normals come from the field.** Central differences on `field.heightAt` at
`NORMAL_STEP = 2` metres, a fixed world distance that does not depend on the
tier. A vertex at a given world point gets the same normal from any chunk at
any resolution.

```
same tier, x border        33 shared verts, max normal delta 0.00e+0
same tier, z border        33 shared verts, max normal delta 0.00e+0
33 meets 17                17 shared, 0.00e+0
17 meets 9                  9 shared, 0.00e+0
33 meets 9                  9 shared, 0.00e+0
far from home, 33 meets 17 17 shared, 0.00e+0
```

Colours, heights, layer weights and the road weight all match to 0.00e+0 across
the same borders. The rim of a flat chunk is 0.0000 degrees off vertical, and
N dot L across the chunk is `0.7589, 0.7589, 0.7589`.

Skirt vertices copy their rim vertex's normal, colour, both weight vectors and
the road weight, and hang exactly 4 m below it: 0 mismatches over 132, 68 and 36
skirt vertices at the three tiers.

`NORMAL_STEP` is 2 because that is the finest tier's vertex spacing, so at that
tier most of the four taps land on points the build has already sampled. With
the per-build height lattice, the cost of exact normals is:

```
33 verts   2.52 ms   against 2.36 ms for the old one-sample-per-vertex build
17 verts   1.26 ms
 9 verts   0.59 ms
```

`BUILD_PER_FRAME` is 3, so a worst-case frame spends 7.6 ms on terrain, and
`chunks.test.mjs` fails if that ever passes 16 ms.

**The road is painted on a 6 m ramp, not a 1.8 m one.** `roadWeightAt` reads
`roadDistanceAt` and fades over `ROAD_FADE`, which is `ROAD_REACH`, which is
where roads.js stops looking, so the ramp reaches zero exactly where the data
does.

```
graded road 7.9 m wide, painted road 15.3 m wide
worst step at 2 m spacing:  graded 1.00, painted 0.47
worst step at 4 m spacing:  graded 1.00, painted 0.83
worst step at 8 m spacing:  graded 1.00, painted 1.00
```

8 m spacing cannot resolve a 15 m feature and never will; that tier starts 448 m
from the player, past the fog. The test asserts the painted road beats the
graded one at every spacing and stays under 0.55 at the near tier.

`colourAt` still paints the field's own narrow `s.road` into the vertex colour,
unchanged, because `roads.test.mjs` reads that contract. The shader takes that
path off the screen instead: the biome tint is faded out by
`smoothstep(0, 0.25, vRoad)`, so wherever there is any road the vertex colour
contributes nothing and the road you look at is the dirt layer.

**The biome step is spent down to 0.04.** The four vegetated rows of
`BIOME_BASE` are now within 0.04 of each other, and what makes a boreal floor
stony and a desert sandy is `climate()`, which reads the same temperature and
moisture with no threshold in it. `biomeStepBudget()` fails the suite if a row
is ever edited back out of that budget.

```
inside the function: halving the sample step halves the worst jump, ratio 0.500
in the world:  47992 steps of 0.1 m, 20 across a biome boundary,
               worst weight jump 0.0437 (was 0.3481)
```

## 3. The attribute layout chunks.js writes

Per vertex, grid and skirt alike, `gridCount + 4 * verts` of each:

| attribute | size | contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `position` | 3 | world space, not chunk local |
| `normal` | 3 | field central difference at `NORMAL_STEP` = 2 m |
| `color` | 3 | biome tint, the old contract, unchanged |
| `aLayerA` | 3 | grass, dry grass, dirt |
| `aLayerB` | 3 | rock, sand, snow |
| `aRoad` | 1 | 0 to 1, faded over `ROAD_FADE` = 6 m |

`aLayerA` and `aLayerB` together are the six weights in `LAYERS` order and they
sum to 1 at every vertex (worst error over 4137 vertices: 4.45e-8). Split into
two vec3 attributes rather than packed, so there is no unpacking in the shader
and no precision loss.

The record `buildChunkGeometry` returns is unchanged:
`{ geo, hasWater, minH, maxH, heights, verts }`.

## 4. terrain_material.js

`createTerrainMaterial(opts)` returns
`{ material, uniforms, genMs, size, quality, setQuality, tiles, dispose }`.

Six layers, each generated as a height field and a colour-mix field in plain
JavaScript (no canvas, which is why `buildLayer` runs in node and has a test),
then turned into two RGBA byte planes:

- **albedo array**, sRGB: rgb colour, alpha roughness
- **normal array**, linear: rgb tangent-space normal with z out, alpha surface
  height

Both go up as one `THREE.DataArrayTexture` each, six slices, mipmapped,
anisotropy 8. Two samplers total; the machine reports 16 fragment texture units
and 2048 max array layers.

The noise wraps by construction: every lattice index is taken modulo the number
of cells across the texture, so the textures tile at any size. Measured seam
step against interior step, per layer, at 128 px: 1.088, 1.039, 1.080, 1.049,
0.974, 0.861.

In the fragment shader, per pixel:

1. six normal fetches, whose alpha is the layer's surface height
2. a height blend: `b_i = w_i * max(w_i + h_i * k - peak + k, 0)`, normalised.
   Weight times height, so a layer with weight zero contributes exactly zero
   however tall its texture, and a taller texture at equal weight takes 83 per
   cent of the pixel.
3. six albedo fetches, weighted by that
4. the same again on the two side planes when the surface is steep, whiteout
   blended, so a cliff has rock on it rather than smeared rock. Guarded by
   `bw.x > 0.004`, which is zero on flat ground.
5. at high quality, the same again at `TILE_B` = 17 m, crossfaded by the dirt
   layer's height channel at about 80 m a tile

`TILE_A` is 3.5 m, `TILE_B` is 17 m.

### setQuality

| quality | what it compiles | measured difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `high` | two scales, height blend, normal maps | reference |
| `medium` | one scale, height blend, normal maps | 1.75 per channel mean abs from high |
| `low` | one scale, linear blend, no normal maps | 8.50 per channel mean abs from medium |

It is a shader define plus one uniform. No geometry is rebuilt and no texture is
regenerated, so it is safe to call from a settings toggle at any time.

### Generation cost, measured

| size | node | Chrome, foreground tab | base level bytes, both arrays |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 256 | 155 ms | not measured | 3.0 MB |
| 512 | 496 ms | 316 ms | 12.0 MB |
| 1024 | 2004 ms | not measured | 48.0 MB |

`TEX_SIZE` is 512. A Chrome tab in the **background** took 2.9 s for the same
512 px set; that is tab throttling, not the generator, and the 316 ms is the
number that matters. 1024 would look better and costs roughly 4x; it is one
option away (`createTerrainMaterial({ size: 1024 })`) and I have not measured it
in a browser, so treat 4x as an extrapolation rather than a reading.

## 5. scene.js

Renderer: ACES filmic, `outputColorSpace = SRGBColorSpace`, exposure driven by
the day. `shadowMap.type` is now **`PCFShadowMap`, not `PCFSoftShadowMap`**:
three r185 deprecated the soft one and silently rewrites it to PCF on the first
shadow pass, warning once as it does. Asking for the deprecated name got the
same 3x3 kernel plus a console warning, so the code now asks for what it gets.
Three has had no legacy light mode since r165, so the intensities below are
already physical.

Shadow frustum: 55 m each way around the player (`SHADOW_BOX`) at 2048
(`SHADOW_MAP`), which is one texel per 0.054 m and a penumbra of about 0.16 m.
Near and far are 40 and 300 rather than 20 and 400, because the light sits
158 m from its target and the rest was depth precision spent on empty space.
`bias` is -0.00015 with `normalBias` 0.03, half a texel: normalBias is what
stops a curved caster striping itself, which lets the constant bias be small
enough not to detach a shadow from the foot casting it.

`lightingAt(dayFactor)` is pure and exported, three stops lerped:

| | midnight | dawn (d = 0.34) | noon |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| exposure | 0.74 | 1.14 | 1.00 |
| sun intensity | 0.10 | 1.45 | 3.20 |
| sun red over blue | 0.37 | 4.17 | 1.16 |
| hemisphere | 0.16 | 0.48 | 0.85 |
| ambient | 0.055, cool | 0.10 | 0.16, warm |
| total light on the ground | 0.239 | 1.943 | 3.450 |

The middle stop is the whole point: a sun lerped straight from moonlight to noon
is never orange, and a day with no orange hour is a day nobody believes.

`applyLighting(rig, d)` puts it on the lights and the renderer, and is what
`setDay` calls, so the node test drives the real assignment code with real THREE
lights rather than a copy of the arithmetic. `sc.lightingAt` and
`sc.applyLighting` are on the returned object.

Every existing export is intact: `createScene`, `setDay`, `setFog`, `follow`,
`resize`, `render`, `dayFactor`, `lights`, `sky`, `skyDomes`, `day`,
`fogPinned`, `dispose`, `dayFactorAt`, `DAY_CYCLE_MS`, `PALETTE`, `WORLD_FOG`,
`skyGradientTexture`. The five node names world_runtime.js hides by
(`sky`, `sun-light`, `hemi-light`, `ambient-light`, `fill-light`) are unchanged
and `world_runtime.test.mjs` still finds all five.

## 6. What main.js and world_runtime.js have to do

**Per frame: nothing.** There is no time uniform and no camera uniform. The
distance fade reads `vViewPosition`, which three already provides, and the
triplanar blend reads the vertex normal. `createWorldStream` builds the material
itself, so as it stands the ground works with no wiring at all.

Two optional hooks:

1. **A quality setting.** `createWorldStream` takes `terrainQuality` (default
   `'high'`) and the stream exposes `setQuality(q)` and `terrain`. To put it in
   the settings window, following the `setRing` and `setGrass` pattern already
   in `main.js` around line 279:

   ```js
   // world_runtime.js, on the returned runtime
   setGround(q) { world.setQuality(q); },

   // main.js applySettings
   if (typeof s.ground === 'string' && typeof runtime.setGround === 'function') runtime.setGround(s.ground);

   // win_settings.js SETTINGS
   { key: 'ground', label: 'Ground detail', kind: 'choice',
     options: ['low', 'medium', 'high'], def: 'high',
     apply: 'world.setQuality(v): a shader define, no rebuild' },
   ```

2. **Texture size.** `createWorldStream(scene, field, { terrainSize: 256 })`
   drops generation from 316 ms to about a third of that on a slow machine.
   Worth hanging off the same setting if `low` should also mean smaller.

The stream disposes the material it made. If a caller passes `groundMaterial` or
`terrain`, the caller owns it.

## 7. The harness node cannot replace

`tools/terrain-shader-check.html`. Serve the repo and open it:

```
cd /path/to/brackenwake && python3 -m http.server 5251
open http://localhost:5251/tools/terrain-shader-check.html
```

It imports the real modules, makes a real WebGL2 context, compiles the material
at all three qualities, renders two views, reads the framebuffer back and
reports how much of it was shaded and how far apart the qualities are.

It earned its place immediately. It caught two bugs the 82-check node suite
could not:

- **`vColor` is a `vec4` in three r185, not a `vec3`.** The fragment shader did
  not compile, at any quality, and the ground drew pure black. The node suite
  now asserts `color_pars_fragment` declares a vec4 and that the injected source
  reads `vColor.rgb`, so the next revision that changes it fails in node.
- **The second tiling scale contributed nothing.** Its crossfade mask read the
  red channel of the albedo array, which is an sRGB texture, so a mid brown came
  back as 0.03 to 0.32 linear and never crossed a threshold placed at 0.5. High
  and medium quality rendered byte-identical frames. It now reads the height
  channel of the normal array, which is linear, and high and medium differ on
  151638 pixels.

Last run:

```

texture set generated in 316 ms at 512 px
quality high / medium / low: 0 console errors, gl.getError 0
high:   286833 of 468000 pixels lit (61%), mean rgb 91,136,67, 410 distinct tones
medium: 286833 of 468000 pixels lit (61%), mean rgb 90,136,67, 395 distinct tones
```

Looked at: five by five chunks of flat home meadow with a 33-to-17 tier change

## 8. What is still true and not fixed

- **A biome boundary still costs 0.0437 of a weight.** It is a hard comparison
  in `field.js`, which is not mine. It is down from 0.3481 and is no longer a
  visible line, but it is a step and not a ramp.
- **`colourAt` still steps at a biome boundary too**, because it indexes the
  palette by the biome name. The shader applies that tint hue-only at
  `uTerTint` 0.42, so it is much weaker than it was, but the same threshold is
  under it. Fixing it properly means a soft classifier in `field.js`.
- **8 m vertex spacing cannot resolve the road**, and the painted weight steps
  1.00 there just as the graded one did. That tier begins 448 m out.
- **1024 px textures are unmeasured in a browser.** Only node's 2004 ms is a
  reading.
- **Nothing here has been seen in the actual game.** The harness renders the
  real material over real chunks with real lights, but `main.js` has not been
  run and the wiring in section 6 has not been applied by me.
