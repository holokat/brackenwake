# Z4: fields, scarecrows, and no gate standing in the grass

Written 2026-09-06, after the user looked at the Greenwold with Z3's dressing
on it and said: "it feels like there are too many random gates all over the
place now, also, why dont we add some fields, scarecrows in fields."

The screenshot had a dozen field gates in open meadow, each one leading from
grass into grass, hedgerow runs eighteen metres long ending in nothing, and
carts and posts scattered across country nobody was driving through. A field
gate standing alone in a meadow is a joke prop. A gate belongs in a run, and a
run belongs round a field, and the Greenwold had neither.

## Files

Changed:

- `src/world/dressing.js` the placement. The farm layout, the run's gap, the
  `only` / `chance` / `along` rules on a kit row, the open country thinning,
  `probeAt` split out of `openAt`, and `dressSeed`.
- `src/world/dressing_models.js` seven new bodies, three palette rows, the
  declared long axis measured against the built geometry, and variants by size.
- `src/world/dressing.test.mjs` 139 checks, up from 76.

New:

- `docs/mmo/wiring/Z4.md` this.

Nothing else was touched. `field.js`, `zones.js`, `sitegrid.js`, `flora.js`,
`arbor.js`, `roads.js`, `grass.js`, `chunks.js` and `world_runtime.js` are as
their owners left them.

## Wiring Fable has to do

**None.** The dressing layer has been wired in `src/game/world_runtime.js`
since Z3 (the import at line 29, the layer at 79, the two stream hooks at 89
and 90, the descent at 118, the frame at 127, the handle at 390 and the

## 1. A gate stands in a gap, or it does not stand

`field_gate` was a SCATTER kind. The 16 m scatter grid rolled one wherever it
felt like it, which is why the meadow grew gates the way it grew beehives.

It is now tier `field`, which is neither of the two grids, and so is `stile`.
Neither grid can roll one, `stubbornList` drops the whole tier so the fallback
cannot put one down either, and `auditKits` throws if a realm ever carries one
on a grid again. There is exactly one piece of code in the world that places a
gate: `emitRun`, when a run leaves a gap for it.

A run with `gate: true` takes one step out of its own middle, builds the
segments either side of the gap as usual, and puts a gate or a stile at the
midpoint of those two, only if both of them stood. A gap whose neighbour fell
in a stream gets no gate at all. The field boundaries are runs of the same
shape and go through the same code.

Measured over 405 chunks of five Greenwold neighbourhoods, 8145 props:

| | |
|---|---|
| gates and stiles found | 174 |
| standing on their own | **0** |
| carrying no run at all | **0** |
| furthest any of them is from the middle of its two neighbours | **0.00 m** (they are placed at that midpoint) |
| in a run of four segments or more | 174 of 174 |

## 2. A run reads as a field boundary

Three things were wrong with a Z3 run and each one was visible from the ground.

**It was too short.** A hedgerow was six to fourteen segments at 3 m, so 18 to
42 m: a squiggle in a meadow. It is 14 to 40 segments now, 42 to 120 m, and a
wall is 13 to 34. It also **wanders less**: 0.14 radians a step became 0.035,
and the wall's 0.10 became 0.03.

**It went straight and stopped.** A run with `corner` turns a right angle
somewhere in its middle third, so it reads as two sides of a field rather than
a line ending in nothing.

**Every segment lay ACROSS it.** This is the one worth stopping on. A hedge
body is authored along its own local X, and `emit` turned every segment to `a`,
the bearing the run walks. A body turned by `ry` lays its local +X along
`(cos ry, -sin ry)`, and the run steps along `(sin a, cos a)`; the dot product
of those two is exactly zero. Forty hedges in a row, each one turned side on.
The Greenwold's hedgerows were combs.

A kit row now declares `along: 'x'`, `ryAlong(dx, dz)` turns the body down the
line, and `auditBodies` measures the declared axis against the built bounding
box and throws if they disagree, so the two files cannot drift apart again.
32 kinds declare an axis. Measured: **4336 run segments lie along their line
and 0 lie across it**, where before the change every one of them was across.

Because a run is now three times as long, it has to start in far fewer cells or
the country fills up with hedge. `chance` on a kit row does that: a hedgerow is
in a cell's pool one time in five and a wall one time in six. Measured: 182
open country runs over those 405 chunks, **median 51.98 m, longest 87.75 m**,
up to 40 segments.

## 3. Fields

A field is not rolled on a grid. It is laid out AROUND A SETTLEMENT, which is
the only way "every village has farmland" and "the country between two villages
is open" can both be true.

**The search grows a farm rather than rolling one.** It looks for ONE field on
fourteen bearings, at four sizes and four distance bands out from the village,
and then GROWS it: a neighbour is the same rectangle one field over on the same
bearing, which is why they share a boundary instead of merely lying near each
other. Ground that refuses stops the growth on that side, so a block of four
against a wood comes out as a block of two, which is what a real farm does.

The first draft rolled a whole two by two block and took the first bearing that
kept anything, and the measurement said what was wrong with it: fourteen of
sixteen villages got a farm and thirteen of those farms were one field. Growing
instead of rolling took it to sixteen of sixteen at 2.4 fields a farm.

**What the ground has to be**, and all of it is measured:

| rule | how |
|---|---|
| meadow | `sampleAt().biome` at every probe. Not desert, not snow, not fen, not beach, not rock |
| dry | no probe in water, in the surf or in a river |
| flat | grade across the WHOLE rectangle under 0.12, corner to corner. Not the six metre slope `openAt` reads under one prop: that number is above 0.12 on nearly every meadow in the world and a field judged on it would never be built anywhere |
| nobody's | the pad is an exact circle test, not a probe. `inPad` is a circle and a field is a rectangle, and a grid of probes stepped round the edge of one and left a corner of three Greenwold farms inside the village green, by less than a metre. The circle the field is inscribed in is compared against the pad's circle instead |
| no road | the ROAD GEOMETRY, walked. `roadsOverlapping` gives the roads whose box touches the field and `roadCrosses` tests every segment of each against the rectangle in the rectangle's own frame. A road is six metres wide and a twenty metre probe grid steps clean over one |
| no wood | `arbor.standAt(biome, x, z, seed).cover` is 0 on a nine by nine grid out to the field's own edge. **That is flora's own function**, the one it asks before it grows a tree, imported rather than reimplemented: a tree stands where its cell's roll comes in under that cover, so a cover of zero everywhere inside the rectangle is a rectangle no stand reaches into |

**What a field is:** rows of one crop (wheat as instanced golden stalks, a row
of cabbages, or a ploughed strip of dark furrows, one roll a field), a boundary
of hedge or post and rail all the way round, one gate in the side that faces
the village, one scarecrow near the middle with a crow on its arm one time in
three, a hay rick in a corner one time in two, and a cart outside the gate one
time in three.

A crop row body is EIGHT METRES OF ROW and not one plant. A sixty metre field
of wheat is about a hundred and fifty instances of one geometry and one draw
call; a stalk at a time would be forty thousand.

## 4. Less in the open, and nothing less beside a road

Two rules, and both are answers to something in the screenshot.

**`only: 'road'`.** A cart, a signpost, a milestone and a wayside shrine are
things that stand BESIDE A ROAD. They are struck out of a cell's pool anywhere
else. Measured over the same sample: **8 of them stand beside a road and 0
stand out in the open**, where before the change they were spread across the
meadow. `poolFor` is driven both ways: a cart is in the pool beside a road and
out of it anywhere else.

**`OPEN_THIN`.** Where a cell has no road and no place near it, the small loose
scatter is multiplied by 0.45. Every record now carries the `near` its own cell
was placed under, so the question can be asked of the prop and not guessed from
where it stands.

The before is the SAME PLACEMENT with `openThin: 1`. The thinning is one roll
drawn after the kind, so switching it off gives back exactly the props that
were there; it is a knob at its old value and not a different code path.

Measured over 188 chunks of open Greenwold meadow, no road and no farm in any
of them, 77.0 hectares:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| loose props | 937 | 654 |
| a hectare | 12.2 | **8.5** |
| the drop | | **30.2%** |

And over the whole Greenwold sample, sorted by what each prop's own cell had
near it, which is the one number the thinning turns on:

| the cell had | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| a road near it | 11 | **11** |
| a place near it | 477 | **477** |
| neither | 782 | **410**, 47.6% fewer |

A roadside kept every prop it had, to the prop. The open is the only ground
that lost anything.

The ANCHOR grid is not thinned, and that is deliberate: its promise is that
open country is never bare, and the look book's third rule is "something
authored every forty metres". All props over the same ground went 4193 to 3910, 6.7% fewer.
What changed in the open is not mostly the count, it is what the things ARE: no
gates, no lone carts, no lone signposts, hedgerows that run a hundred metres
and turn a corner, and three new things that mean something standing alone in a
field.

**Three new Greenwold anchors**, because taking the road furniture out left the
open grid with hay ricks and little else: a **sheep fold** (a ring of dry stone
with a way into it), a **dew pond** (still water in a dug hollow, a stone rim,
reeds on one side) and the hay rick it already had.

## 5. A bug in the seed, found by measuring

`noise.hash2` mixes its seed in as `seed * 2147483647`. The world seed is
20260904, so that product is 4.35e16, past 2^53, where a double stops holding
every integer. The low bits of the seed's contribution are rounded away, two
salts of the same cell come out nearly the same number, and **every roll drawn
after another roll is correlated with it**.

Measured on the real world seed, over 160000 scatter cells whose "is there
anything here" roll passed, the kind roll that should have come out
22 / 33 / 44 per cent beehive, sheaf and boundary stone came out:

| | beehive | sheaf | boundary stone |
|---|---|---|---|
| wanted | 22.2% | 33.3% | 44.4% |
| the raw world seed | **72.2%** | 26.3% | **1.5%** |
| the seed cut to twenty bits | 24.5% | 32.2% | 43.3% |
| a seed of 1000, small enough to be exact | 24.7% | 31.7% | 43.6% |

The Greenwold's meadow was very nearly all beehives, and the boundary stone the
user asked to keep had all but vanished. `dressSeed` cuts the world seed to
twenty bits before anything in this file hashes with it, and the mix comes
back: 205 boundary stones to 81 beehives over 121 chunks, where the skew gave
the opposite.

**Only this file is fixed.** `flora.js`, `sitegrid.js` and `field.js` hash the
same way with the same seed and are not Z4's to touch. It is worth somebody's
eye: anywhere two rolls of the same cell are drawn with neighbouring salts, the
second one is not independent of the first.

Every prop in the world moves as a result. Nothing is saved about a prop's
position, so nothing on disk cares.

## 6. What it costs

Everything below is printed by `node src/world/dressing.test.mjs`.

| | |
|---|---|
| placement, warm | 0.24 ms a chunk, 20.3 props a chunk, against a budget of 3 |
| placement and build, warm | 0.25 ms a chunk |
| worst single chunk, warm | 0.50 ms |
| a chunk of farmland, placed and built | 0.26 ms over 81 chunks round Coldwell, 2003 props |
| farmland draws | 19 draws, 2003 instances, 0.43M triangles |
| the 19 x 19 ring on the Greenwold and Verdant border, both farming | 33 draws, 5554 props, 1.28M triangles, 0.20 ms a chunk |
| bodies | 160, 46478 triangles, 291 a body, built once in 70 ms |

Two of those wanted work to get there.

**33 draws on the border ring and not 40.** Every anchor kind was grown in two
bodies. A rib cage twelve metres across wants two, because two of them in sight
are two objects and one repeated is wallpaper; a hay rick four metres across
does not, because at that size a random turn and a scale between 0.85 and 1.25
is already more variety than the eye takes in. `variantsOf` now gives two
variants to anchors of 5 m or more and one to the rest. With two for everything
the border ring came to exactly 40, which is the budget and not under it.

**The farm layout is cached and asks the world for its own neighbours.** A farm
is four hundred metres across and about a hundred and fifty chunks can see one,
so `buildFarm` runs once per (seed, settlement) and is kept. It calls
`sitesNear` on its OWN centre rather than taking the calling chunk's list,
because a layout built off the caller's list would come out differently
depending on which chunk asked for it first. Measured: a farm laid out from
nothing is the farm that was there before, and a chunk full of farmland is the
same whichever way the player walked in.

The search was 14.2 ms a settlement in its first form. Two things brought it
down. A screen probe at each candidate's own centre throws out the sea, the
mountain and the road for one sample instead of sixteen. And the WOOD is asked
before the ground: `arbor.standAt` is a fifth the price of a ground sample and
a stand of trees is what refuses most rectangles by a wide margin, 556 of 800
in the first sweep of the Greenwold, so asking it first turns most failures
into six microseconds instead of thirty. A farm now lays out in about 1.6 ms,
once, ever.

**Cold chunks.** The very first chunk of a cold 19 x 19 walk near Hollowgate
costs 15.4 ms; the same chunk under Z3 cost 10.5 ms. That cost is not the
dressing: it is `field.sampleAt` and `roads.js` building every road cell and
site cell the walk touches on their first touch, which the Z3 suite's own note
calls out. Z4 pays more of it earlier because it asks for sites out to 464 m
instead of 214 m, and it only does that where something could actually farm
(nine `realmAt` calls, pure arithmetic, decide). Over the same 361 chunks the
MEAN cold chunk went 0.41 ms to 0.34 and the warm chunk 0.33 to 0.24, both
better than Z3, because the open country carries fewer props.

## What is NOT verified

- **Nothing has been seen.** Every number above is a node measurement. Seven
  new bodies (wheat, cabbages, furrows, a rail fence, a scarecrow, a scarecrow
  with a crow, a sheep fold, a dew pond) have never been looked at in a frame.
  `auditBodies` says each is the size its kit asked for, stands on the ground
  and is light enough to instance; it cannot say whether a field of wheat reads
  as a field of wheat at eighty metres, or whether the scarecrow's silhouette
  carries. That wants an eye at the dev bench.
- **A lone tree can still stand in a field.** The wood check refuses any stand
  of trees, which is what flora grows a wood out of, but flora also allows one
  LONE tree to every sixty metre square of open ground and that lattice knows
  nothing about a field. An oak in the middle of a wheat field is the right
  answer anyway, so it was left; if it ever looks wrong, the fix is in
  `flora.js` and not here.
- **Grass still grows in the corn.** `grass.js` was not touched and does not
  know a field from a meadow. Whether a crop row wants the grass turned off
  under it is a look decision nobody has made.
- **A field is scenery.** `userData.dressing` carries the kind, the realm and a
  name for every new thing, and nothing reads it. A gate does not open, a field
  is walked through and not around, and a crop cannot be harvested.
- **The hash bug is fixed in one file only.** `flora.js`, `sitegrid.js` and
  `field.js` draw correlated rolls from neighbouring salts of the same cell in
  exactly the way section 5 describes, and were left alone because they are not
  Z4's. Somebody should look.
- **The cold first chunk is 15.4 ms.** It is 10.5 ms without Z4 and the extra
  is site and road cells warming earlier than they used to. Nothing here
  amortises it across frames.
