# D2: the world clock, and Wyrmsoul

`14-KALDERA.md` section 3, built. For six seconds (ten after Frostreach) the
player and the dragon are one creature: the world moves at a fifth of its
speed, the dragon's breath comes out of the player's hands, and everything the
player touched lands at once when time resumes.

D1 (`D1-DRAGON.md`) built the animal, the Bond and the nine gift ids. This is
what holding one of those gifts DOES, plus the clock the whole thing runs on.

```

src/game/wyrmsoul_visuals.js         wings, eyes, trails, the see-through pass

src/game/app/system.js               one sync per frame, at the top of the first phase
```

## 1. The world clock

The frame record now carries two clocks.

```js
frame = { dt, now, nowS,                    // the player
          worldDt, worldNow, worldNowS,     // everything else
          timeScale,                        // worldDt / dt, for anyone who wants it
          day, night, centre }
```

**In ordinary time the two are the same number to the millisecond.** That is
measured, not assumed: sixty ordinary frames leave a debt of `0.000 ms`.

`ctx.clock` (`createWorldClock` in `app/context.js`) owns the second one and is
the only thing that can move the two apart. `app/system.js` syncs it once a
frame, at the top of whichever phase runs first, and `sync` is idempotent on
`frame.now`, so a test that drives one phase gets the same clock the game does.
There is no second code path.

Three rules the clock will not break, each of which was a bug waiting:

- **Monotonic.** It never goes backwards, so a job already in `combat.js`'s
  pending list cannot become un-landed.
- **Never ahead.** It is clamped to the player's clock.
- **At full speed it IS the player's clock**, rather than an integral of `dt`.
  `main.js` clamps `dt` to 50 ms and does not clamp `now`, so integrating would
  make the world lose whatever a hitch cost, for ever. A tab left in the
  background for a minute would come back with a world a minute behind and every
  queued swing stranded. Measured: a 60 000 ms hitch leaves a debt of `0.000 ms`.
  Debt exists ONLY while the world is deliberately slowed.
- **Paid back.** `catchUp(ms)` pays the whole debt over that many milliseconds of
  real time. Measured: six seconds at one fifth gives the world `1200.0 ms` and a
  debt of `4800 ms`; half a second of catch up moves it `5300 ms` and the two
  clocks are `0.00 ms` apart again.

`frame.worldDt` carries the same 50 ms ceiling `main.js` puts on `dt`, so the
catch up pays its debt in CLOCK time (which is what makes the swings land) and
not in one enormous step of monster movement.

### The headline measurement

> "a monster 4 m away closes 1 m in the time the player closes 5 m"

Driven with `monsters.stepToward`, the real movement function, at the same
speed for both, for exactly one real second of dragon time:

| | closes |
| --- | --- |
| the player, on `dt` | **5.000 m** |
| the monster, four metres off, on `worldDt` | **1.000 m** |
| the ratio | **5.000** |
| the same monster in ordinary time | 4.000 m (it arrives) |

Driven again through the real `stepMonster` AI, a wolf twelve metres out over
two seconds: **10.63 m** in ordinary time against **3.40 m** in dragon time,
which is 3.13 times and not 5. That is not a bug and it is not rounded to the
tidy number: `stepMonster` is a state machine with an alert delay and a wander
it has to leave, and those cost FRAMES rather than seconds, so the slow run
spends the same frames in them and comes out ahead of a flat fifth.

**The player's clock (`dt`, `now`, `nowS`), and on purpose:**

| file | call | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `systems/player.js` `move` | `rig.update(dt, ...)` | he never slows |
| `systems/player.js` `late` | `tickPools(actor, dt, inCombat(actor, now))` | his pools are his |
| `systems/abilities.js` `update` | `abilities.update(dt, nowS)` | "cooldowns and casts run at full speed" |
| `systems/abilities.js` `update` | `itemBar.update(dt)` | the same |
| `systems/abilities.js` `late` | `effects.update(dt)` | see the note below |
| `systems/combat.js` `run` | `targeting.update(dt)` | the cursor is the player's |
| `systems/combat.js` `run` | `updateAttack(now, nowS)` | the auto attack's rhythm is his own |
| `systems/combat.js` `late` | `targetRing.update(dt, ...)` | a HUD ring |
| `systems/combat.js` `swingAt` | `monsters.swingAt(actor, t, { now })` | his swing, his clock |
| `systems/dragon.js` `update` | `entity.run(frame)` | "the player, the dragon ... at full speed" |
| `systems/dragon.js` all of Wyrmsoul | `frame.now`, `frame.dt` | the effect is the player's |
| `systems/ui.js` `late` | `hud.update(dt, ...)`, `floaters.update(dt)` | "and the HUD" |
| `systems/inventory.js` `late` | `tradeNet.update(now)` | a network channel, not a world |
| `systems/input.js` `click` | `route(ray, now, nowS)` | a click is the player's |

**`effects.update(dt)` on the player's clock is a decision, not an oversight.**
The pool holds the player's own bolts and the swing, cast and flinch clips on
the player's rig, and every one of those is the player's. A monster's SHOT is
not in there at all: `monsters.js` steps its own projectiles inside
`monsters.update`, which is already on the world clock, so an arrow really does
hang. What is on the player's clock and belongs to the world is the impact
burst a monster's blow throws, which lasts a third of a second and is over
before anybody could time it.

**`frame.day` and `frame.night` as `main.js` writes them are still the player's
clock**, and `systems/player.js` reads `frame.day` for the lantern. `main.js` is
not this piece of work's file. The consequence is exactly one lamp being about
five seconds of a six minute cycle out of step with a sky that is hanging, which
is under a hundredth of the cycle and is not visible. Everything that draws the
sky, the sea or the light of the day takes its own day factor from `worldNow`.

### Combat's pending list, and the joint the whole thing turns on

`combat.js` (which is not this piece of work's file, and was not edited) stamps
every queued job with `at = opts.now + SWING_LAND_S`, off whatever clock the
QUEUER used. A monster queues from inside `monsters.update`, which was handed
the world clock. The player queues through `systems/combat.js`'s `swingAt`,
which passes `frame.now`. One pending list, driven by the world clock:

- **a monster's swing lands on the world clock.** It hangs, and lands when the
  world gets there, which is in the pulse.
- **the player's swing lands on the player's clock**, as soon as the world clock
  reaches that number. Which for most of dragon time is the pulse.

### Where the player's own blows really land, measured

This is the one place the design's sentence and the arithmetic do not line up
perfectly, and it is measured rather than glossed. Six swings, one a second,
through the real resolver, during a six second call:

| thrown at | lands at |
| --- | --- |
| 0.0 s | **1.52 s** (inside dragon time) |
| 1.0 s | 6.02 s |
| 2.0 s | 6.12 s |
| 3.0 s | 6.20 s |
| 4.0 s | 6.30 s |
| 5.0 s | 6.40 s |

So a swing thrown in the first second lands inside dragon time, five times
slower than the 0.3 s it takes in ordinary time, which reads as your own blow
hanging with everything else. Every later one waits for the pulse. **One of six**
lands early and five land at the end.

**The breath is not left to that.** It is HELD and released in the pulse, one
entry a frame, in the order it was thrown, so "everything you touched happens at
once" is literally true of the thing the ability is actually for. One a frame is
what makes the order a fact rather than a hope: `combat.update` flushes
everything due in a frame in REVERSE queue order, so two jobs falling in one
frame come out backwards. One a frame cannot.

Measured, over 600 frames: the key held for four and a half seconds throws
**five** breaths (the one-a-second gate), each catching **five** bodies, and all
**25** are accepted by the resolver with **0** refused. Nothing is left held,
`combat.pendingCount` is **0** at the end, every one of the five bodies lost
health, none of them lost any while the world was hanging, and the order they
landed in was **0, 1, 2, 3, 4**.

## 2. `wyrmsoul.js`: the rules

Pure, exported, and driven both ways in the test. Nothing in the file has a
scene, a HUD, an audio cue or a clock of its own.

```js
canCall(record, dragon, actor, { now, state, playerPos, underwater })
  -> { ok, reason, say }
call(state, now, record)   -> the new state, plus bondTo and seconds
tick(state, now)           -> { state, running, ending, justEnded, left }
giftsHeld(record)          -> { base, senses, tail, trueFire, wings, roar, frost, deep, last }
breathFor(state)           -> [fire] or [fire, frost]
durationMs(record)         -> 6000, or 10000 with Frostreach
timeScaleFor(state)        -> 0.2 while running, 1 otherwise
inCone / coneTargets       the breath's shape
tailSweep                  the knockback on the call
playerTier / roarOutcome   the end pulse's roar
flyStep                    the wings, bounded
fireGround / inGround / groundBurn    the Ember Wastes' ground fire
deathSwap                  the Ashen Throne's gift. NOT WIRED: see below.
CALL_LINE / endLine / tooltipFor / giftGainedLine     the words
auditWyrmsoul()            called at import; a tenth gift cannot ship silently
```

### The numbers, and where each came from

| | value | source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `CALL_BOND` | 100 | the design |
| `BASE_MS` / `FROST_MS` | 6000 / 10000 | the design |
| `TIME_SCALE` | 0.2 | the design |
| `END_MS` | 500 | the design's "over half a second" |
| `TIRED_MS` | 30000 | the design |
| `BREATH_RANGE_M` | 15 | the design |
| `BREATH_EVERY_MS` | 1000 | the design |
| `TAIL_M` / `TAIL_KNOCK_M` | 4 / 4 | the design's "within four metres" |
| `ROAR_STUN_S` | 1.5 | the brief |
| `FLY_CEILING_M` | 30 | the brief |
| `APART_M` | 40 | exported from `dragon.js`, written once |
| `BREATH_ARC_DEG` | 60 | **INVENTED.** The design gives the length and calls it a cone and gives no angle. The widest arc that still reads as a cone, and the same kind of number `abilities_runtime`'s `arcDegrees` already is. |
| the breath's damage | the age's bite, doubled | **DERIVED**, not tabled twice, off `dragon.js`'s `AGE_STATS`. hatchling 2 to 6, drake 12 to 24, young 28 to 52, dragon 60 to 100. A bite is one set of teeth; the breath is the whole animal's fire. |
| `FIRE_GROUND_R` / `_S` / tick | 7.5 m / 8 s / a fifth of the breath's low roll, once a second | **INVENTED.** The design gives "ignites the ground and burns after time resumes" and no numbers. Eight seconds outlives the six second effect, which is the whole point of the sentence. |

### The nine gifts, and what each switches on

| gift id | realm | power | what it does here | wired |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `greenwold` | the Greenwold | `base` | the time scale and the fire breath | yes |
| `verdant` | Verdant Deep | `senses` | the material pass: monsters visible through walls | yes |
| `saltmarch` | the Saltmarch | `tail` | a 4 m knockback pulse on the call | yes |
| `ember` | Ember Wastes | `trueFire` | the breath leaves burning ground for 8 s | yes |
| `stormpeaks` | the Stormpeaks | `wings` | Space flies, ground to ground + 30 m | yes |
| `boneyard` | the Boneyard | `roar` | flee under your tier, stun the rest 1.5 s | yes |
| `frostreach` | Frostreach | `frost` | a second breath on slot 2, and ten seconds | yes |
| `sunken` | the Sunken Kingdom | `deep` | lifts the underwater guard on the call | yes |
| `throne` | the Ashen Throne | `last` | the one time death swap | **no. See below.** |

`base` is true for every call whether the Greenwold's gift is held or not: a
player who reached a Bond of 100 without it is not made to stand there holding a
key that does nothing.

`auditWyrmsoul()` runs at import and fails loudly if a gift id switches nothing
on, if a power hangs off an id `dragon.js` has never heard of, if a power has no
line to say, or if an age's breath comes out at zero. `D1-DRAGON.md` owns which
gifts are held; this owns what holding one does; two files agreeing by hand is
two files that will one day disagree.

### `deathSwap`: the one thing that is not wired, and why

The Ashen Throne boss fight does not exist in this build, and the design gives
the swap to that fight and to no other. `deathSwap(actor, dragon, opts)` is
exported and exposed as `window.__bw.wyrmsoul.deathSwap`, and called without
`{ boss: true }` it returns

```js
{ swapped: false, reason: 'no boss fight',
  say: 'The last gift waits for the Ashen Throne. There is no such fight in this build yet.' }
```

and changes nothing. A one time gift that could be burned in a meadow is worse
than a gift that is not there yet. With `{ boss: true }` it hands back the two
writes (`playerHealth`, `dragonFalls`) for the fight's own code to make. Both
paths are driven in the test.

## 3. The system

`systems/dragon.js` gained three hooks and is the only place any of this touches
the running game.

- **`hotkeys`.** R calls it. While it is running, the bar's first slot (and its
  second, with Frostreach) is the breath. The press is **swallowed**, and this
  phase runs BEFORE `abilities.update`, so the ability in that slot never sees
  the key. `abilities_runtime.js` needed no line changed. Driven both ways: with
  Wyrmsoul up, `1` is swallowed and `2` is not until the frost gift is held;
  outside Wyrmsoul the bar keys are nobody's business but the bar's.
- **`move`.** The wings, after the player system has walked the body. The dev fly
  camera's own movement rule out of `camera.js`, bounded to the ground and 30 m
  over it, writing the same `rig.pos` the walk writes, which `player.js` already
  knows how to be pushed about through. Measured: Space lifts the body to 14 m in
  half a second and never past 30 m; without the Stormpeaks gift Space does
  nothing at all and the body stays at 0.00 m.
- **`update`.** After `entity.run(frame)`, which is D1's. The tick, the end
  pulse, the held breaths, the ground fire, the visuals and both HUD widgets.

### The call

Bond to 0, the dragon tired for thirty seconds, the clock to 0.2, the visuals
on, the cue, and the tail if the Saltmarch gave it back. Every one of those says
something:

```
Wyrmsoul. The world slows. 6 seconds of it, and the Bond is spent.
You see what is alive through leaf, wall and dark.          (with the senses)
Space flies while it lasts, and you land where you are when it ends.  (with the wings)
The tail sweeps: one thing goes off 4 m and off their feet. (with the tail)
```

A refused call says the reason and plays `denied`:

```
The Bond is at 0. Wyrmsoul answers at 100.
It is too far to answer: 47 m, and the pact reaches 40.
It is down, and cannot answer. Fight over it until the Bond climbs.
You are stunned, and cannot call it.
You are dead. Nothing answers the dead.
The fire will not take under the water. The Sunken Kingdom is where that changes.
Wyrmsoul is already up.
```

### The end

The roar (if held), the cue, the flash, the clock's `catchUp(500)`, and the held
breaths released one a frame. When the pulse is over, and not before, the line
with the counted number in it:

Three lines taken off the test's own log rather than composed here:

```
The roar goes out at tier 2: 1 run, 1 are stunned for 1.5 s.
Time catches up: 25 breaths land.
Time catches up: 3 blows land.
It is spent. It fights, but the Bond does not climb for 30 s.
```

A call that landed blows, breaths, a rout and a stun together reads
`Time catches up: 5 blows and 3 breaths land, 2 of them run, one is left
standing and stunned.`, which is `endLine` driven directly in the test.

Nothing in that line is a number that was not counted. `landed` is what the
resolver ACCEPTED at release; anything it refused because the thing was already
down by the time time caught up is said separately.

### The tired window, and the one place this reaches into D1's number

"The dragon is tired for thirty seconds: it fights, but no Bond is gained."
`dragon.js` owns the Bond and is not this piece of work's file, so the gain is
taken back the moment it is made, off the three events D1 fires AFTER the write
with `gained` on them (`hitTogether`, `fed`, `tookBlow`). One place, three
events, said once rather than every swing:

```
It is still spent from Wyrmsoul, so that was worth no Bond.
```

Measured: a feed inside the window moves the Bond from 0.0 to 0.0, and the line
is said once over the whole window. The tidier fix is one `if` in `dragon.js`
and belongs to its owner.

### Dying inside it

`combat.js` is the one place anything dies, so this is the one place that is
told. The effect ends, the clock is put back and the debt paid, the visuals come
off, the Bond empties and the dragon falls with you. Measured: all five.

```
You go down inside Wyrmsoul. The Bond empties, and it falls with you.
```

### A gift gained

D1's own line says which realm gave something back. This says what it MEANS,
once, and it is watched off `record.gifts.length` rather than off an event,
because a realm's dungeon calls `entity.grant` directly and there is no gift
event to listen to. Both paths are driven in the test.

```
the hatchling takes back wings from the Stormpeaks.        (D1's line)
Wyrmsoul can do one more thing: Space flies, for as long as it lasts.   (this one)
```

## 4. The HUD

### The Bond arc

`hud.setBond(value, name, callable)`, called every frame by the dragon system,
which runs after the fight and before `ui.late`, so what the Bond did this frame
is drawn this frame.

A long gold arc beside the pools rather than a fourth bar, because the Bond is
not a pool: it is not spent by walking about and it does not come back on its
own. One SVG semicircle of radius 26 with a dash offset for the fill, re-strung
only when the rounded number moves. The dragon's name is over it, and it is
**"the hatchling"** until the player names it. At 100 the arc goes bright gold
and glows and the name goes with it. `hud.clearBond()` takes it off for a
character that has no dragon.

`bondDash(fraction)` and `bondArcSvg(fraction)` are pure and exported, and both
are driven at 0, 0.5, 1, 9 and -3.

### The thirteenth cell

At the far right of the rail, outside `BAR_KEYS`, where nothing else may go.
`hud.setWyrmsoul(state)` lights it when callable, burns it while the effect is
up with the seconds left in it, and dims it otherwise. Clicking it goes through
the same door as the key.

**The key is `R`, and it is free.** Every key already spoken for was counted
before it was chosen, and the test drives the list rather than taking it on
trust: `windows.RESERVED_KEYS` (w a s d q e space shift control tab), the eleven
window keys (C B K P V M N Escape F2, and the two panels with no key), the twelve
bar keys (1 to 0, minus, equals), the eight item keys (F5 to F12), the four tool
keys (1 to 4, shared with the bar), and dev's F1 and backquote. R is in none of
them.

**The mark is drawn, not painted:** a dragon's eye in gold, one inline SVG, a
lidded almond around a radial iris with a slit pupil, a catchlight and a brow
ridge. It is a real mark rather than a letter in a box while it waits for art.

**The tooltip** is the same card as an ability's: name, key, chips
(`6 s`, `100 Bond`, `within 40 m`), what Wyrmsoul is, which realms have given
something back, the tired line in amber, and in red the reason it will not
answer right now. `wyrmsoul.tooltipFor()` builds the words and is pure.

### The screen, while the world is slow

`#bw-wyrmshell` is a fixed div with one radial gradient: clear in the middle,
amber at the edges, `pointer-events: none`, faded in over 0.18 s. It goes on
with `active` and off with it.

`#bw-wyrmflash` is the white sheet at the end. `hud.wyrmFlash(1)` raises it and
`hud.update` runs it down on the PLAYER's clock, so it is over in `FLASH_S`
whatever the world is doing. Measured: **28 frames, 0.47 s**, against a
`FLASH_S` of 0.45.

### The two cues

`tools/synth-sfx.mjs` grew two recipes; `node tools/synth-sfx.mjs` writes them,
deterministically, and `audio.js`'s `SYNTH_FILES` grew two rows.

- **`wyrmsoul_call`**, 1.9 s. A saw at 41 Hz with five harmonics bent DOWN a
  fifth over the file (the sound of everything slowing), lowpassed at 700 Hz,
  with a 5 Hz growl on its amplitude; three heartbeats under it at 0.10, 0.72
  and 1.34 s, each a lub-dub pair of 55 to 28 Hz sines 140 ms apart; a long dark
  room behind it.
- **`wyrmsoul_end`**, 1.0 s. A 12 ms click of full band noise for the snap, a
  sine sweeping 320 Hz to 2.6 kHz in 90 ms for the flash, then a bandpassed
  noise swell for everything the world owed arriving at once, with a 55 Hz sine
  under it for the weight of it landing.

**What that costs, said out loud.** The synth tool caps a file at two seconds and
`audio.js` has no looping cue, so the heartbeat runs for the first 1.9 s of a six
second effect and then the amber at the edges of the screen carries it alone. A
third, looping bed is the thing to make the day somebody wants it. It is written
into `audio.js` beside the two rows as well as here.

## 5. The visuals

`wyrmsoul_visuals.js`. Attaches to `player.js`'s rig ANCHORS and never edits the
rig or its poses: `parts.back` and `parts.head` are two Object3Ds the pose
already moves, so a wing on the back and an eye on the head follow the body for
free. `player.js` needed no line changed, which matters this week because
somebody else is in it.

**304 triangles for the whole effect**, counted off built geometry by
`auditWyrmsoulVisuals()` and printed by the test, against a budget of 2,000:

| | triangles |
| --- | --- |
| two wings, 4 x 4 segments each | 64 |
| two eyes, an 8 x 6 sphere each | 160 |
| the trails, one instanced quad, capped at 40 | 80 |
| **total** | **304** |

- **The wings** are two additive planes on the back anchor wearing a flame
  texture generated in code: a vertical gradient from white hot through orange to
  a dark tip, cut by three octaves of value noise, faded to nothing at every
  edge. Deterministic from one integer hash, so it is the same bytes in node and
  in the browser and a test can read it back. Measured: 32 x 32 gives 32 colour
  bands with both fully opaque and fully clear pixels, and two calls are byte
  identical. They open over about a tenth of a second (0.130 to 0.987 over 31
  frames) rather than popping, and they beat on the PLAYER's clock, at full speed,
  while the world hangs.
- **The eyes** are two small emissive spheres at `y = 0.166` in the head's frame,
  which is where `player.js` puts the eyes, in the gold of the pact.
- **The trails** are one `InstancedMesh` of a single additive gold quad, stretched
  along each monster's own heading by its speed. A thing that did not move gets
  none: measured, one mover and one stander give a count of exactly 1, and the
  first frame of all gives 0 because a trail needs two frames of position before
  it means anything.
- **The senses pass costs no triangles at all.** Every monster mesh's material is
  taken and replaced with ONE shared additive gold material with `depthTest`
  off, which is the whole of seeing through a leaf, a wall and the dark, and every
  one of them is handed back when the effect ends. The originals are kept per
  MESH and not per monster, because a monster's parts share materials and a map
  keyed by monster would put the wrong one back on a swapped limb. Measured: two
