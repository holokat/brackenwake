Wave B, task T1. Written after the work, with the numbers this file quotes
measured by `node src/world/town_layout.test.mjs` on world seed 20260904.

## What is there now

Seven precinct towns are built rather than scattered. Each one is a wall with
gates, a paved square with a well or a fountain and a waystone at its edge, an
inn with a sign, a smith with a forge, a healer, a stable with pens, a bank,
stalls round the market, houses out to the wall, streets between all of it, and
whatever that realm puts between its buildings. Five of the people who live
there stand in their own doorways.

## The three decisions worth arguing with

**The wall stands at 66 m, not at the precinct's edge.** `field.js` grades a
pad by blending raw ground into the site's height from `flatR * 0.55` outward,
so a 120 m precinct is dead flat only to 66 m. Measured on the real field, the
height spread round the ring at 70 m is at most 1.1 m and at 96 m it is 42.9 m
at Cairnfoot. A wall at 96 would be a wall on a cliff. So the town proper is
132 m across and the rest of the precinct is its approach, which is where the
ring of stones round Hearthhome and the cairns above Cairnfoot stand.

**The gates face the cardinal points.** The contract asks for the road links
where they can be read. They cannot be read from here: `roads.linksForCell`
wants a field, and `site_models.buildSiteMarker(site, heightAt)` has none. It
also turns out to matter very little. Measured: Hearthhome, the Red Queen's
Harbour, the Last Well, Cairnfoot and Coldseat have **no** road links at all,
and the Canopy Court and Cinderport have one each. `layoutTown` takes
`opts.bearings` and uses them when a caller has them; the line that would
supply them is below.

**The harbours face the water that is really there.** Neither port has sea
inside its pad. `seawardOf` walks 48 bearings out to 118 m on the real height
field and takes the one whose ground crosses `SEA_LEVEL` soonest. Cinderport
comes out at 308 degrees with the shore at 86 m and the water 14 m deep beyond
it, so its three barges float. The Red Queen's Harbour comes out at 113 degrees
with the shore at 114 m, so its jetties cross a tidal flat first and its three
hulls sit aground on it, heeled over, which is what a town built out of wrecks
looks like. `waterlineAt` is the rule: a hull's waterline is the sea, or the
ground plus half a metre where the ground is higher, so no hull is ever floating
in the air or buried in the mud. Measured on all six.

Over all seven, measured in `town_layout.test.mjs`:

- **Every lot inside the precinct.** The furthest corner of anything is 114.1 m
  of the 115 allowed, and it is a standing stone in Hearthhome's ring.
- **No two lots overlap.** 5,939 pairs tested by separating axis on the real
  rectangles at their real angles, 0 overlaps. `lotsOverlap` is driven both
  ways, including a rectangle clipping another's corner at 90 degrees.
- **The square is empty.** The nearest corner of anything to a town's middle is
  20.8 m and the square is 12 m.
- **Every named building once.** inn, smith, forge, healer, stable, pens, bank,
  in all seven, exactly one each. The inn is the largest building in its town.
- **Every gate on the wall.** 22 gates over seven towns, all at the wall's own
  radius and all a gap in it. Two to four each.
- **Nothing stands in a street.** The closest building is 0.81 m off the kerb
  and the rule is 0.8 m.
- **Determinism.** The same site and seed lay the same town out, byte for byte
  through JSON. A different seed lays a different one out and it is still legal.
- **A rolled town declines.** The four rolled towns the world puts within 30
  cells of the origin all get `null`, as does an authored site that is not one
  of the seven, as does a site with no `sub`.
- **Nothing floats and nothing is buried.** `footingFor` stands every body on
  the lowest ground under its own footprint and makes the difference up in a
  stone plinth. Inside the wall the worst fall under a building is 0.000 m, so
  no building has a plinth. Twelve bodies on the graded shoulder do, the worst
  falling 2.74 m across itself.
- **The waystone survives the merge.** It is built, merged and tagged on its
  own and joined to the town afterwards, the way `mine_models` does a cut.
  Merged with the town it would have kept `userData.waystone` on paper and lost
  it to a click.

## The Banker, and one thing done differently from the contract

The contract asks for `banker` with `appearsIn: ['town']` and the service
`bank`, and says the panel is Talk until banking exists. Two things followed
that the contract did not say and the code needed.

**The Banker has `buys: ['ingots']`.** `win_talk.auditTalk()` runs at import and
refuses at load any role whose panel would offer nothing but a Talk tab. A
Banker with no goods and no trainable skill is exactly that role, so adding one
as written would have made the game fail to boot. A bank buying bullion is true
to what a bank is, and it gives the panel a second tab. When there is a Bank
tab, `buys` can go. The line for that is below.

**The Banker has `needs: 'bank'`.** Adding a role to `appearsIn: ['town']` puts
it in the pool `npcsFor` draws from, and the world's rolled villages have no
bank, so bankers began appearing in fields. `needs` was already the Ranger's
gate for a forest edge; it is now general, `auditNpcs` refuses a `needs` nobody
supplies, and `streetFor` passes `bank: !!plan`, which only the seven have.

`docs/mmo/wiring/WAVE-B.md` was added to the list of documents
`npcs.test.mjs` checks its vocabulary against, because the Banker and the
`bank` service are written down there and not in the four design documents.

## Wiring Fable has to do

None of it is needed for the towns to work. All four are improvements to
something already standing.

**1. A waystone click, in `src/game/world_runtime.js`, in `pick()`.** The
waystone carries `userData.waystone = true` and `userData.site`, and the pick
already finds it, but it comes back as an ordinary site hit. One line carries
the flag out:

```js
          if (site) { cands.push({ d: h.distance, out: { kind: 'site', site, waystone: !!h.object.userData.waystone } }); break; }
```

Then `interact.js` can say what touching it did, which the contract asks for.

**2. Gates that face the roads, in `src/world/site_models.js`, in
`buildSiteMarker`.** `buildTown` already takes `opts.bearings`. If the field
reaches this far, the line is:

```js
  if (site.kind === 'town' && site.authored) { const t = buildTown(site, heightAt, opts); if (t) return t; }
```

with `createSiteMarkers` passing `{ bearings: linksForCell(field, site.cx, site.cz).map((b) => Math.atan2(b.x - site.x, b.z - site.z)) }`. Worth two of
the seven towns, and only one gate each; the cardinal points are the answer for
the rest.

**3. A Bank tab, in `src/game/win_talk.js`, in `tabsFor`.** When banking exists:

```js
  if (role?.services?.includes('bank')) out.push('bank');
```

and then `NPCS.banker.buys` can go back to `[]`.

**4. The Red Queen's Harbour wants water dug for it, in `src/world/field.js`
(V1's file).** The pad levels 120 m of ground at y 7.4 and the sea is at
`SEA_LEVEL`, -0.8, so the town's own harbour basin is drawn as a plane over
ground that is above it and only shows past 106 m. The jetties and the aground
hulls read correctly as they are, and this is not a blocker; but a basin cut
into the seaward arc of that one pad, below `SEA_LEVEL`, would let the engine's
own water fill it and the ships float.

## Reading the plan

```js
layoutTown(site, seed, opts) -> null | {
  id, sub, realm, kind: 'town', name, x, z, precinctR, palette, spec, note,
  wall:      { kind, r, thickness, height, gaps: [{ kind, bearing, half }] },
  gates:     [{ kind: 'gate'|'harbour', x, z, yaw, bearing, w }],
  square:    { x, z, r, centre: { kind: 'well'|'fountain'|'sweetwell', x, z, r } },
  streets:   [{ kind: 'avenue'|'quayway'|'ring', x1, z1, x2, z2, w }],
  lots:      [{ kind, x, z, w, d, yaw, r, ring, bearing, outside?, beyond?, prop? }],
  buildings: lots without the dressing and without the water side,
  props:     the dressing, a view of lots,
  waystone:  { x, z, yaw, h: 4, r },
  port:      null | { bearing, shoreR, quayR, reach, half, mole, quay },
}
```

`REQUIRED_LOTS` is the seven kinds every town has exactly one of.
`lotOf(plan, kind)` and `lotsOfKind(plan, kind)` read them out.
`doorOf(lot, out)` is where somebody stands to be at a door and which way they
look, and it is the same function `npcs_runtime` and `town_models` both use, so
a lantern and an innkeeper are never at two different doors.
