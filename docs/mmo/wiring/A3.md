**The count.** A kind table cannot change how many sites there are, only which
kind each one is. So the chance a cell holds anything went up with the table:
`SITE_CHANCE` 0.62 inside the heart, `WILD_CHANCE` 0.93 outside it. Measured
over 6400 cells: 92.9% of wild cells hold something, of which the old seven
kinds are 61.4%, against the 62% they have always had. **The total count of
rolled sites is x1.51, and not one town or hamlet was lost to make room.**

**The heart does not move.** A cell whose nearest corner is within `HEART_SAFE`
(1500 m) of the origin rolls the old table at the old chance. The gate is on the
CELL and not on the site's own centre, because `field.test.mjs`'s digest hashes
the site that NAMES each sample as well as the one that shapes it, and a sample
at (1000, 1000) sits in cell (2, 2), whose site may stand 1900 m out. All 44
heart cells were compared against a second, inlined implementation of the
pre-A3 roll and are identical; `field.test.mjs`'s digest is
`6408cb4e...`, unchanged.

**The castle holds a town.** A cell that draws the castle slot looks at its eight
neighbours for a rolled or authored town, then walks toward it to
`CASTLE_STAND_D` (300 m), clamped inside its own cell's middle 60%. Measured
over 6400 cells: 157 cells drew the slot, 35 kept it, 122 rolled again on the
wild table with the castle struck out and became something else, none of them a
castle. Every castle in the world carries `site.town`, `site.townAt` and the
name `<Town> Keep`, and stands within 400 m.

The one thing this cannot do is ask the terrain, because `sitegrid.js` has never
been allowed to know where the ground is. If `field.siteInCell` later refuses
that town, the keep still stands with the town's name on it, which reads as a
keep that outlived its town.

## 3. Fire

`fire.js` is the only fire in the world outside a spell.

- A flame is two `THREE.Sprite` quads on a four frame `DataTexture` sheet built
  in code, so it builds in node with no canvas. `update(dt)` walks the frames at
  `FLAME_FPS` (11) and flickers the scale; measured, a second and a third of
  flame walks all four frames and the texture offset walks with it.
- The coals are one merged mesh with an emissive material. `setNight(k)` takes 0
  in full day and 1 at midnight and opens it from `DAY_GLOW` (0.42) to full:
  measured 0.57 by day against 1.35 at midnight. **A fire is never out by day,
  because a cold camp is an empty camp.**
- Every ember of every fire in a structure is ONE `THREE.Points`.
- **One `PointLight` per structure, never more**, standing at the biggest fire of
  it. A temple with four braziers has one light. Driven both ways, including
  `castLight: false` for no light at all.
- A lit window is `createGlows`, not a fire: no flame, no ember, no light of its
  own, but the same `setNight`. Every pane of one colour is one draw call.

All of it is driven by `createSiteMarkers.animate(dt, nightFactor)`, the call
that already turns a mine's wheel. The three argument
`update(x, z, radius)` still leaves everything standing still, measured.

## 4. The tomb's door, and why the dungeon roll is untouched

A tomb is a rolled kind called `tomb`: it takes a 21 m pad, `placeFor` can give
it a habitat, and the map draws a tomb. What the player clicks is a DERIVED site
of kind `dungeon`, built by `structures.tombDoor(site)`, exactly as a mine's cut
is a derived site of kind `cave`:

```js
{ id: `${site.id}#door`, tomb: site.id, kind: 'dungeon', authored: true,
  cx: site.cx + TOMB_DOOR_CELL, cz: site.cz, x, z, y, flatR: 6,
  facing: site.facing + Math.PI, name: site.name, article: 'a door into a tomb' }
```

`interact.decide` reads `pick.site.kind === 'dungeon'` off `userData.site`, and
both the click path and E go through that one call, so nothing in `interact.js`
or `world_runtime.js` needed a line. The door slab and its ring carry the door;
every other mesh of the marker carries the tomb.

`dungeon` is still a row of `KINDS` with the weight it has always had. The door
owns no cell, is never returned by `cellRoll` or `sitesNear`, and takes a
generator cell 200,003 away from every real one so its level is not some other
dungeon's level. All of that is measured, both directions.

### 5.5 `src/game/world_runtime.js`, the smoke

One argument. `createSiteMarkers` already takes an options object and passes it
to every build:

```js
  const siteMarkers = createSiteMarkers(scene, discovery, terrainY, { effects });
```

Without it every fire is exactly what it is: flame, coals, embers, one light.
With it a burning wreck also puffs smoke into the shared particle pool every
`SMOKE_EVERY` (0.55 s), which is where smoke belongs because it has to drift out
of the marker's own space and be capped with everything else. Measured both
ways: two seconds of a burning wreck with the pool is 3 puffs and 33 particles,
without it 0 puffs and the same 8 draw calls.

If `world_runtime.js` does not have an `effects` in scope at that line, the
handover has to come from `main.js` instead; the option is read once at build
time, so it must be passed when the markers are created, not later.

## 6. Known gaps, honestly

**The map draws the new kinds grey and labels them with their raw id.**
`win_map.js` has `SITE_COLOUR[s.kind] || '#e0e0e0'` and
`KIND_WORD[s.kind] || s.kind`, so a bandit camp appears on the map as a grey dot
labelled `bandit_camp`. `win_map.js` belongs to E2 and `zones.js` to V1, so A3
could not add the rows. `auditMapWords()` stays green (it only walks
`ARTICLE`'s keys, and the new kinds are not in it) and `win_map.test.mjs` passes,
but it passes by luck: the three sites its loop finds first happen to be two
shrines and a town, and a wild kind among them would fail the row
`Object.values(KIND_WORD).includes(r.kindWord)`. The rows wanted:

```js
// src/game/win_map.js, SITE_COLOUR
  tower: '#b0a0d8', temple: '#e8dcc0', castle: '#d8b0a0', bandit_camp: '#c86050',
  graveyard: '#9098a8', tomb: '#8a8078', arena: '#d0a870', fountain: '#8fd0e0',
  gate: '#c0b8a0', burned_farm: '#8a6050', watchtower: '#b8a878',
// src/game/win_map.js, KIND_WORD
  tower: 'tower', temple: 'temple', castle: 'castle', bandit_camp: 'bandit camp',
  graveyard: 'graveyard', tomb: 'tomb', arena: 'arena', fountain: 'fountain',
  gate: 'gate', burned_farm: 'burned farm', watchtower: 'watchtower',
```

and, because `auditMapWords` requires every `KIND_WORD` key to be in `ARTICLE`,
the same eleven kinds need rows in `zones.js`'s `ARTICLE` (or `EXTRA_ARTICLE`).
The articles are already written, one per row, in `sitegrid.WILD_KINDS[i][2]`.

The toast a player gets on walking up to one is fine already:
`systems/world.js` says `you found <b>${s.name}</b>, ${s.article}` and reads the
article off the site, which `cellRoll` sets from the table.

**A gate does not know where the road is.** `buildSiteMarker` is handed
`heightAt` and nothing else, so a gate stands on its own rolled `facing` rather
than across the road it should straddle. Aligning it needs `field` (for
`sampleAt(x, z).road`) handed to `buildSiteMarker`, or A2's `wayside.js` to
place them. Left as it is rather than faked.

**A fire is silent.** `audio.js` has no crackle in its table and `effects.js`
does not get to add a cue that has no file behind it, so `effects.fire()` plays
nothing. One recorded loop and one row in the audio table would fix it.

**The cell margin, for the record.** `sitegrid.test.mjs`'s "every candidate stays
inside its cell margin" was red at 16 of 3957 when this task started, from V1's
authored `megastructure` and `landmark` rows, and V1 has since fixed it. A3
briefly put thirteen castles into that count and fixed its own: the castle's
walk toward its town now clamps half a metre inside the border, because clamping
to the border itself lands on 0.8000000000000001 of the cell in floating point.
It reads 0 of 5935 now.
