# V7: one forest. Arbor grows it, flora.js streams it

The user asked for the Arbor scripts to generate the game's forest: "various
types in various locations, varying wind". Two generators existed. V6 ported
Arbor (`src/world/arbor.js`) and nothing imported it. V2 had written the
infrastructure that is proven against the game (`src/world/flora.js`: one
`TreeField` per kind, instanced rebuilds, LOD bands at 55 m and 140 m, records
the axe can chop, stumps, regrowth, streaming). This merges them into one
forest: Arbor grows the trees and picks where they stand, flora streams them
and keeps every rule the game already had.

Files owned and changed: `src/world/flora.js` (rewritten), `src/world/arbor.js`,
`src/world/arbor_textures.js`, `src/world/tree_gen.js`, `src/world/flora.test.mjs`
(rewritten), `src/world/arbor.test.mjs`, and this document. Nothing else was
touched. `npm test` is green.

Every number below came out of `node src/world/flora.test.mjs` (117 checks) or
`node src/world/arbor.test.mjs` (153 checks). Nothing here is an estimate.

---

## 1. What a player walks into

`flora.js` no longer has a `DENSITY` table of its own for trees. A biome names a
forest type in `arbor.FOREST_TYPES`, and the forest type carries the mix and the
density. Two translations sit on top of Arbor's tables, both in `flora.js` and
both audited:

- **`SPLIT`.** Arbor's `spruce` becomes two game kinds, `fir` and `spruce`, half
  the weight each. The game has always called its conifer a fir, and
  `interact.js` NOUNS knows both words; growing the one recipe twice under two
  names at different seeds also puts two conifer silhouettes in a boreal forest
  instead of one.
- **`EXTRA`.** A kind Arbor cannot grow, spliced in with its weight taken off
  the top so the mix still sums to 1. There is exactly one: `cactus`, at 0.34 of
  the desert.

The result, printed by the test:

| biome | density | dry mix | wet mix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| meadow | Natural | oak .45, beech .35, birch .20 | same |
| boreal | Dense | fir .30, spruce .30, pine .25, birch .15 | same |
| sakura | Natural | sakura .50, birch .40, oak .10 | willow .50, sakura .30, birch .20 |
| desert | Open | dead .49, palm .16, cactus .34 | palm .56, dead .10, cactus .34 |
| mountain | Open | pine .70, oak .30 (thins from 46 m to 78 m) | same |
| snow | Open | fir .50, spruce .50 | same |
| beach | Open | palm .85, dead .15 | same |
| ocean | | nothing | nothing |

`wetMix` is Arbor's own mechanism and is chosen per tree, not per chunk, off
`isWet(sample)`. `sakura` gained a `wetMix` in arbor.js so a bank in a cherry
wood grows willow; the desert already had one. Willow and palm are also still in
`WATER_SPECIES` as a belt and braces guard, and the test drives it both ways:
over a 40 by 40 chunk sweep, 226 willows and palms stand on wet ground and zero
on dry, and a dry desert grows 281 other things and not one palm.

## 3. Prototypes: five per species, one per settled frame

Each kind gets `VARIANTS = 5` Arbor prototypes, at seeds derived from the world
seed (`hash2(i, kind.length * 37 + i, seed + 700)`), through
`arbor.prototypeFor` so the cache is shared.

Maturity is **stratified** rather than drawn at random: `forestPrototypes` draws
each tree's maturity uniformly, which over five draws comes out narrow often
enough to give a stand of one age (pine came out 13.3 to 14.8 m). Arbor's own
0.55 to 1.25 range is now split into five strata with a jitter inside each, so a
stand always has a sapling and an old tree in it and no two stands are the same.

### Off the streaming path

A field is created with ONE prototype in it and `update()` grows one more
whenever the world around the player has settled (nothing dirty, no grass tiles
waiting), in `WARM_ORDER`, until every kind has five. Boulders and cactus grow
the same way: `tree_gen.growTreeVariant` was pulled out of `buildTreeVariants`
so a tree_gen stand can also be grown one tree at a time, and `boulderField`
grows one boulder per frame too.

Measured: the meadow world warms from cold in **110 settled frames**, and the
worst single frame is **83 ms**. That frame is always the FIRST field of a
tree_gen backed kind, because `materialsFor('cactus')` (95 ms), `rockMaterial`
(78 ms) and `stumpMaterial` (70 ms) rasterise 512 px textures in plain JS the
first time they are asked for. That cost predates this merge and is why the warm
order exists. Growing one Arbor prototype is **2 to 9 ms**.

Until a stand is full, a record draws `vi % grown` rather than `vi`, so a tree
that appears before its own prototype exists borrows one and changes to its own
once, quietly, inside the first second or two. `variantOf` is always asked for
`VARIANTS`, never for the count grown so far, so a record's index is never
permanently repaired against a half built stand. Both directions are tested.

**One thing that is not measured here.** In node there is no canvas, so Arbor's
bark and leaf sheets come back blank. `arbor_textures.js` now probes the host's
2D canvas once for every method it uses and falls back to the stub with a
console warning if anything is missing, instead of throwing halfway through
`normalFromHeight` (which is what a partial `document` shim in
`world_runtime.test.mjs` used to do). So **the first-prototype cost of an Arbor
species in a real browser is higher than the 2 to 9 ms above**, by however long
one 256 x 512 bark pair and one 128 x 128 leaf sheet take to draw and read back.
The sheets are cached per (style, colour) and per leaf kind, so it is paid once
per species, on a settled frame, and never again.

## 4. LOD: the reference's leaf LOD inside flora's bands

flora keeps its bands, at `tree_gen.LOD_RANGE` = **55 m and 140 m**, re-read
every `REBAND_M` = 24 m of walking. What each band draws is Arbor's, and
`arbor.LOD_BANDS` is the table:

| band | leaves | bark | shadow | raycast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| near, 0 to 55 m | every quad | every branch order | casts, with a cut out depth material | pickable |
| mid, 55 to 140 m | one quad in three at 1.8x | order 1 and below | no | no |
| far, 140 m out | 24 quads at 4x | the bole alone (order 0) | no | no |

The mid band is `docs/reference/arbor-forest-optimized.html`'s own
`leafGeo(3, 1.8)`, unchanged. The far band is a fixed BUDGET of quads rather than
a fraction, because a fraction of a spruce and a fraction of a birch are very
different numbers and the far band is where the tree count lives: 24 quads is 48
triangles per tree whatever grew there.

Two new functions in arbor.js do it, and neither regrows the tree:

- **`leafLod(geo, stride, mul, cap)`.** A leaf is four consecutive vertices, and
  `pushLeaf` writes its corners so that the midpoint of the first two IS the
  point the leaf was hung on. Scaling the quad about that midpoint therefore
  grows the leaf without moving it off its twig. Measured over all 800 leaves of
  a mid band oak: the worst anchor moves **2.7e-7 m**, and the edge length is
  **1.80000x**.
- **`barkLod(proto, maxOrder)`.** `connect()` now records the branch order of
  every bark triangle in `c.Ord`, and `barkLod` filters the index by it. It is an
  **index only** geometry: it shares the prototype's position, normal and uv
  attributes, so the twigs cost nothing extra in vertex memory and nothing extra
  to upload. Measured on one oak: 7,804 / 244 / 84 triangles, and the far bark's
  reach in xz is 1.39 m against the full bark's 5.72 m, so it really is the bole.

Prototypes also share geometry with distance, on `tree_gen.LOD_SHARE` = [1, 2, 3]:
five silhouettes near, three at mid, two far. That is what bounds the draw calls.

### The near band, per 64 m chunk, at each biome's own Arbor density

The budget the merge was given: a Natural chunk under 250,000 triangles, a Dense
chunk under 500,000. Measured over 24 real chunks of each biome, with the real
prototypes and the real placement:

```
biome     density  trees/chunk   mean       worst           near band disc
boreal    Dense    55.6          427,307    508,730 (64)      991,415
meadow    Natural  24.0          207,195    357,460 (45)      480,722
sakura    Natural  14.7          142,129    239,408 (23)      329,760
beach     Open      9.5           69,010    223,630 (29)      160,114
mountain  Open      5.0           41,151    191,718 (23)       95,475
desert    Open      5.0           19,942    125,434 (14)       46,268
snow      Open      1.1           10,092     36,902  (4)       23,415
```

Every biome is inside its budget on the mean, which is what the budget is written
in. The **worst** column is a chunk that happened to draw twice its type's own
density (the fbm thinning varies), and it is reported rather than asserted: no
64 m chunk is ever wholly inside the near band anyway, because the near band is a
55 m circle, **2.32 chunks of area**. The last column is that circle's real cost,
which is the number to watch if the frame time ever goes. Boreal is just under a
million triangles for every leaf within 55 m of the player.

`DETAIL` per kind is the lever that got it there, and it is not taste. Oak is the
most expensive tree Arbor grows (22,620 triangles at detail 1, against beech's
8,432) and it is 45% of the meadow, so it sets the meadow's number: at detail 0.7
a meadow chunk is 264,000 triangles and at 0.6 it is 207,000. birch takes 0.5
rather than 0.7 because it is in both the meadow mix and the boreal one and has
to take the lower of the two. The test fails if any of this drifts.

### Draw calls and the whole frame

```
25 chunks of meadow (a 5 x 5 ring)   105 draw calls, 1.17 M triangles
25 chunks of boreal (a 5 x 5 ring)   110 draw calls, 2.14 M triangles, 1,153 trees
169 chunks of boreal (13 x 13, the whole tree ring at TREE_TIER)
                                     113 draw calls, 2.75 M triangles, 5,400 trees
                                     near 254, mid 1,212, far 9,331 instances
                                     (bark and leaves counted apart)
                                     near 1,017,756 tris, mid 1,056,842, far 540,596

```

Draw calls are bounded by the bake, not by the tree count: five prototypes over
three bands is at most 20 layers per species, and the whole world's trees fit in
113 calls. Five prototypes of a species now share ONE bark material and ONE leaf
material (`buildPrototype` makes a set per tree, and five identical
`MeshStandardMaterial`s over the same cached bark sheet are five sets of uniforms
for no difference on screen); the test asserts at most two materials per kind.

The worst re-band frame, rebuilding one field at a time on the 200 ms clock, is
**1.47 ms**.

## 6. Placement

`arbor.placeTrees` gives the layout: its jittered grid, its four rolls per cell,
its fbm thinning, its spacings (21 / 13 / 8 for Open / Natural / Dense) and its
thresholds (0.44 / 0.34 / 0.24). flora's rules go on top through `keep`, which
placeTrees calls only after it has drawn every roll for that cell, so a road or a
lake is a hole in the forest rather than a different forest. Verified: a `keep`
that rejects half a chunk returns byte for byte the filter of the unclipped list.

`recordsFor` runs **one placeTrees pass per biome the chunk actually holds**
(`biomesIn`, a 9 by 9 probe), and each pass keeps only its own ground, so a
border chunk grows both biomes at each biome's own spacing. A chunk with one
biome in it costs one pass.

What flora still enforces, all inside `keep`, all tested over 1,600 chunks:

```
none in water or river                        0 of 22,022 records
none on a slope over 2.0 m per 2 m            0
none inside a site clearing                   0
nothing within 125 m of the farm              0
nothing where road > 0.15 (the verge keeps its trees)
```

And after the layout, per tree: the per species tree line and slope limit
(`LIMITS`, the numbers tree_gen already had, plus beech and spruce), and
`WATER_SPECIES`. Measured tallest of each kind against its own line:

```
palm 19/24  willow 5/40  cactus 29/40  sakura 39/50  oak 61/66  beech 45/66
birch 46/66  fir 86/95  spruce 90/95  pine 67/95  dead 39/110
```

with 11 conifers standing above the oak line, so the rule is per species and not
one global tree line, both directions.

**Boulders and ore are not Arbor's.** Arbor grows no rocks, so `rock` keeps
flora's own 8 m cell roll and its old per biome numbers, and the ore rings around
cave mouths are untouched. That is what keeps the pickaxe working in every biome,
and `auditBiomeHarvest` throws if a biome loses either tool.

A tree can land up to `spacing * 0.45` (5.8 m at Natural) outside the chunk that
generated it. It is assigned to the chunk that generated it, never by position,
exactly as before.

### The record contract, unchanged

```js
{ x, z, gy, s, sy, ry, vi, alt, oa, chunk }
```

`of(t)` returns `{ x, y: t.gy, z, s, sy, ry }`, the prototype's foot is at the
origin and it is unscaled, so both layers of a tree take the same transform and
there is no per part y offset. `chopTree`, `shudder`, `treeCopy`, `pickTree`,
`regrowTrees`, `dumpTrees` and the tree editor are untouched and are exercised in
node against a real field: three swings fell an oak, the instance count drops by
two, `felledUntil` is set, a fourth swing does nothing, a stump stands within a
centimetre of where the tree was and at the width of the trunk that stood there
(0.87 m against 0.87), `regrowTrees` puts the tree back and the stump goes, and a
broken boulder leaves no stump.

`sy` (Arbor's per tree y stretch) is the one field added. `tree_edit.dumpTrees`
does not write it, so a hand placed tree saved and reloaded comes back with
`sy` undefined, which `of` reads as 1. That is a cosmetic loss on editor saved
trees only; streamed trees never go through `dumpTrees`.

`variants[i].baseR` still means the trunk radius at the base. For an Arbor stand
it is `proto.radius`, measured off the parameters that grew the trunk.

## 7. Wind

`flora.update` ticks **both** clocks, in seconds: `tree_gen.tickWind` (the grass
and the cactus) and `arbor.tick` (every leaf on every tree). Verified: after
`flora.update(35750, ...)`, `arbor.windUniforms.uTime.value` is 35.75.

On the flora object, for the settings window and for gameplay:

```js
flora.setWind(v)             // 0 dead calm, 0.5 the studio default, 1.5 a gale.
                             // Sets BOTH generators, so the grass leans with the
                             // forest.
flora.wind                   // the current base
flora.setWindVariation(on)   // off falls back to one flat wind everywhere
flora.windAt(x, z, t)        // { strength, angle, dirX, dirZ, gust }, the same
                             // expression the vertex shader evaluates. `t`
                             // defaults to the clock update() last ticked.
```

Measured at base 0.5: (0, 0) reads 0.591, (2000, 2000) reads 0.497, and (6, 0)
reads 0.594. So the far side of a valley sways differently from the near side and
a single tree does not shear. `setWind(0)` stills the field exactly, everywhere,
and putting it back gives 1.418 at base 1.2, inside the `[0.35, 1.65] x base` the
construction guarantees.

**Unverified:** the GLSL. V6 said this and it is still true. Nothing has compiled
`windHook`. The first browser run should check the console for a shader error
before judging the sway.

## 8. Foraging

```js
flora.treesFor(cx, cz)   // -> [{ x, z, radius }]
```

`main.js` line 300 already prefers `flora.treesFor` and falls back to its own
loop; the preferred path is now live. Radius is the prototype's own measured base
radius times the record's scale. The test recomputes it from the prototype rather
than from the same expression, so it is a comparison and not a tautology: over a
meadow chunk, 17 trunks between 0.17 m and 1.24 m, **worst error 0.0000%** (the
tolerance asked for was 5%). A felled tree is out of the list and a regrown one is
back in, both tested.

## 9. tree_gen.js: what is live and what is dormant

A block at the top of `tree_gen.js` says this in the file itself. In short:

**Still live, every frame or every field:** `growBoulder`, `rockTextures`,
`rockMaterial`, `icosphere` (every boulder and ore seam in the world);
`stumpGeometry`, `stumpMaterial` (every cut stump); `windUniforms`, `tickWind`,
`setWindStrength` (the grass clock, which `grass.js` reads); `LODS`,
`LOD_RANGE`, `LOD_SHARE`, `lodForDistance`, `geometryVariant` (the 55 m and 140 m
bands, shared by both generators); and the whole growth model for **cactus only**,
because Arbor has no cactus recipe.

**Dormant, not deleted:** the recipes for `oak`, `birch`, `pine`, `fir`, `willow`,
`palm`, `sakura` and `dead` in `tree_gen.SPECIES`. Arbor grows all eight now.
Kept rather than cut because `tree_gen.test.mjs` measures every one of them and is
the regression baseline for the growth model itself; because the farm's themes may
still want a hand placed tree that is not a forest tree; and because nothing
builds a species unless something asks for it by name, so a dormant species costs
one table entry and no memory, no geometry and no texture. If they are ever cut,
their rows come out of `tree_gen.test.mjs` in the same commit.

One function was added: `growTreeVariant(species, seed, i)`, the i-th tree of a
stand, pulled out of `buildTreeVariants` so a tree_gen stand can be grown one
tree at a time. The seed and both option draws are exactly what
`buildTreeVariants` used, so the i-th tree is the same tree either way, and
`tree_gen.test.mjs` still prints the same triangle counts.

## 11. What is unverified, and what is a judgement call

- **Everything about how it looks.** There is no browser in this lane. Fable
  verifies. The geometry, the counts, the placement and the bands are measured;
  whether an Arbor spruce reads as a spruce on a Brackenwake hillside is not
  something a triangle count can say.
- **The GLSL**, as above and as V6 flagged.
- **Spruce's lowest whorl droops to -2.5 m** at the needle tips on some seeds.
  That is the reference's own behaviour (`gravity` is multiplied by 1.6 on a
  conical habit and the lowest whorl starts one segment up from a trunk that
  itself starts at `-0.4 * scale`). Records are planted at `s.h - 0.15`, so the
  bottom skirt buries. Left as it is, deliberately: it reads as a trunk clear of
  the ground, and on a slope it is invisible. Flagging it rather than silently
  changing a reference number.
- **Trees are bigger and there are more of them than before.** `placeTrees`
  scales each tree by 0.55 to 1.5, which is Arbor's own spread and not flora's
  old 0.86 to 1.16, and a boreal chunk went from about 35 trees to 56. That is
  the Arbor look, and it is the point of the change, but it is a big visual step
  and it is the first thing to look at on screen.
- **The far band is 24 leaf quads.** It is not a billboard impostor. Past 140 m a
  tree is a bole and a couple of dozen very large leaf cards. It measures well
  (in the worst case ring the far band holds 9,331 of the 10,797 instances and
  draws 540,596 of the 2.75 M triangles) but it is the band most likely to read
  wrong in motion, and the pop at 140 m is a pop, not a cross fade.
- **`userData.foliage` is set to null on Arbor leaves, deliberately.** Arbor
  carries leaf colour in vertex attributes, not in `material.color`, so a
  seasonal pass that tints the material would do nothing to them. Autumn belongs
  in `buildPrototype({ autumn })`, which recolours per leaf. Nothing in this fork
  reads `userData.foliage` for world flora today (only `farm/assets.js` writes it
  for the farm's own trees), so nothing regressed; a future season pass has to
  rebuild the stand rather than tint it.
