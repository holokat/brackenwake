# M1: the mines are drawn, and a cut is a thing you can click

Design notes this file serves: `docs/mmo/09-WORLD-ZONES.md` section 4, which
authored seven outdoor mines, and `docs/mmo/wiring/Z1.md` section 2.2, which
built them as data and then said, in as many words, that a player could not see
or enter one because `buildSiteMarker` had no `mine` branch.

The user's words: "I want to author outdoor mining caves with rarer ore", and,
for the whole polish wave, "get as close to high fidelity as possible".

Owned and changed: `src/world/mine_models.js` (new), `src/world/site_models.js`,
`src/world/mine_models.test.mjs` (new), `src/world/site_models.test.mjs` (new),
this document. Nothing else was touched: `sites.js`, `world_runtime.js`,
`dungeon.js` and `main.js` are all untouched, and `enterDungeon` needed no
change, exactly as Z1 promised.

---

## 1. What is now in the world

A mine is not one place, and that is the whole design. It is:

| part | how many | what it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the yard | 1 | headframe with a turning wheel, a shaft collar, crates, barrels, a water trough, a rack of picks, a barrow, a sawhorse, a stack of pit props, a rope coil, a spoil heap flecked with the mine's richest ore, and a board with the mine's name burnt into it |
| a cut | 2 to 4 | a timber portal round a black opening, iron straps and bolts, rock cut back around it, a few metres of rail track on sleepers, an ore cart tipped at the end of it with its load spilled, spare props leaning on the frame, and a lantern that comes up at dusk |
| a seam | 4 to 7 | a collar of country rock with a band of the ore's own colour outcropping through it and seven emissive glints on the band |

Seven mines, twenty one cuts, forty two seams. All measured, all built from the
real `zones.js` sites through `field.js`, in `mine_models.test.mjs`.

## 2. The one line that makes a cut enterable

`site_models.buildSiteMarker` gained a `mine` branch. It builds the yard, one
group per `mine.mouths[i]` and one per `mine.seams[j]`, and tags them:

```js
mouth meshes   userData.site = mouth      // kind 'cave': enterDungeon takes it
yard meshes    userData.site = mine       // kind 'mine': nothing to go into
seam meshes    userData.site = mine       // so the hover names the mine
               userData.seam = seam       // and a caller can tell it from the yard
```

`world_runtime.pick` reads `userData.site` off the nearest hit and hands it on;
`interact.decide` already routes `kind === 'cave'` to `{ action: 'enter' }`; and
`world_runtime.enterDungeon` already accepts `kind === 'cave'`. So clicking a
cut goes underground with **no change to any file outside this one**, and
`dungeon_gen.js` reads the cut's own `oreBand`, which is the richer ladder the
mines exist for. Driven both ways in `site_models.test.mjs`: every one of the
twenty one cuts passes `enterDungeon`'s own predicate, and the mine and a seam
are both refused by it.

## 3. Per frame: the wheel and the lanterns

Two things move, and neither is baked into a merged mesh:

- the headframe wheel over the yard turns at `WHEEL_RPS`, 0.06 turns a second,
  one turn in seventeen seconds
- a lantern hung on one post of every cut lights at dusk, flickers, and goes
  out at dawn

**The hook, and where to call it.** `createSiteMarkers` now exposes
`animate(dt, nightFactor)` and also accepts the same two as optional fourth and
fifth arguments of `update`. `nightFactor` is **0 in full day and 1 at
midnight**, which is the other way round from the `dayFactor` that
`world_runtime.update` is handed.

`world_runtime.js` already has both numbers in `updateWorld`, so the whole
change is one argument list:

```js
// src/game/world_runtime.js, in updateWorld
siteMarkers.update(x, z, world.viewRadius, dt, 1 - dayFactor);
```

or, if the owner would rather keep the streaming call as it is:

```js
siteMarkers.update(x, z, world.viewRadius);
siteMarkers.animate(dt, 1 - dayFactor);
```

**Until that lands, everything still works and nothing moves**: `dt` defaults to
0 and `nightFactor` to 0, so the three argument call `world_runtime.js` makes
today builds the mine, tags it, and leaves the wheel still and the lanterns
unlit. That is measured, both ways, in `site_models.test.mjs`: the wheel is
turned three seconds, the old three argument call is made, and the wheel has not
moved; then `animate(1, 1)` moves it and lights eight lantern pieces over four
cuts; then `animate(1, 0)` puts them out again.

There was no existing per-frame path for site markers to copy. Camps' fires do
not flicker: `buildCamp('campfire')` is merged into the marker like everything
else and `createSiteMarkers` had no update at all. This is the first one.

**No lights are added.** A `PointLight` per lantern would be twenty one lights
in a scene that runs four, so the lantern is emissive glass and an emissive
flame and nothing else. If the renderer ever gains a light budget for props,
this is where one would go.

## 4. The one thing left to wire, and it is not mine

`main.js`'s `doInteract` fallback finds the nearest site with
`runtime.sitesNear(p.x, p.z, 60)`. **That call can never return a cut**, because
a cut owns no site cell: it is a record hanging off the mine. Measured in
`site_models.test.mjs`: standing at a cut, `sitesNear` returns nothing at all
and `sites.mouthsNear` returns two cuts within ten metres.

This does not break anything today, because `main.js` prefers
`interact.enter()` and `interact.js` works off the hover pick, which is a mesh,
which now carries the mouth. The fallback branch only runs for a build of
`interact.js` that predates `enter()`. But if anyone ever wants a proximity
based "press E at the mouth" that does not need the cursor on the timber, the
lookup is `sites.mouthsNear(field, x, z, r)`, which Z1 exported for exactly
this, and it belongs in `main.js` or `interact.js`, neither of which is mine.

## 5. Where the terrain fought back, and what was done about it

Everything below was measured against the real field over all seven mines
before a single triangle was placed.

**The yard is flat and the hillside is not.** The yard is level to 0.00 m out to
10 m from its centre and +/- 0.21 m at 12 m. A cut is a different world: the
ground falls 3.7 m over the six metres in front of it (9.5 m at the Ember Cut,
which is a 60 degree slope), rises 0.73 m over the two metres behind it, and
cross-slopes by up to 2 m over the width of the portal.

So:

- **every post grows down to its own footing.** A post cut to one length would
  have floated at one side of the portal and been buried at the other.
- **the track follows the ground.** Each of the five sleepers asks `heightAt`
  where it stands and each rail spans the pitch between two of them. Laid level
  it would have ended 5 m in the air. Measured: on the steepest cut the track
  drops 5.12 m over five sleepers, and every sleeper is on the ground under that
  sleeper to within a nanometre.
- **the cart is seated by measurement, not arithmetic.** It lies on its side, so
  where its bottom is depends on its own rotation; `seatOnGround` drops it until
  its lowest vertex touches.

**Depth is painted, not dug.** `field.js` is Z1's and it flattens the yard and
nothing else, so nothing here may carve a recess into the hill. A mouth's
darkness is four layers over about a metre: a rock overhang at z +0.4, the
timber frame at +0.10, jamb walls from 0 back to -0.34, and a black to deep grey
gradient plane at -0.34. A deeper recess would look better and cannot be had:
the hillside rises behind the mouth and would show inside the opening as a lit
wedge. `MOUTH_SILL`, a 0.45 m stone kerb across the foot of the opening, is what
hides the worst of that intrusion, and it is a real thing at a real adit.

**If a deeper cut is ever wanted**, the change belongs in `field.js`: give a
mine mouth its own small levelled pad the way a cave gets its mound. That is
Z1's file, and this file would not need changing at all: it already reads the
mouth's ground through `heightAt`.

**A seam has a boulder in the middle of it.** `flora.js` already puts exactly
one minable ore boulder at each seam's centre, at `SIZE.ore` = 1.00 to 1.70 on a
unit icosphere. So the outcrop built here leaves a 1.00 m core clear, which is
the smallest boulder flora can put there, and a bigger boulder leans into the
collar, which is what an outcrop is meant to look like. Measured: the closest
anything comes to a seam's axis is 1.05 m over all forty two seams.

**And two seams can be 0.60 m apart** (the Low Shoulder). That is why the
outcrop is a low collar rather than a ring wall: two that overlap read as one
bigger outcrop instead of as two rocks fighting.

**Nothing in a yard reaches past 6.25 m** from its centre, against
`YARD_PROP_R` of 6.8 m, against a nearest-seam distance of 7.09 m over all seven
mines. A prop that reached further could bury the boulder a player came to
swing at. The audit throws if a future prop creeps out there.

## 6. The bug this file shipped once

`mergeParts` returns a group of merged meshes and the caller re-parents them:

```js
for (const child of mergeParts(S).children) g.add(child);   // WRONG
```

`Object3D.add` unparents, which splices the array being walked, so **half the
merged meshes were silently left behind**. A mouth came out at 642 triangles
instead of 2,046 with its iron, its jamb and its ore heap simply missing, and
nothing anywhere threw. The fix is a copy of the list, which is what
`mergeByMaterial` had always done and what was not copied across. It is
regression tested in `mine_models.test.mjs` ("six materials merge into six
meshes and not three").

## 7. Merging, and why a mine does not use `mergeByMaterial`

`site_models.mergeByMaterial` buckets by colour hex across the whole group and
**deletes every attribute but position and normal**, because the kit's houses do
not agree about uv sets. Everything in a mine is textured, so that merge would
have handed back untextured grey; and bucketing across the whole group would
have fused the second cut's timber into the first cut's, leaving no way to say
which triangle belongs to which mouth.

So `mine_models.mergeParts` buckets by material **identity** (a small cached set:
timber, iron, rock, ore flecked rock, jamb, the gradient, trough water, plus a
band and a glint per ore and a board per mine, all shared across all seven) and keeps `uv`, and
`color` where the material asks for it. Each part is merged on its own, in its
own local frame, and tagged before it joins the marker.

The cost of merging per part rather than per mine is draw calls: a mine is 54 to
81 meshes where a town is 42. That is the price of being able to tell one cut
from another with a raycast, and it is paid deliberately.

## 8. Measured numbers

Everything here is printed by `npm test` and none of it is an estimate.

| | measured | budget |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a cut | 2,046 triangles, 7 draws | 3,000 |
| a yard | 3,898 triangles, 8 draws | 8,000 |
| a seam | 524 triangles, 3 draws | 600 |
| a whole mine | 13,180 triangles on average, 92,260 for all seven | |
| build time | 11.2 to 19.1 ms per mine | a town is 10.4 ms |
| yard reach | 6.25 m | 6.8 m, against a 7.09 m nearest seam |
| deepest foot on level ground | -0.384 m | -0.45 m |
| track drop, steepest cut | 5.12 m over 5 sleepers | |
| ground samples per cut | 26, every one the field's own answer there | |
| wheel | 0.0600 turns a second, one turn in 17 s | |
| lantern | 0 at noon, 2.20 on the glass and 3.52 on the flame at midnight | |

**The one performance note.** A mine is now the most expensive thing the world
builds: 19.1 ms for the Millrun Adit against 10.4 ms for a town. The streamer
builds one site per frame, so that is a single dropped frame, once, when a mine
comes into the 300 m ring, and it is not repeated. If it matters, the fix is to
let `createSiteMarkers` build a mine's ten part groups over ten frames instead
of one; the parts are already independent groups, so nothing else would have to
change.

## 9. Textures, and node

Every sheet is drawn in code on a 2D canvas: timber grain with plank seams and
knots, hammered pitted iron, granular bedded rock, the mouth's radial gradient,
and the sign board. Each carries a normal map derived from a parallel height
sheet through `arbor_textures.normalFromHeight`. They are cached, so all seven
mines share one set, except the sign, which is per mine by definition.

There is no canvas in node, so `mine_models.js` takes the same injectable
factory `arbor_textures.js` takes and falls back to a stub. The one thing the
arbor stub cannot do is text, and a sign board is text, so the stub here adds
`fillText` and **records what was drawn**. That is not a test seam: it is the
only way any headless caller can find out what a board says, and it is how
`mine_models.test.mjs` proves all seven boards drew their own mine's name, in
the HUD's own display face (`ui_theme.theme.fonts.display`, Cinzel).

## 10. Where the ore colours come from

`ores.js` gives each vein a colour as a **word** ("blue black", "grey with a
white flash"). `log_piles.ORE_WORD` keeps a hex next to each of those words, and
this file reads the hex from there rather than typing a third copy. That is also
what makes a seam's band the same colour as the lumps that break off it, since
`buildOreHeap` reads the same table.

`mine_models.test.mjs` holds all ten hexes against the words in `ores.js` and
goes red naming both if anybody ever edits one without the other.

## 11. What is unverified

- **Nothing here has been seen.** There is no browser in this session. Triangle
  counts, positions, ground contact, tagging, colours, the sign's text and both
  animations are measured in node against the real field; how the whole thing
  LOOKS is not, and screenshots are the next step.
- **The lantern's brightness is a guess.** `LANTERN_GLOW` of 2.2 was chosen
  against the scene's ACES tone mapping without seeing it.
- **The gradient plane is a compromise** and may still read as a sticker from a
  low angle. If it does, the answer is a mouth pad in `field.js` (section 5),
  not more geometry here.
- **Draw calls per mine are high** (54 to 81). No frame time has been measured
  with a mine on screen.
