# M4: the painted map

Written 2026-09-06. Files owned and touched: `src/game/map_paint.js` (new),
`src/game/map_paint.test.mjs` (new), `scripts/paint-map.mjs` (new),
`docs/maps/` (new, ten files), this note. Nothing else in the repo was edited.

## What it is

The user put four World of Warcraft zone maps on the table: parchment, an
irregular land bounded by hatched ridges and coast, stippled canopy, pale water,
dashed roads, every place marked and named in a display face, a torn ornate
border, a banner with the zone's name. Ours was a heat map of biome colours with
circles on it.

`map_paint.js` is a painter that turns the game's own world data into that kind
of chart. It is not an illustration of Kaldera; it is Kaldera, read off the same
functions the player walks on:

| what is on the map | where it comes from |
| --- | --- |
| the ground, its colour, its height, its slope | `field.sampleAt` on a grid |
| the coast and the two echo lines outside it | marching squares over the water mask that grid holds |
| the rivers | the zero set of the field's own river noise, and then `field.sampleAt().river` at every point of it, which is what decides whether a metre of it is drawn |
| the mountains and the hachures | the height grid: local maxima that stand out of the ground around them, and the gradient where it is steep |
| the forest stipple | biome and moisture, with the density words `arbor.FOREST_TYPES` uses |
| the roads | `roads.roadsForCell`, the same polylines the ground is graded to |
| the realms, their colour, their rim and their name | `zones.zoneAt` through `field.sampleAt().realm`, so a realm is the shape it actually holds on the ground and not the circle in the table |
| the places | `zones.authoredSites()` and `field.siteInCell` |
| what is hidden | `opts.known`, the game's own discovery model |

Move a realm and the map moves with it. That is the point of painting it out of
the data rather than drawing it by hand: it is the guide the user paints over,
and a guide that lies is worse than none.

## The surface

```js
import { paintMap, paintGround, paintMarks, paintFrame, makeCache } from './map_paint.js';

paintMap(ctx2d, {
  field,                       // createWorldField(WORLD_SEED)
  x: 0, z: 0, w: 16000, h: 16000,   // the world rectangle, in metres
  px: 2048, py: 2048,               // the drawing, in pixels
  known: { zones: true, places: aSet },
});

paintMap(ctx2d, { field, realm: 'boneyard', px: 1300, known });   // one realm, close up
```

`realm` sets the rectangle and the banner from the realm's own disc. `known` is
`{ zones, places }`, each a Set, an array, or `true`. The three layers are
separately callable, and that matters: `paintGround` is the whole cost and does
not change when a player finds a hut, `paintMarks` is the places, `paintFrame`
is the border and the banner. `opts.cache` from `makeCache()` keeps the sampled
grid, the traced rivers and the roads between calls.

Options a caller is likely to want: `samples` (grid points along the long side),
`drawScale` (draw cells per sample cell, worked out from the pixel size if not
given), `rolled: false` (authored places only, no rolled huts), `names: false`
(the caller writes its own realm names), `rims: false`, `roads: false`,
`rivers: false`, `frame: false`, `banner`, `rose`, `scale`, `title`, `subtitle`.

Nothing is asked of the context but paths, rects, text, dashes and a transform.
No gradient, no clip, no filter, no pattern, no image. The test's recorder
throws if the painter ever reaches for one, because node has no canvas and an
SVG writer has no filter, and a picture that is only right in a browser is a
picture nobody can check.

## How it is smooth without a blur

Every field the ground is made of (the biome and realm tint, the water, the
realm border wash, the blanking over undiscovered country) is sampled on a
coarse grid, box blurred in JavaScript, then read back bilinearly at up to three
times the resolution and mixed into ONE colour per draw cell, emitted as one
`fillRect`. Runs of the same colour along a row are merged, so an ocean is a
handful of rectangles. Soft edges come out of the blur, not out of a canvas
filter, which is why it draws the same on every context. The lines a chart is
made of come from marching squares over the same grids.

## What was measured

`node src/game/map_paint.test.mjs`, all through a recording 2D context that logs
every call with the colour and dash set at the time. **36 checks, 36 pass.**
Every gate is driven both ways.

- **Realm names.** All nine realms walked: each name drawn exactly once, 9 of 9.
  None walked: 0 names, and 0 place names, and 11,659 hatch strokes over the
  blank country instead.
- **One place.** Hearthhome found: named once, 12 draw calls, 21 path points
  within 40 px of its own pixel. Not found: **0 draw calls and 0 words.**
- **The sea.** Ten probes on the recorded ground fills, five on points the field
  calls open water and five on land, each clear of the shore by two sample
  strides: 5 of 5 water painted as water, 5 of 5 land not.
- **Roads.** In a view over a cell that owns roads: 2 roads owned by
  `roadsForCell`, 2 drawn, 2 dashed strokes, shortest 12 points. With
  `roads: false`: 0.
- **Hachures.** On the steepest ground in the world (196, 88), where it falls
  2.99 m per metre: 4,081 of 10,000 lattice points hachured. On the farm's own
  flat disc: **0 of 10,000**, and 0 mountains.
- **Rivers.** Every one of 4,338 drawn points is a river in the field
  (`river >= 0.10`, not open water); 0 are not. The fast path the paint uses
  refuses 18,408 contour points cheaply against the ground grid first and is
  still 0 wrong. Coverage: **206 of 262** cells the field gives a strong river
  (`river > 0.6`) are within 160 m of a drawn line, **79%**.
- **Determinism.** Two runs of the same options: 171,387 context calls,
  identical, in the same order.
- **The cache.** Cold paint 266 ms of which 181 ms is the field; then one place
  is discovered and the repaint is `cached: true`, **0.00 ms of field work**,
  and the marks cost **0.1 ms**.
- **The audit.** `auditMapPaint()` passes; delete one kind's glyph and it
  throws; put it back and it passes.

Timing, world map, 2048 px, 208 samples, no cache, on this machine:

| context | cold | warm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the recording context (logs every call) | 469 ms | 505 ms |
| a null context (the painter's own arithmetic only) | 453 ms | 320 ms |

Of the warm 320 ms, about 210 ms is `field.sampleAt` and the grids derived from
it and about 110 ms is issuing 342,273 draw calls.

At the size the game draws at, 640 px over the whole world with 128 samples:
**cold 168 ms, cached repaint 18 ms, the marks 0.8 ms.**

**What is NOT measured: a real canvas.** The `canvas` npm package is not
installed and is not a dependency of this game, so the brief's "under 400 ms at
2048 px with a real canvas" is unverified. What is verified is the painter's own
work and the number that budget exists for: a repaint after a discovery costs
0.1 ms with the cache, because the ground is not resampled.

## Three things the map found out about the world

These are not bugs in the painter. They are what the ground actually says, and
somebody should decide whether they are wanted.

1. **Kaldera is half drowned.** Inside `COAST_MIN` (6.5 km), **43.2%** of the
   continent is open water, and over the whole 16 km square it is 60.6%. The
   Greenwold's own close up is a green archipelago in a shallow sea rather than
   the farmland the sheet describes. Measured on a 200 by 200 grid of
   `field.sampleAt`.
2. **`sample.water` is not "the sea".** It is `h < SEA_LEVEL`, and `field.js`
   carves every river bed to -1.8, which is under it. The first draft of this
   painter read `s.water` and so painted every river as a piece of sea AND threw
   away every river it traced, on the grounds that the point was in the sea. The
   field settles it one line later: water with a river in it is a river, water
   without one is `biome === 'ocean'`. Anything else that reads `s.water` to
   mean the sea has the same bug. The map now reads the biome.
3. **The Saltmarch and the Ashen Throne had no name on the world map**, because
   both realms' centres of mass sit near the rim of the sheet and a name that
   overhung the border was being dropped rather than pulled inside it. Found by
   the test that counts nine names, not by looking.

## Wiring Fable has to do

`win_map.js` is another builder's file and I have not touched it. Two lines put
the painting under the map, and one option keeps the two from writing the same
names twice.

**1. The import**, beside the others at the top of `src/game/win_map.js`:

```js
import { paintGround, makeCache } from './map_paint.js';
```

**2. The underlay.** In `drawMap`, replace these six lines, which are the whole
of "1. the ground, one filled cell per sample" and the flat fill above it:

```js
  g2d.save();
  g2d.fillStyle = '#0d1014';
  g2d.fillRect(0, 0, size, size);

  // 1. the ground, one filled cell per sample
  for (let j = 0; j < n; j++) {
    for (let i = 0; i < n; i++) {
      const s = field.sampleAt(x0 + (i + 0.5) * stride, z0 + (j + 0.5) * stride);
      const [r, g, b] = shadeFor(s);
      g2d.fillStyle = `rgb(${r},${g},${b})`;
      g2d.fillRect(Math.floor(i * cell), Math.floor(j * cell), Math.ceil(cell), Math.ceil(cell));
    }
  }
```

with this:

```js
  g2d.save();

  // 1. the ground, painted. map_paint.js reads the same field on the same grid
  // and lays it down as a chart: parchment, coast, rivers, relief, forest,
  // roads, and a soft border round each realm. It reads the same discovery this
  // panel does, so unwalked country comes back as blank hatched paper.
  // docs/mmo/wiring/M4.md. `names: false` because the zone loop below already
  // writes every name this panel is willing to say, and each of those is a
  // click target. The cache is the panel's own, so the two second redraw and
  // every redraw after a discovery costs the marks and nothing else.
  const ground = paintGround(g2d, {
    field, x: cx, z: cz, w: span, h: span, px: size, py: size,
    known: { zones: opts.zonesFound, places: opts.discovered },
    cache: opts.paintCache, samples: n, names: false, rolled: false,
  });
```

**3. The panel's cache**, so the ground survives between redraws. In the panel's
`mount` (beside `this._canvas = document.createElement('canvas')`):

```js
    this._paintCache = makeCache();
```

and in `render()`, in the object handed to `drawMap` (beside `size:
this._canvas.width`):

```js
      paintCache: this._paintCache,
```

**4. Two numbers to look at afterwards.** The painted ground costs more than the
128 by 128 fill did: **168 ms cold at 640 px with 128 samples, 18 ms on a cached
redraw**, measured against a null context. `MAP_BUDGET_MS` is 130, so
`win_map.test.mjs` will go red on the cold draw. Either raise it to 260 and say
in the comment that it is a first draw and that a redraw is 18 ms, or have the
test measure the second draw, which is what a player actually waits for.

**5. Layer 3 is now saying the same thing twice.** The zone tint by danger band
and the unwalked hatch are both painted by the underlay in a warmer language. My
suggestion, which is a judgement and not a requirement: keep the zone RIM stroke
and the name (they are the click target and the danger reading) and drop the
`fillRect` of `DANGER_TINT` and the `hatch()` call, or take both down to about a
third of their alpha. Nothing breaks if it is left alone; the map just carries
two washes.

**Nothing else is needed.** `paintMarks` and `paintFrame` are deliberately NOT
in the wiring above: this panel draws its own sites, its events, its waypoint
and its player arrow, and it should go on doing so.
