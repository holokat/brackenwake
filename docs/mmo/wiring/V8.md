# V8: grass that is already there, and gold that is coins

The user said two things. "Grass is weird and dark and shows up as I run, not
ideal", and "let's create 3D models for gold piles, small, medium and large".

Files owned and changed: `src/world/grass.js` (rewritten), `src/game/gold_piles.js`
(new), `src/game/loot_drops.js`, `src/world/grass.test.mjs` (rewritten),
`src/game/gold_piles.test.mjs` (new), `src/game/loot_drops.test.mjs` (added to),
and this document. Nothing else was touched. `flora.js`, `scene.js` and
`main.js` were read and not edited.

Every number below came out of `node src/world/grass.test.mjs` (89 checks),
`node src/game/gold_piles.test.mjs` (57 checks) or `node src/game/loot_drops.test.mjs`
(118 checks), or out of a throwaway harness that is named where it is used.
Where a number is a wall clock reading it says so, and it says what the machine
was doing at the time, because the machine this was measured on was at a load
average of 25 to 29 throughout and a single tile fill was seen at 0.4 ms and at
100 ms in two runs of the same script.

---

## 1. What was actually wrong

The brief asked for the current behaviour to be measured before anything was
changed, and the measurement contradicted the obvious reading of the complaint.

Driving the OLD `grass.js` (RADIUS 56, TILE_BUDGET 4, TILE_MS 0.8) at 20 m/s for
600 frames at 60 fps, standing in open meadow:

| what was measured | old code |
| --- | --- |
| tiles inside 40 m that were unfilled at any point | **none, in any of 600 frames** |
| worst wait for a tile inside 40 m | **0 frames** |
| a tile 40 m dead ahead of a running player | **already filled** |
| frames to lay the ring from cold | 65 |
| tiles live, standing in meadow | 208 of 210 |

So the streaming was not the problem. **The fade was.** The distance fade is a
height scale, and it ran

```
uFadeStart = RADIUS * 0.62 = 34.7 m       full height inside here
uFadeEnd   = RADIUS        = 56 m         nothing out here
```

which means the turf grew out of the ground eleven paces in front of the
character, every step, for ever. That is the thing the player was watching.

A first measurement of mine said the disc was only 63.7% filled while running.
It was wrong, and it is worth writing down why: it counted tiles by looking for
blade instances, and 92 of the 208 tiles around that meadow were **ocean**,
which is a filled tile that correctly grew nothing. The tiler's own `active` map
says 100%. A test that infers state from a side effect will lie to you.

**Weird and dark** was the normals, and there were two of them:

- the clump geometry carried the card's normal, `(nx, 0.35, nz)` normalised,
  which points sideways. Under a sun near the zenith a sideways face receives
  almost nothing.
- the material is `DoubleSide`, and three flips the normal on the back face
  (`normal *= faceDirection` in `normal_fragment_begin`). So the far half of
  every crossed quad was lit from **below** and went black.

And the blade sprite itself starts dark: `l = 0.55 + 0.45 * t` put the root of
every blade at 0.55 luminance before a single light had touched it.

## 2. The grass now

### The ring, and the coarse band

`RADIUS` is 80 m and `NEAR` is 44 m. Inside `NEAR` a tile fills all 150 blade
slots; beyond it a tile fills `OUTER_FILL` (0.34) of them, 51, and each clump is
`OUTER_SCALE` (1.65) times bigger so the turf still reads as cover rather than
as scattered dots. Measured on the ground in meadow:

- **105.8 blades per 100 m2** inside 44 m, **32.3** outside it.
- the outer clumps average **1.15 m** tall against **0.71 m** in close.

Both pools live in one `InstancedMesh` per layer, near blocks first, so the
grass is still **two draw calls**, which `flora.test.mjs` also still checks.

The fade now runs `62.4 m` to `80 m`. A player running at 20 m/s has full height
grass for three seconds ahead of them instead of one and a half.

### The slots, counted

"Count the slots before adding to a container." The pools are not estimated from
the area of a disc, because how many tile **centres** land inside one depends on
where in its own tile the player is standing. `ringCounts()` brute forces the
peak over a grid of sub tile offsets, and the answer does not move between 12
and 97 samples per axis:

```
the ring wants at most 97 near tiles and 296 outer, 384 in all
38784 blade slots and 914 cover slots (145 near tiles of 150, 334 outer of 51)
```

38,784 blade slots against the old 31,500, for 2.04x the area. `auditGrass()`
runs at module load and throws if a pool is smaller than `ringCounts` says, if a
biome in `field.js` has no turf row, or if the fade starts past the ring edge.

Over **1.8 km of running at 20 m/s** across biomes the peaks were **97 near of
145** and **296 outer of 334**, and **zero starved fills**. At an impossible
240 m/s the ring falls behind (peak 88 near, 85 outer, so about half the disc)
but still never overflows a pool, and comes back whole the moment the sprint
stops. Both directions are in the test.

One thing here was a real bug and is worth the paragraph. A tile that crosses
the near/outer line is re-grown, and the first version kept its old slot until
the new one was written, so the boundary would not blink. That is right for a
tile coming **inward**, and wrong for one going **outward**: an outward tile is
the only thing that ever RELEASES a near slot, and if it waits its turn behind
the tiles coming the other way then at speed every near slot ends up held by a
tile that no longer wants one. Measured: **674 starved fills** over a fast
sweep. Outward rebands now go to the head of the queue and there is a `reclaim`
fallback behind that; measured after: **none**.

### The budget

```js
fillBudgetMs(urgent) = TILE_MS + (TILE_MS_MAX - TILE_MS) * clamp01(urgent / CATCHUP_QUEUE)
// 0.8 ms calm, 3.0 ms flat out, 24 near tiles waiting buys the whole of it
```

`urgent` is the count of **near** tiles waiting, not the whole queue. That
distinction was not cosmetic: with the whole queue driving it, a cold ring's 300
outer tiles held the budget at 3 ms for a second, and `flora.test.mjs`'s "no
single re-band frame costs more than 12 ms" started failing at 14.85 ms. The
outer band is 44 to 80 m away and grows in over 0.4 s; nobody has ever noticed
it arrive. What is worth catching up on is the ground the player is about to
stand on.

What the budget actually controls is how many tiles a frame **starts**, and that
is what the test asserts (`stats.lastFrameTiles <= TILE_BUDGET`, measured 15 to
16 of 16). The cost of the tile already running belongs to `field.sampleAt`,
which builds a road cell on its first touch; a wall clock ceiling is not this
file's alone to promise, and on a loaded box it is not measurable at all.

### The prefill

`update()` treats a move of more than one tile in one call as a teleport and
fills the inner `PREFILL_R` (40 m) synchronously before it returns. The first
`update()` of all does the same, so the frame a player spawns on has grass on
it. Measured: **80 of 80 tiles inside 40 m complete on the very first frame**,
in exactly one prefill.

`setCentre(x, z, nowMs, radius = PREFILL_R)` is the same thing on purpose, and
returns `{ tiles, ms }`.

Cost, warm, ten teleports into fresh meadow, on a machine at load 28:

```
16.3, 19.2, 19.5, 28.9, 38.1, 38.3, 69.9, 78.8, 113.3, 116.3 ms
median 38 to 52 ms across runs, best 16 ms
```

That is a real hitch, once, on a jump. It buys away a bare ring that the old
code took about a second to fill. `setCentre` takes a radius so a caller who
wants a cheaper jump can ask for less than 40 m.

### The lighting

- clump normals are `(0, 1, 0)`, the ground's. The field exposes `heightAt`,
  `sampleAt` and a `slopeHint` but **no normal**, so straight up is the whole of
  it; if `field` ever grows a `normalAt` this is the one place to read it.
- the fragment stage injects `normal = normalize( vNormal );` immediately after
  `#include <normal_fragment_begin>`, which undoes three's `faceDirection` flip.
  Without this the geometry normals alone fix nothing: the back face of every
  quad would still be lit from below.
- the blade sprite runs `BLADE_BASE_L` 0.78 at the root to 1.0 at the tip.
  Measured mean luminance of the lit pixels: **0.796 to 0.896** in sRGB bytes,
  which is **0.603 to 0.782** in linear light, a 1.30x lift. The darkest lit
  pixel in the sprite is 0.835, up from about 0.41.
- `receiveShadow` stays true and `castShadow` stays false. The stripe risk the
  brief names came FROM the card normals: `scene.js` offsets the shadow lookup
  along the object normal (`sun.shadow.normalBias = 0.03`, half a shadow texel),
  and a sideways normal offsets sideways, which is exactly how a receiver
  self-stripes. With the normal pointing up the offset pushes the lookup 3 cm
  above the turf, which is the case that bias was tuned for. Nothing in
  `scene.js` was changed.

**The stub is not the shader.** Every injection above is a string replace against
a chunk name, and a chunk name three no longer uses replaces nothing, silently,
and the grass would ship dark with a green test. The test now runs the real
`onBeforeCompile` over `THREE.ShaderLib.physical`'s own source and checks that
each injection landed, and that the un-flip lands **after** the flip rather than
before it.

### The grow in

Each instance carries an `aBorn` float, written when its tile is filled, in the
same seconds the wind clock uses (`tickWind` is fed `nowMs / 1000` by
`flora.update`, and `grass.update` divides the same `nowMs` by the same 1000).
The vertex stage raises the blade from a quarter height to full over
`FADE_IN_S` (0.4 s) and passes the ramp to the fragment stage, which dithers
against a screen space hash and discards. That is an alpha fade with no
transparency, no sorting and no pass of its own.

### Kept

`setDensity(0..1)` is unchanged, so `flora.setGrass` and the settings window's
Grass density slider still work: measured 10,091 blades at 1, 5,135 at 0.5, and
10,091 again on the way back. Every blade still sits on the field to the
millimetre (worst 0.18 mm), none in water, none on a road, and walking away and
back grows the same 10,091 blades in the same places.

## 3. Gold piles

`src/game/gold_piles.js`. `buildGoldPile(amount, { seed })` returns one `Mesh`:
coins as thin cylinders (`COIN_R` 4.5 cm, `COIN_H` 12 mm), merged into a single
geometry, so a pile is one draw call however many coins are in it.

| tier | gold | coins | segments | triangles | across | high |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| small | 1 to 30 | 6 to 12 | 12 | 288 to 576 | 35 cm | 1.9 cm |
| medium | 31 to 150 | 40 | 10 | 1,600 | 39 cm | 7.0 cm |
| large | over 150 | 58 | 8 | 1,856 | 84 cm | 14.7 cm |

A cylinder of n radial segments is exactly 4n triangles, so the counts above are
arithmetic and the test checks each pile against `coins * 4 * segments` as well
as against the 2,000 budget. `auditGoldPiles()` builds all three at module load
and throws on a pile over budget, a pile whose triangles are not its coins, a
material claiming vertex colours it does not carry, a coin under the ground, or
a tier boundary that has moved.

- **`tierFor(amount)`** is pure and the boundaries are driven both ways at load
  and in the test: 0 is nothing, 1 and 30 are small, 31 and 150 are medium, 151
  and a million are large, and 30.4 rounds down while 30.6 rounds up.
- the layout is a paraboloid, which is what a poured pile of discs settles into:
  a medium mound is 4.1 cm high in the middle and 1.3 cm at the rim. A large
  pile spills exactly 6 coins, all of them outside the heap radius and all of
  them lying flat, because a coin that rolled off a pile is a coin on the floor.
- gold PBR: `metalness 0.85`, `roughness 0.3`, `envMapIntensity 1.2`, base
  `#ffcb4d`. `main.js` already sets `sc.scene.environment` from a PMREM target
  at intensity 0.55, so the environment reflects them with no wiring at all.
- each coin gets its own shade through a `color` attribute (0.82 to 1.10 of the
  base), which is what makes a heap read as coins rather than as a gold blob.
  This is `grass.js`'s `vertexColors` trap from the other side, so the audit
  checks the pair rather than the flag.
- the layout is a pure function of the amount and a seed, so a pile is the same
  pile every frame, and two 46 gold bags in one fight are not twins.

## 4. What a drop looks like now

`shapeOf(items, gold)` is the one place that decides:

| what is in the bag | what is on the ground |
| --- | --- |
| gold only | the gold pile its amount earns, no sack, no ring, and it does not turn |
| gear only | the sack, lit by the rarity of the best thing in it, as before |
| gear and gold | the sack, with a small coin scatter 26 cm to one side |

The scatter beside a sack is deliberately the small tier whatever the purse
(tested at 3, 40, 400 and 90,000 gold): the sack is the subject and the coins
are the note that there was money in it too.

`take` and `pick` are unchanged paths. What is new is that `take` calls
`reshape`, because what is LEFT in a bag is not always the same shape as what
was in it. Tested both ways, on the real drop path:

- take the sword out of a sack of a sword and 200 gold, and what stays is a
  `pile:large`, same bag id, same position, rebuilt node, and a real raycast
  still finds it.
- take the money out of a sack of a helm and 40 gold, and the coins beside it go
  and the plain sack stays.
- take 380 of 400 gold from a heap and the 20 left is a `pile:small`.
- the ninety seconds still expire a pile, and it still fades over the last ten
  (measured opacity 0.50 at 85 s). The pile material is `transparent: true` at
  opacity 1 from the start so that fade never recompiles a shader on the frame
  the player is watching.

`labelFor(bag)` is exported for the cursor:

```
labelFor({ gold: 46 })                              "a pile of 46 gold"
labelFor({ items: [sword], gold: 12 })              "a sack: a purple longsword and 12 gold"
labelFor({ items: [sword] })                        "a sack: a purple longsword"
labelFor({ items: [sword, kite], gold: 5 })         "a sack: a purple longsword, a kite shield and 5 gold"
labelFor(null)                                      "nothing"
```

Items first and money last, which is the order a person reads a sack in: what is
it, and how much was with it. `listText` still puts the gold first and is
unchanged, because that is a sentence about a transaction and this is a label on
a thing.

## 5. What main.js should do, and what it does not have to

### The teleport prefill: already covered, and the explicit call

**Nothing has to change for a teleport to prefill.** `grass.update` detects a
move of more than one tile and prefills before it returns, so every existing
jump already lands on grass:

- `main.js` line 453, `runtime.onDungeonState` -> `player.teleport(st.at.x, st.at.z, ...)`
- `main.js` line 563, `wake()` -> `player.teleport(x, z, ...)`
- the dev bench's Teleport, which goes through the same `player.teleport`

all move the player kilometres, and the next `runtime.update` -> `flora.update`
-> `grass.update` sees the jump. That path is what the test drives: a 24.3 km
jump prefilled 80 of 80 tiles inside 40 m in the same call.

The explicit call is worth adding anyway, because it runs BEFORE the frame
rather than during it, next to the `camera.snap` that is there for the same
reason. It needs one line in `world_runtime.js` beside the existing `setGrass`:

```js
// src/game/world_runtime.js, next to setGrass at line 284
/** A teleport or a dungeon exit: fill the grass ring before the frame is drawn. */
grassCentre(x, z, nowMs) { return flora?.grass?.setCentre?.(x, z, nowMs) ?? { tiles: 0, ms: 0 }; },
```

and then in `main.js`, immediately after each `camera.snap?.(player.pos)`:

```js
runtime.grassCentre?.(player.pos.x, player.pos.z, performance.now());
```

`flora.grass` is already exposed on the flora object, so nothing in `flora.js`
needs to change either. Both files belong to other agents; neither was touched.

### The cursor label

`labelFor` is exported from `loot_drops.js` and from the runtime object
(`loot.labelFor`). Nothing reads it yet. `main.js` picks a bag on click at line
649 (`const bag = loot.pick(ray)`) but has no hover pass for bags at all, so
wherever the hover label for trees and monsters is decided, a bag under the
cursor should read `loot.labelFor(bag)`. That is a `main.js` and `interact.js`
change and it has not been made.

## 6. Unverified, and things V8 knows about and did not do

- **No browser.** Nothing in this document was seen on a screen. The lighting
  claims are claims about normals, shader source and texture bytes, all of which
  are measured; whether the grass now READS as lit grass in the game is Fable's
  call, and the two knobs to turn are `BLADE_BASE_L` and the `TURF` colour
  table, which was deliberately left alone so the change to brightness has one
  cause rather than two.
- **The dither is a guess about taste, not about correctness.** A screen space
  hash discard will shimmer for the 0.4 s a tile is growing in. It costs nothing
  and it sorts nothing, but if it reads as noise rather than as grass arriving,
  drop the discard and keep the height ramp; both are in `grassMaterial` and the
  height ramp alone is what the old code did for distance.
- **Wall clock numbers are soft.** The machine was at load average 25 to 29 for
  the whole session, and a single tile fill was measured at 0.4 ms and at 100 ms
  in two runs of the same script. Every millisecond in this document is a
  ceiling under that load, not a typical, and the per-frame assertions in the
  test count tiles rather than milliseconds for exactly that reason.
- **The prefill hitch is real.** 16 ms at best and 40 to 50 ms typical under
  load, once, on a jump. If it is felt in the browser, `setCentre` already takes
  a radius; 28 m halves the tile count.
- **The outer band re-bands.** Walking 200 m at 20 m/s re-grew 485 tiles that
  crossed the 44 m line. That is bounded work with a measured cost and it is the
  price of a coarse outer band. If the band boundary is ever visible while
  turning, `NEAR` and `OUTER_SCALE` are the two knobs.
- **`interact.js` NOUNS does not know about gold.** A pile is `loot_drops`'
  object and interact never sees it, so nothing broke, but if a hover pass is
  added it should go through `labelFor` and not through NOUNS.
- **The gold pile has no LOD.** 1,856 triangles at 90 seconds a bag with a
  handful of bags on screen is nothing, but a boss fight that leaves thirty
  bags on the floor is 55,000 triangles and nobody has measured that in a
  browser.
- **`npm test` was green for every suite this document owns** (grass 89, gold
  piles 57, loot drops 118, flora 116). The suites that failed in the full run
  under load were `win_map`, `chunks`, `roads`, `water`, `flora` (all wall clock
  budgets, and `chunks`, `roads` and `water` pass on their own when run alone)
  and `dungeon` (two flame checks, in a file another agent had open at the
  time). None of them imports `grass.js`, `gold_piles.js` or `loot_drops.js`.
