# P1: the plan loader, and the nine places drawn off the paintings

Written after the work, 2026-09-06. Every number quoted here is printed by
`node src/mmo/plans/plans.test.mjs` and `node src/world/plan_models.test.mjs` on
world seed 20260904.

The user decided that a named place is authored from a concept image and not
generated. Fable read the Greenwold's nine paintings into
`docs/concepts/greenwold/PLANS.md` and the model list into `MODELS.md`. This
task is the loader: the nine paintings as data, an audit that will not let a
broken one load, and a builder that stands each of them on the real ground with
a code built stand-in for every piece the user has not modelled yet.

Nothing here is wired into the running game. The lines that would do it are at
the end of this file and belong to Fable.

## The five decisions worth arguing with

**The tables are pure and the bodies are not, and that split is deliberate.**
`FOOTPRINT` is declared in `src/mmo/plans/footprints.js` and re-exported by
`plan_models.js`, so `plan_models.FOOTPRINT` is that one object and there is no
second copy to drift. It is split because four files have to read a plan
(`site_models.js`, `dressing.js`, `npcs_runtime.js`, `monsters.js`) and two of
those are pure today. `src/mmo/plans/index.js` loads without THREE; measured.

**A plan is merged in TAG groups, not in one pass and not per piece.**
`mergeByMaterial` bakes every mesh of one colour into one geometry and throws
the rest of the userData away, so merging a village in one pass would leave
nothing to click and merging every piece on its own would cost a draw per
building per colour. So a piece whose model is in `SOLO` gets its own group and
its own name, and everything else shares one. Measured on Hearthhome: 59 draws
with the three cottages named, 48 with them sharing. The three cottages are
therefore NOT named through the merge, and every building somebody KEEPS still
is. A piece built from a glb always gets its own group, because it has its own
materials and could never have shared a bucket anyway.

**The stand-ins are built out of the town's own palette and not out of
`dressing_models`'s bodies.** The dressing kit has a hedgerow, a cart, a
milestone and a hay rick already, and borrowing them looked free. It is not:
those bodies carry their colours in a vertex attribute over four shared
materials, and `mergeByMaterial` deletes every attribute but position and
normal. A borrowed hedgerow would have come out of the merge the flat colour of
its base material. So the bodies here are thirty six parametric builders on
plain materials taken from `town_layout.PALETTES`, which means a plan and a town
in the same realm are built out of the same limewash and the same thatch.

**The Standing Hedge is one stone and a `repeat`, and it is the only one.**
`PLANS.md` says the ring is a mile across with nine sarsens and that the
painting is of one of them. So `waystones.json` carries the composition of one
stop plus `repeat: { kind: 'ring', count: 9, radius: 805, face: 'in' }`, and
`buildPlan` lays it nine times with each copy turned so its carved face looks
back at the ring's middle. Measured: the built ring is 1,616 m corner to corner
and costs exactly nine times what one stone costs. This is an extension to the
plan format the brief gave and it is here because the alternative was a plan
with a radius of 811 m or nine near identical files.

**Two footprints may overlap only when one of them says so.** A wheel is on a
mill, a lantern hangs in an arch, a lookout platform is in an oak, a mine mouth
is under a headframe, a badger sett is in a beech's roots. Each of those carries
`on: '<the model it stands on>'`, and the audit does not merely excuse the pair,
it DEMANDS that a piece which claims it really is standing on one. Five in the
nine, and the test drives the claim both ways.

## Where the nine differ from PLANS.md, and why

Thirteen places. Every one is a decision, not a slip.

1. **Hearthhome's bank stands at 18, 4 and not 16, 4.** The stall ring is at
   radius 12 and the stall on bearing 80 reached 13.4 m east, which is 1.4 m
   inside a bank whose west wall the sheet puts at x 12. Two metres of bank
   moved rather than six metres of market.
2. **Hearthhome's eight trees are grown, not placed.** `PLANS.md` says "eight
   oaks and beeches inside the wall between the houses" and gives no
   coordinates. They are found by a search that clears every building, every
   stall, every other tree by 3 m and every doorstep by 1.2 m, which is the bug
   T2 found in the Last Well's inn door and is not going to happen again.
3. **The stable's pen and hay rick have coordinates the sheet does not give.**
   The pen at 36, -18 and the rick at 43, -12, both clear of the stable, the
   healer and the wall.
4. **The Beech Hangar's largest beech moved from -18, -16 to -21, -19**, because
   the fallen trunk the sheet lays at -14, -8 is 14 m long and its lower end
   stood inside it.
5. **The Beech Hangar's plan radius is 66 m and not "a 120 m circle".** The
   hollow way's own ends, which the sheet puts at -50, -40 and 50, 40, stand
   64.0 m out. A 60 m radius would have cut both ends off the path.
6. **The stand is forty beeches**, as the sheet says: four named large ones and
   thirty six grown against the same rectangle test the audit runs.
7. **An arc and an oval are laid as chords, because a run is a line.** The
   village wall is 36 chords of an oval of 46 by 40 with the gate standing in
   one of them; the hollow's chalk overhang is 20 chords of the arc from bearing
   200 through north to 160; the chalk pits' face is one straight run.
8. **Udd and Hald are not in the plan by name.** `PLANS.md` names them at the
   stable and the bank; they are entries in `npcs_runtime.GIVEN_NAMES` and not
   part of S2's cast, so the plan carries `{ name: null, role: 'stablemaster' }`
   and `{ name: null, role: 'banker' }` and the game names them the way it names
   every other villager. Copying the two names into a data file would have made
   a second source for something `nameFor` already decides.
9. **The chalk pits' foreman is a Provisioner.** The sheet says "the foreman at
   the hut door" and there is no foreman, miner or overseer in `npcs.js`. A
   Provisioner stands there and buys ore, which is the nearest true thing. The
   line that would give him his own role is below.
10. **The Legion camp holds three and not four.** The sheet's four soldiers are
    the Tithe Wagon's escort, which is E2's event and E2's to walk. The camp
    keeps a watch of two soldiers and an archer at the standard, the spear rack
    and a brazier.
11. **The banner outside the Old Cellars does not lean.** The sheet has it
    "planted crooked, 15 degrees"; a plan has a yaw and no lean, so it is turned
    and stands upright, and the lean belongs in `legion_banner.glb`.
12. **The Sunken Chapel's footprint is 7 by 12 and its yaw is 270.** The sheet
    says "yaw 90 (west door at -6, 0)" and those two disagree: at yaw 90 the
    door faces east. Turned to 270 with the 12 m running east to west, the west
    door lands at exactly -6, 0, which is the number the sheet actually cares
    about. `chapel_sunken`'s belfry is built over that door rather than at the
    side, which is what the painting has.
13. **The Standing Hedge is one stone and a `repeat`,** which is the fifth
    decision above.

## Two of the nine are not sites at all

`zones.SITE_KIND` has no row for the sheet kinds `wild` and `road`, so
`buildZones` skips them and **the Beech Hangar and the Kingsroad are subzones
with no authored site**. They have a plan, a centre and a name, and nothing will
ever build them, because `site_models.buildSiteMarker` is only ever handed a row
out of `authoredSites()`.

Two lines in `zones.js` (V1's file) fix it:

```js
const SITE_KIND = {
  hub: 'town', town: 'town', hamlet: 'hamlet', dungeon: 'dungeon',
  mine: 'mine', cave: 'cave', ruin: 'ruin', shrine: 'shrine', camp: 'camp',
  megastructure: 'megastructure', landmark: 'landmark',
  wild: 'landmark', road: 'landmark',
};
```

and, beside them in `SITE_FLAT_R`, the two pads the table above asks for:

```js
  beechhangar: 0,        // a hollow way is a cut, not a table
  kingsroad: 0,          // a road follows the ground it is on
```

That adds 20 sites across the nine realms (every `wild` and `road` place in the
sheet), all of them pad-less, all of them landmarks, so `buildMegalith` answers
null for the ones it does not know and the old marker stands in. It also needs
`zones.test.mjs` and `win_map.js`'s word audit looked at, which is why it is
here and not done. Until it lands, seven of the nine plans reach the world and
two do not. `plan_models.test.mjs` builds all nine either way, synthesising the
two rows itself, and says so where it does it.

## Wiring Fable has to do

Five places. Quoted exactly.

**1. The hook, in `src/world/site_models.js`, in `buildSiteMarker`.** Before the
town hook, so that Hearthhome takes the painting and not `layoutTown`'s packer:

```js
import { PLANS } from '../mmo/plans/index.js';
import { buildPlan } from './plan_models.js';
```

and, immediately after the `site.kind === 'mine'` line:

```js
  // P1: a place somebody painted is built from the painting and not rolled.
  if (PLANS[site.sub]) { const g = buildPlan(PLANS[site.sub], site, heightAt); if (g) return g; }
```

Hearthhome's plan therefore REPLACES T1's town and T2's keep for that one town.
The manor carries `userData.keep` and the standing stone `userData.waystone`, so
both of those wirings keep working; the other six towns are untouched. The
Standing Hedge's plan likewise replaces V1's `buildMegalith` for `waystones`,
because the hook stands above it, and builds the same nine stones with the
boundary stones, the offerings, the worn path and the hedgerow the painting has.

**2. The dressing switches off inside a planned place, in
`src/world/dressing.js`, in `inPad`.** Today the gate reads `site.flatR`, which

export function inPad(site, x, z) {

with `import { peopleFor } from '../mmo/plans/index.js';` and

**4. The plan's monsters, in `src/game/monsters.js`.** The plan puts Old Grist
at 30, -10 in the Beech Hangar and the Miller's Son's six in the hollow, at
points, not on a chunk roll. `spawnsForChunk` returns early on the group chance
before it has an `out` to push into, so this wants its own function beside it
rather than a line inside it:

```js
import { spawnsFor } from '../mmo/plans/index.js';

/** The monsters a planned place stands at its own points, in this chunk. */
export function plannedSpawnsForChunk(field, cx, cz, sites, night) {
  const out = [];
  const x0 = cx * CHUNK, z0 = cz * CHUNK;
  for (const s of sites) {
    if (!s.sub) continue;
    spawnsFor(s.sub, night).forEach((sp, i) => {
      const x = s.x + sp.x, z = s.z + sp.z;
      if (x < x0 || x >= x0 + CHUNK || z < z0 || z >= z0 + CHUNK) return;
      out.push({ id: sp.id, cx, cz, i, groupKey: `${s.id}:plan`, night,
        key: `${s.id}:plan:${i}`, x, z, y: field.sampleAt(x, z).h });
    });
  }
  return out;
}
```

called beside `spawnsForChunk` in `rescan`. Twenty two spawns over the nine,
fifteen of them night rows. `HABITAT_BY_PLACE` keeps rolling the open country of
each place on top, which is right: the plan says who is definitely there and the
habitat says who else might be.

**5. The models, when the user has made them.** `buildPlan` is synchronous
because `buildSiteMarker` is, so a glb is loaded ahead of the build and taken out
of a cache. One line in `src/game/world_runtime.js`, beside the model preload:

```js
import { loadPropsFor } from '../world/plan_models.js';
import { PLANS } from '../mmo/plans/index.js';

  for (const plan of Object.values(PLANS)) loadPropsFor(plan);
```

It answers a count and never throws: a model that is not on disk leaves its
stand-in standing. Files go in `public/models/props/<id>.glb`, named by the id in
`MODELS.md`, metres, Y up, forward +Z, origin at the centre of the footprint with
the base on y = 0, which is `tools/validate-glb.mjs`'s contract and the space
`FOOTPRINT` is written in. A markers already built will not change until it
leaves range and comes back; that is `createSiteMarkers`' own streaming and needs
nothing.

**6. Optional: a role for the pits foreman.** In `src/mmo/npcs.js`, if the chalk
pits should have their own person rather than a Provisioner:

```js
  miner: R({ id: 'miner', name: 'Miner', sells: ['ore'], buys: ['ore'], teaches: ['mining'], needs: 'mine' }),
```

with a `ROLE_TINT` row, and then `greenwoldpits.json`'s one person becomes
`{ name: null, role: 'miner' }`. `win_talk.auditTalk` refuses a role whose panel
is nothing but a Talk tab, which is why it buys something.

## What is NOT verified

- **Nothing has been seen.** Every claim above is a node measurement on the
  height field. Nine places have been built, counted and measured and none of
  them has been looked at. Thirty six stand-in bodies want an eye at the dev
  bench's tour stops, and the tour needs the hook in section 1 before any of
  them is on screen.
- **Two of the nine cannot reach the world at all** until `zones.js` gains
  `wild` and `road` in `SITE_KIND`. The Beech Hangar and the Kingsroad build
  correctly in the test, which synthesises their site rows.
- **The pads in the table above are recommendations and not measurements.** What
  is measured is the fall of the ground under each plan TODAY. What a `flatR` of
  40 would do to the Mill Run has not been run, because `field.js` is V2's and
  mid flight.
- **The arrival points are data and nothing reads them.** Every plan carries
  `arrival: { x, z, yaw }`, checked to be outside every footprint and inside the
  radius, ready for `win_dev.tourStops()` and for whatever puts a player down at
  a waystone. No line is wired.
- **A stand-in is a size, not a building.** It is the right footprint, the right
  height, the right way round and the realm's own colours, and that is all it is
  meant to be. `MODELS.md`'s triangle budgets and texture sizes are the user's
  to hit; `FOOTPRINT` is what a finished model has to match, and nothing checks
  a delivered glb against it yet. That check belongs in
  `tools/validate-glb.mjs`.
- **`npm test` was not run.** V2 is mid edit on `field.js`, `zones.js`,
