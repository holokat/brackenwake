# E1: the emotes

Eight things a player does on purpose, a wheel on **X** to pick one, and the
five things that take one off you again. There are no other players in
Brackenwake yet; this is the half that has to exist before there is anything
worth waving at.

```
src/game/player.js               EMOTE_POSES: eight bodies, in the gait's own language
src/game/emotes.js               the table and the rules. No THREE, no document, no clock
src/game/win_emotes.js           the wheel, key X
src/game/app/systems/emotes.js   the wiring, one frame at a time
```

## The eight

| id | key line | how long | ends with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `wave` | you wave | 2 s | you put your hand down |
| `sit` | you sit down on the grass | held | you get up |
| `bow` | you bow | 1.5 s | you straighten up |
| `cheer` | you throw your arms up and cheer | 1.5 s | you stop cheering |
| `point` | you point straight ahead | 2 s | you lower your arm |
| `dance` | you dance | held | you stop dancing |
| `laugh` | you laugh | 1.5 s | you get your breath back |
| `lie` | you lie back and look at the sky | held | you get up |

`sit` and `lie` carry `locks: true`, which is the row saying the body is off its
feet: a movement key ends them and the words are "you get up". `auditEmotes`
refuses a locking emote whose ending says anything else, so the two cannot drift.

Five run for a fixed time and go back to the idle **saying nothing**, because a
wave that stops waving has already announced itself. Every other ending has a
sentence, in the emote's own noun:

```
move    you get up / you stop dancing / you put your hand down
swing   you swing, and that is the end of your dance
cast    you break off your rest to cast
hit     the wolf ends your dance          (or "something", see below)
down    you go down, and your sit with you
```

## Where it sits in the frame

```
world  player  EMOTES  combat  abilities  inventory  world_life  dragon  ui  dev  input
```

Straight after `player`, because the player system has just walked the body and
written the gait pose, and this writes over it. Before `abilities`, because
`effects.update` runs in `abilities.late` and ADDS a swing or a flinch to what
the pose left; that is the order effects.js documents and the order it needs.

Build order is `world player combat inventory abilities ui emotes world_life
dragon dev input`: `deps` is `['player', 'combat', 'ui']`, because the wheel is
registered with the window layer at boot and the panel context is filled in
here, the way `dragon.js` does it. `wiring.test.mjs` holds both strings.

## The walk always wins

Two gates, checked every frame, in this order:

1. `emoteStep(state, now, walking())`. `walking()` is true when ground speed is
   over `STILL_SPEED` (0.2 m/s), when he is airborne, or when any of
   `w a s d space` is down. The key check matters: a player leaning on W against
   a wall has no speed at all, and would otherwise sit there for ever.
2. Even past that, the pose is only written when `rig.state.anim === 'idle'`.
   Nothing here reaches into player.js's decision about what he is doing; it
   only declines to draw over it.

Starting one while moving is refused out loud ("you cannot sit on the move"),
rather than starting an emote nobody can see.

## The channels, and why there is a clear

`poseCharacter` rewrites hips.position.y, the six leg joints, both arms' x and
z, the torso and head pitch and the breath scale **absolutely, every frame**, so
anything an emote writes there is gone the moment the emote is.

The emote poses have to reach further. A cross legged sit turns the knees out
(`legL/R.rotation.z`, `shinL/R.rotation.z`) and lying on your back tips the whole
pelvis over (`hips.rotation.x`). **Nothing else in the game zeroes those**, so
`clearEmotePose(parts)` does, exactly once, on the frame the emote stops. It is
the discipline `effects.js`'s `resetOwned` keeps and it is here for the same
reason: a channel nobody resets accumulates, and a body that sat down once would
never stand up.

`player.test.mjs` proves this is needed rather than assuming it: it poses a lie,
then poses a run, and measures that the pelvis is **still** tipped 1.57 rad;
then clears and measures all eighteen channels back at zero.

## What ends one, and how each is detected

| ending | how it is known |
| --- | --- |
| moving | `walking()`, above |
| swinging | `actor.lastSwingAt` moved. That is `combat.queueSwing`'s own mark, so a click that swings sets it in the `click` phase, before this system runs |
| casting | `abilities.casting` or `abilities.pending`. Reached lazily with `ctx.has('abilities')`, so the system boots without one |
| being hit | `actor.health` lower than it was last frame |
| going down | `player.dying` |

**Why the hit is watched and not hooked.** `combat.onHit` fires for hit EFFECTS
(a burning blade, thorns, a reflect) and NOT for a plain bite. A system that
only listened to it would sit through a wolf eating it. The general case is the
one `combat.js`'s own system already uses on itself: health lower than last
frame. The hook is registered as well, because when it does fire it carries the
attacker by name and it fires in the same frame.

**Naming what hit you.** Nothing on the resolver records who last struck an
actor, so `whoHit()` answers only when the answer is not in doubt: the one live
monster within `HIT_REACH` (3.5 m). Two of them on you and the honest line is
"something ends your dance". Both directions are measured: one wolf standing
over you is named, two wolves are not, and a wolf 40 m away is not.

Combat runs after this system, so a plain blow ends the emote on the **next**
frame, 16 ms later. An effect hit ends it in the same frame, through the hook.

## The wheel

`win_emotes.js`, key **X**, id `emotes`, a standalone window that centres itself
in `open()` rather than taking the cascade. Eight round segments on a 104 px
circle inside a 292 px box, clockwise from the top in the table's own order,
each with a stroked stick figure and the word under it. A click starts the emote
and closes the wheel; Escape closes it; opening any codex page closes it,
because it is a window like every other one.

**X was free.** `RESERVED_KEYS` keeps `w a s d q e space shift control tab`. The
registered panels take B, C, K, M, N, P, V, F2 and Escape; the ability bar takes
1 to 0, minus and equals; the item bar takes F5 to F12. `win_emotes.test.mjs`
does not take that on trust: it registers the ten real panels of the game in the
real window manager and reads back `windows.keyOf('emotes')`, which is `'x'`,
and checks no panel lost its key to another.

**The pick says nothing itself.** `ctx.emotes.start(id)` writes the line, so
there is exactly one place in the game that says "you sit down on the grass"
whichever door the emote came through.

## The slash commands, which nothing reads yet

There is no chat input in Brackenwake. `EMOTE_COMMANDS` maps `/wave` to `wave`
and so on for all eight, plus `/sitdown`, `/liedown`, `/laydown` and `/dancing`,
and `commandFor('/Wave at the guard')` already drops the case and the trailing
words. **Nothing in the game calls it.** It is exported so that whoever builds
the chat line has one call to make rather than a second list of names to keep in
step, and `auditEmotes` fails if an emote ever has no `/id` command.

## The harness

```js
window.__bw.emotes.start('dance')     // starts it, and says the line, the real way
window.__bw.emotes.stop()             // takes it off, and says why
window.__bw.emotes.current            // 'dance', or null
window.__bw.emotes.list               // the eight ids, in the wheel's order
window.__bw.emotes.commandFor('/sit') // 'sit'
```

`start` here is the same function the wheel calls. There is no preview path and
no second code path: the test path is the real path.

## The poses, measured

Every pose is written in the language the gait is: absolute rotations on the
rig's own parts, and no time of its own beyond `s.emoteT`. `plantLegs` solves
both feet onto the ground with the same `legIK` the walk uses, so a pose that
drops the hips bends the knees rather than pushing the boots through the field,
and a pose that raises them above standing height straightens the legs and takes
the feet off the ground, which is what a hop is.

The cross legged sit is not eyeballed: the thigh angles are solved so the knee
lands at (0.30, 0.24, 0.33) in the body's frame and the shin so the ankle lands
back across the midline at (-0.06, 0.10, 0.30). Read off the rig:

```
sit    hips 0.361 m   knee x 0.299, z 0.334   right ankle x -0.054, y 0.104   head 0.967 m
lie    hips 0.158 m   pelvis -1.5708 rad      head y 0.121, z -0.609          1.847 m end to end
```

Three seconds of each pose, sampled at 20 Hz, with the whole body's bounding box
measured every sample:

| pose | lowest point | tallest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| wave | 0 mm | 1.985 m |
| sit | -39 mm | 1.775 m |
| bow | 0 mm | 1.784 m |
| cheer | -4 mm | 2.118 m |
| point | 0 mm | 1.784 m |
| dance | -4 mm | 1.776 m |
| laugh | 0 mm | 1.784 m |
| lie | -17 mm | 1.775 m |

Nothing goes more than four centimetres into the ground, and six of the eight
stay within four millimetres of it. The sit's 39 mm is the breath on the hips at
the bottom of its cycle.

`auditEmotePoses(ids)` fails loudly in both directions: an emote with no pose
and a pose answering to no emote. The system calls it, and `auditEmotes`, before
a single frame runs, so a ninth emote with no body stops the boot rather than
shipping as a menu entry that does nothing.

## Verified

Measured, not asserted. `npm test`: all suites green. `npx vite build`: clean.

- `src/game/emotes.test.mjs`, 87 checks. Every emote started; every timed one
  driven frame by frame to its end (a wave ends on frame **120** at 2000.0 ms
  and is still a wave at 1999 ms; a bow on frame **90**); every held one run
  **1800 frames**, thirty seconds, and still going, then ended by one frame of
  walking. The audit driven false nine different ways.
- `src/game/win_emotes.test.mjs`, 32 checks. The real panel built against a fake
  document and **all eight buttons clicked** through the real listener: eight
  starts, in order, and eight closes. The key proved by registering the ten real
  panels of the game. X opens the wheel, X again closes it, Escape closes it, C
  closes it.
- `src/game/app/systems/emotes.test.mjs`, 84 checks. A real `createPlayer` rig
  walked by the real controller, in main.js's own frame order. Standing, the
  hips sit at 0.875 m; a second into a sit they are at 0.366 m, **50.9 cm down**;
  one frame of walking ends it, says "you get up", and twenty frames later the
  hips are back at 0.876 m with the gait in charge. All five movement keys tested
  one at a time. A dance ended by a swing, by a real wolf blow through the real
  resolver, by a cast, and by dying, each with its own sentence. All eight poses
  measured on the body for a distinct head and hand position.
- `src/game/player.test.mjs`, 108 checks, up from 78. The eighteen cleared
  channels, the sit and lie geometry, the three second ground sweep, and a proof
  that a run posed after an emote is bit for bit the run it would have been.
- `src/game/wiring.test.mjs`: the frame order and the build order strings were
  updated to carry `emotes`. Those two expectation strings are the only lines
  changed in a file this agent does not own.

## Not verified

- **Nothing was run in a browser.** Every pose number above comes from world
  matrices on a real rig in node, which is where the geometry lives, but how the
  eight read at 5 m with a camera and a light on them is Fable's to confirm with
  screenshots.
- **The paper doll shows the emote.** `paperdoll.js` reparents the LIVE rig into
  its own scene to draw the character sheet and the HUD portrait, and does not
  re-pose it, so opening the sheet while lying down draws a lying down portrait,
  framed by a camera that expects a standing one. That is existing behaviour
  meeting a new pose rather than a change, and it is unmeasured.
- **Gear on a posed body.** A weapon is parented to `handR` and follows the arm,
  so a wave with a sword raises the sword. No ray was fired at a sitting body to
  check that a slung bow or a cloak still sits where it should.
- **The slash commands.** `EMOTE_COMMANDS` is exported and tested as a table.
  Nothing in the running game calls it, because there is no chat line to call it
  from.
