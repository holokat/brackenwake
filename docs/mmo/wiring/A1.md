# A1: trees

Written 2026-09-06. Files: `src/world/arbor.js`, `src/world/arbor.test.mjs`,
`src/world/flora.js`, `src/world/flora.test.mjs`. `arbor_textures.js`,
`tree_gen.js` and `grass.js` were read and not changed; why is at the bottom.

The brief was the user's: "I dont really like our trees either, they look ok at
times and other times too many, doesnt feel organized and some have leaves on
the bottom and none of the top, totally messed up."

That is three complaints and they had three separate causes.

## 1. Leaves on the bottom and none at the top

This was real, it was measurable, and it was one species class.

A spruce grew a leader of `0.34 x h`, hung every one of its whorls on THAT axis,
and stacked the remaining two thirds of its height out of apical continuations.
The whorls, and therefore every needle in the tree, lived in the bottom third.
Measured on the shipped code, leaf anchors in ten bands up the trunk:

```
spruce before   36  34  16   4   2   3   1   2   1   0     8% in the top half
spruce after     0  16  21  14  10  10   8  10  10   2    38% in the top half
```

The lowest needle stood 2.29 m UNDER the ground. Spruce and fir are 60% of the
boreal mix and all of the snow, so two whole biomes were upside down.

Three other species had leaves below the turf as well: willow at 2.62 m under,
sakura at 1.81 m, fig at 1.12 m. The cause there was different, and that is the
point: this was a class, not a case.

### What the class was

`growAxis` decided where a tree branched from a two case table keyed off its
habit, and every species took whichever line it fell into. A conical tree got
its whorls on the first axis and nothing else did; every round tree got its
lowest limb at 35% of a bole that was 40% of its height, which is a seventh of
the way up. Nothing anywhere held a floor under the canopy.

### What replaced it

Every species now says four numbers out loud, and `auditForestTypes()` throws at
import if one of them is missing or nonsense:

| field | what it is |
|---|---|
| `bole` | the first axis as a fraction of the height. On a conical habit this is now the WHOLE leader, 0.9, because a spire is one trunk with branches hung off it. |
| `latFrom` | where the lowest lateral sits on the first axis. `bole x latFrom` is the bare trunk the player sees: a pine's 0.52 x 0.82 puts its first limb at 43% of its height. |
| `latDeep` | laterals per axis past the first, as a fraction of `lat`. A spruce carries 6 to a whorl and 2 off each of those; without it the whorls square up and the axis cap is spent before the leader is finished. |
| `crownBase` | the fraction of the height under which no leaf is ever emitted. |

`pushLeaf` is the only place a leaf is written, so it holds two floors: the
anchor must clear `crownBase x h`, and the whole quad must clear
`LEAF_GROUND_CLEARANCE` (0.16 m). A leaf that fails either is dropped and
counted into `proto.leavesDropped`.

Two other things in `growAxis` changed for the same reason:

- **Laterals are emitted top down.** `AXIS_CAP` is a hard stop and whatever it
  cuts is the tail of that loop. Going up the trunk meant a capped tree lost its
  crown and kept its skirt. Going down, it loses its lowest branches, which is
  what a tree in a wood looks like. Driven in the test with `lat` at 9: the cap
  is hit at 1,423 axes and 90% of the leaves are still in the top half.
- **`detail` no longer moves a branch.** It used to multiply the leaf loop
  count, so a tree grown at detail 0.4 consumed a different number of random
  draws and every branch after the first leaf came out somewhere else. The shape
  measured at detail 1 was not the shape flora.js shipped: sakura at its shipped
  detail had a quarter of its blossom in the bottom fifth of the tree and a
  tenth of it at detail 1. Every leaf is now still DRAWN and only some of them
  built, so the same tree grows at every detail and the leaves that survive are
  a strict subset of the full canopy. The test proves both.

## 2. Silhouettes

Each species has to be recognisable at 100 m. `arbor.test.mjs` grows all eleven
at four seeds each, at the detail flora.js actually ships, and measures three
numbers per species: how much of the canopy is in the top half, how far up the
lowest leaf is, and crown radius over height. Crown coverage is 1,200 rays a
species, fired inward at the tree's own branch triangles from three crown radii
out, counting the ones a leaf stops before they arrive. That is the number
behind "dense enough to hide the branch scaffold".

A negative "lowest leaf" is a leaf under the ground. There are none now: the
lowest vertex of every species, every seed, clears 0.16 m.

The spruce's coverage fell 4 points and its crown narrowed from 0.43 to 0.31 of
its height, both on purpose: it was a wide bush and it is a spire.

Willow's 51% in the top half and 9% crown base are also on purpose. Its whips
are meant to sweep the grass, so its `crownBase` is 0.04 and the ground
clearance is all that holds them up. The test judges it against its own numbers
rather than a broadleaf's, and so does the conical habit, because a cone
legitimately carries most of its needle area low down.

The three bands have to agree about where the canopy is, or the tree changes
shape as the player walks toward it. The mid band is held to the tenth: no tenth
more than 6 points off the near band, every species. The far band keeps 24 quads
whatever grew, so one quad is 4.2 points of any tenth and holding it to the
tenth would only measure the quantisation; it is held to the two numbers a
silhouette at 140 m is made of, the share in the top half and the middle of the
canopy.

`TRI_BUDGET` and `LOD_BUDGET` are tree_gen's and are untouched; `tree_gen.test.mjs`
still passes, and so does flora's per biome near band budget (below).

## 3. Not organized

The reference thinned one fbm field against one threshold. That is a fog of
trees with soft holes in it: no stand has an edge, no clearing has a shape, and
a meadow and a wood differ only in how thick the fog is. Measured: 1% of a
meadow had no tree within 25 m of it.

A stand is now a place. `arbor.STANDS` gives each forest type a `cover`,
`grove`, `hedge` and `lone`; the world is cut into cells of `grove x 2.2` metres
and a live cell holds one stand with a jittered centre, a radius, a bearing, a
species roll of its own, and either a round grove or a long thin hedgerow.
`standAt(type, x, z, seed)` takes the strongest stand over a point and returns
how much of it is there, 1 at the heart falling to 0 over the last 30 m.
Everything comes out of that one number: whether a tree grows, how old it is,
and what species it is. It is a pure function of the seed and the place, so two
chunks meet along a stand's edge without either knowing the other exists.

Two things this needed elsewhere:

- **The placement grid is now one lattice over the whole world.** It used to
  start afresh at each chunk's corner, which put five cells of 13 m into 64 m
  and left the spacing between two chunks a metre out. Harmless while a tree was
  a roll against a threshold; not harmless at all once a lone tree in the open
  is "the one cell of its lattice square that may grow one".
- **`mixRoll`**, an independent roll per cell, so how often a tree takes its
  stand's species and which species it takes are not decided by the same number.

Trees per 64 m chunk, on the real field, the flat scatter against the stands:

```
meadow     14.0 ->   2.3        boreal     38.1 ->  15.4
sakura     14.5 ->   4.9        beach       8.2 ->   2.9
mountain    5.2 ->   1.4        snow        5.2 ->   1.7
desert      4.5 ->   0.8
```

Open ground with no tree within 25 m of it, flat scatter against stands:

```
meadow  1% -> 36%    boreal  0% -> 10%    sakura   1% -> 17%
desert 16% -> 72%    mountain 16% -> 58%   snow  16% -> 61%    beach 16% -> 57%
```

The emptiest spot in 49 chunks of real meadow is 100 m from the nearest tree.
80% of a meadow's trees stand in one of its stands and 97% of a boreal's do.

Also measured and committed:

- **The edge.** A boreal stand thins over its last 30 m. Trees by metres inside
  the rim, in sixes: `158 391 718 952 1146 7489`.
- **Age.** Mean scale 0.94 over 8,635 trees at the heart of a stand against 0.74
  over 323 at the rim, and no tree outside the reference's own 0.55 to 1.5.
- **Hedgerows.** Of the meadow stands of four trees or more, 42 read as a line
  and 55 as a copse, measured as the aspect ratio of the point cloud.
- **Lone trees.** 345 of them over 198 ha, 1.74 a hectare, on a 65 m lattice; no
  lattice square holds more than one, and 1.74 a hectare is fewer than the 2.78
  that "one per 60 m" works out at.
- **Stand purity.** `flora.STAND_PURITY` is 0.8: a grove of oak is a grove of
  oak with a beech or two in it. A tree on wet ground always draws for itself,
  so a bank in a cherry wood still grows willow.

### Roads

`ROAD_CLEAR` is `max(4, ROAD_HALF_WIDTH + 1)`, so it follows roads.js if A2 ever
widens a road. The old rule was `sampleAt().road > 0.15`, which is a strength and
not a distance: it works out at about 2.7 m from the centreline and it is zero
everywhere the grading has not run. `roadDistanceAt` gives metres, so the rule is
metres.

Measured over 529 chunks and 2,089 records: none within 4 m, closest 4.89 m. The
other direction, so the rule is not vacuous: 156 of 156 points taken on the
roads themselves are refused.

### Avenues

The Greenwold plants its roads: one beech each side every 18 m, `AVENUE_OFFSET`
metres off the centreline, which is `ROAD_CLEAR + 1.2` so a trunk is always
clear of the carriageway and always inside the distance `roadDistanceAt` can
see. `auditBiomeHarvest()` throws if those two ever stop being true, if a realm
on the avenue list does not exist in zones.js, or if it is planted with a tree
no biome grows.

Measured: 70 avenue trees over the Greenwold, all of them 4.85 to 5.54 m off the
centreline. The longest Greenwold road is 679 m and carries all 70, which is
10.3 per 100 m of a possible 11.1, with 34 pairs across the road and 35 gaps of
exactly one step along it. The same roads with every sample reporting a
different realm plant nothing at all.

## The budget

The near band per 64 m chunk, at each biome's own density, against the budget
the merge was given (250,000 Natural and Open, 500,000 Dense):

```
snow       1.4 trees   27,411      mountain   2.0 trees   31,199
desert     0.8 trees    6,043      meadow     2.8 trees   93,940
beach      3.6 trees   78,710      boreal    14.1 trees  227,481 of 500,000
sakura     4.9 trees  162,970
```

Meadow was 305,954 before, which was over the budget it was supposed to be
inside. Fewer trees, in stands, bought that back, and some of it was spent again
on `DETAIL`: every kind went up (oak 0.6 to 0.9, birch 0.5 to 0.85, spruce 0.5
to 0.7, and so on), because a canopy at half detail has half its leaves and
shows the sticks through it. Growing one prototype, warmed, costs 0.5 ms (palm)
to 10.6 ms (oak); the committed frame budget of 120 ms for a warming frame still
passes at 85 ms.

## Wiring Fable has to do

Nothing. No signature changed: `createFlora(scene, field, opts)`,
`flora.onChunk`, `flora.offChunk`, `flora.update(nowMs, x, z)`, `flora.treesFor`,
`flora.setGrass`, `flora.setWind`, `flora.windAt` and `recordsFor` are all as
they were, and `world_runtime.js` needs no edit.

Two things to know rather than to do:

1. `flora.js` now imports, read only, from two files other builders own:
   `roadsForCell`, `roadDistanceAt`, `ROAD_HALF_WIDTH` and `ROAD_REACH` from
   `roads.js` (A2), and `ZONE` from `zones.js` (V1), plus `SITE_CELL` from
   `sitegrid.js` (A3). If any of those names moves, flora stops importing.
   `auditBiomeHarvest()` runs at import and names the Greenwold explicitly, so a
   realm rename in zones.js fails loudly here rather than quietly unplanting the
   Kingsroad.
2. `AVENUE_KIND` in flora.js is `{ greenwold: 'beech' }`. Every other realm is
   one line: add the realm to `AVENUE_REALMS` and its tree to `AVENUE_KIND`, and
   the audit checks the tree is one a biome grows.

## What was not done, and why

- **`grass.js` is unchanged.** It already refuses to grow on a road
  (`sampleAt().road > 0.20`, about 2.5 m from the centreline) and its own suite
  passes untouched. Blades stand in the outer half metre of the carriageway,
  which reads as a dirt road with grass at its edges rather than as a bug, so I
  left A2's road surface to A2.
- **`tree_gen.js` is unchanged.** The only thing it still grows is the cactus,
  which has no canopy and none of these problems. `TRI_BUDGET`, `LOD_BUDGET`,
  `LODS`, `LOD_RANGE`, `lodForDistance` and `geometryVariant` are all still its,
  still exported, and `tree_gen.test.mjs` passes as it did.
- **`arbor_textures.js` is unchanged.** The bug was geometry, not pixels.
- **Nothing was seen in a browser.** Every number here is from node. The bark
  and leaf sheets are stubbed in the tests, so the colours, the alpha cut and the
  wind on screen are the same code as before but are not verified by me.
- **The world moved under this work.** V1 is editing `field.js` and `zones.js`
  at the same time, so the per biome chunk counts above are against the terrain
  as it stood on the afternoon of 2026-09-06 and will drift as relief lands. The
  shape numbers, the coverage and the stand geometry do not depend on the
  terrain and will not.
