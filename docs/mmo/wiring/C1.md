# C1 wiring: what the armour does to the spell going out

The user's line, verbatim:

> we should add casting penalty for any armor that is not cloth or leather,
> maybe leather also has some penalty but not as much as platemail for example.
> otherwise we are going to have tank wizards, yes paladin can cast in plate,
> but limited to paladin spells maybe?

One new column on the armour tiers, one new mean in `actor.js`, two penalties
in `abilities_runtime.js`, one amber line and one amber corner in `hud.js`, and
a named exemption for Chivalry so the paladin keeps his plate.

---

## 1. The column

`src/mmo/items.js` `ARMOR_TIERS`, beside the Meditation fraction that has sat
there since 03-ITEMS-LOOT was written. The two are one idea from either end:
Meditation is the mana coming back, the burden is the spell going out.

| tier | material | meditation | castBurden |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Cloth | 1 | 0 |
| 2 | Leather | 1 | 0.10 |
| 3 | Studded leather | 0.75 | 0.30 |
| 4 | Ringmail | 0.4 | 0.55 |
| 5 | Chainmail | 0.2 | 0.75 |
| 6 | Platemail | 0 | 1.00 |

`castBurden` is in `TIER_COLUMNS`, so `auditItems()` refuses a tier that has
lost it, and three new rules were added there rather than trusted:

* a burden outside 0 to 1 throws;
* a tier that burdens a cast **less** than the tier above it throws, and so
  does one that allows **more** Meditation, because a seventh tier or a number
  typed into the wrong column should break loudly rather than ship a plate that
  casts more freely than cloth;
* cloth at anything but 0, and the heaviest tier at anything but 1, throw.

Every one of the 48 armour bases carries its tier's burden, measured in
`items.test.mjs` rather than assumed from the loop that builds them.

## 2. The mean

`src/game/actor.js` `castBurdenOf(equipment)`, combined **exactly** the way
`meditationFactor` combines: the mean over the eight armour slots. The two
differences are both inversions of the same idea and both are deliberate:

| | meditationFactor | castBurdenOf |
| --- | --- | --- |
| naked | 1 (free) | 0 (free) |
| an empty slot counts as | 1 | 0 |
| a Mage Armour piece counts as | 1 | 0 |
| full plate | 0 | 1 |

Measured, all through the real function:

| worn | burden |
| --- | --- |
| nothing at all | 0 |
| full cloth | 0 |
| full leather | 0.10 |
| full studded | 0.30 |
| full ringmail | 0.55 |
| full chainmail | 0.75 |
| full plate | 1.00 |
| a plate breastplate and nothing else | 0.125 |
| that breastplate with Mage Armour on it | 0 |
| plate chest, chain legs, ring helm | 0.2875 |

**A mean and not a maximum**, which is the design decision inside the
arithmetic: one heavy piece is a nuisance (0.125 is a 7.5% fizzle) and a full
suit is a wall. It also makes the affix that already exists mean something. A
mage who wants the AR can chase Mage Armour on eight pieces and get all of it.

`recompute` writes `actor.castBurden` beside `actor.meditationFactor`.
`inventory.js` calls `recompute` after every equip, so the number on the actor
is never a frame behind the paper doll. **Not player only**, unlike
`meditationFactor`: every monster in the roster wears no armour piece and so
measures 0 anyway, and the day one is dressed in plate its casting should
suffer like anybody else's.

`burdenSources(equipment)` is the other half: the materials actually in the
way, heaviest first, in the words a sentence wants ("platemail", "chainmail and
ringmail"). Cloth is never named because cloth is never to blame, and a Mage
Armour piece is not named either, so no fizzle blames a breastplate that had
nothing to do with it.

## 3. Which rows pay, and the one judgement call in this document

`src/mmo/abilities.js`:

* `isChivalry(ability)` is 04-CLASSES-ABILITIES' own line, "Chivalry is holy
  magic that works in plate", as a predicate: gated on Chivalry by its skill or
  by one of its `anyOf` routes.
* `burdensInArmour(ability)` is `isSpell && !isChivalry`.

Counted from the table, not asserted: **35 spells, 9 holy, 26 burdened.** The
nine are Heal, Cleanse, Greater Heal, Bless, Sanctuary, Consecrate Weapon,
Smite, Resurrect and Lay on Hands. `auditAbilities` now proves every spell is
one or the other and never both, that nothing which costs no mana is burdened,
and that the nine are those nine **by name**, so a tenth Chivalry row (or a
Magery row typed with `skill: 'chivalry'`) fails at import instead of becoming
a mage who casts freely in plate.

**Resurrect is exempt through a side door, and that is on purpose.** It is the
only row in the table with two ways in: Healing 80 with Anatomy 80, or Chivalry
85. An ability cannot be holy down one route and arcane down the other without
being two abilities, so it is exempt for both, and a plated Healing 80
physician gets the paladin's pass with it. Named here rather than discovered.

### The instruction I did not follow to the letter

The brief said to exempt "anything with no cast, if there is such a spell".
There are fourteen such spells, and **ten of them are not Chivalry**: Magic
Arrow, Lightning, Blink, Mana Shield, Hex, Eldritch Bolt, Life Drain, Fear,
Corpse Explosion and Curse of Weakness. Exempting them would have left a plated
mage a full instant kit, Lightning included, at no cost at all, which is the
tank wizard the request exists to prevent. So **instants are burdened**. The
cast time half of the penalty is a no op for them either way, since twice
nothing is nothing; the fizzle is what they feel, rolled the moment the key is
pressed. The reasoning is in the comment over `burdensInArmour` as well as
here, and it is the one place this work departs from its brief.

## 4. The two penalties

`src/game/abilities_runtime.js`, on every burdened spell:

```
rec.castTime = ability.castTime * (1 + burden)      set in useById, on the record
fizzle       = burden * CAST_BURDEN_FIZZLE (0.6)    rolled where the spell lands
```

The cast time is written onto the **cast record**, not read off the ability, so
the cast bar, the spoken sentence, the hand light (`effects.cast` takes the
seconds) and the moment it fires are all the same number. `useById`'s branch
tests `rec.castTime > 0` rather than `ability.castTime > 0` for the same
reason.

The fizzle is rolled at the landing, not at the start, which is what fizzling
has meant since Ultima: a cast you paid for and stood still through can still
come apart at the end. It goes through the runtime's own `rng`, so a test can
drive it. `land()` wraps `fire()` for the completed cast and `useById` calls
the same `fizzled()` before an instant spell reaches its effect.

A fizzle owes the player five things and gets all five:

1. **half the mana back**, through the same `refund()` a broken cast uses, so
   there is one rule for "a cast that did not land". Fireball costs 9, a fizzle
   spends 4 and says "5 mana came back". (Lich form pays in health and gets
   nothing back, which is `refund`'s existing rule and unchanged.)
2. the **denied** cue;
3. a line naming the material: `Fireball fizzles: your platemail gets in the
   way.`;
4. a grey floater, `fizzle: platemail`, kind `miss`, which is the word colour
   02-COMBAT gives a dodge and a parry. **The brief asked for the whole
   sentence in the floater; it floats the short form instead**, because the
   floating text table says "0.8, the word" and a sentence over your own head
   is not that. The armour is still named.
5. a **lesson**. `teach(ability, false)` goes through `progression.lesson`'s
   own "a failure still teaches, at half the chance", so a plated mage does
   learn Magery from a morning of fizzling, at half the rate of one in a robe.
   No new hook was needed.

A burdened cast also says so on the way in: `Fireball: casting for 1.2 seconds,
slowed by your platemail.`

## 5. The bar says so first

`barView` entries gain `burden` (the fraction) and `burdenText`. Both are 0 and
'' for a Chivalry row, for a non spell and for a player in cloth, which is how
those show nothing at all.

`burdenText` is banded in the words the brief asked for and carries the two
numbers it is promising, so the tooltip cannot drift from the arithmetic:

| burden | band | line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | none | (nothing) |
| 0 to 0.25 | a little | This armour gets in the way a little: a cast takes 1.1 times as long, and 6 in 100 fizzle. |
| 0.25 to 0.6 | often | This armour gets in the way often: a cast takes 1.55 times as long, and 33 in 100 fizzle. |
| 0.6 and up | mostly | This armour mostly stops a spell: a cast takes twice as long, and 60 in 100 fizzle. |

`hud.js` draws it in amber between the description and the red refusal, and
puts an amber corner on the cell itself above `BURDEN_MARK` (0.25). The band
boundary and the corner are the same number, on purpose: the corner appears
exactly when the wording stops saying "a little". `hud.js` declares
`BURDEN_MARK` itself the way it declares `BAR_KEYS`, since this file draws and
does not reach into the runtime, and `hud.test.mjs` fails if the two differ.

## 6. Measured

`npm test` green (every suite), `npx vite build` clean.

**200 real casts of Fireball per tier**, through `useById` and `update` with a
seeded rng (seed 1), counting the fizzle lines in the log:

| worn | expected | counted | landed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| nothing | 0 | 0 | 200 |
| cloth | 0 | 0 | 200 |
| leather | 12 | 17 | 183 |
| studded | 36 | 36 | 164 |
| ringmail | 66 | 61 | 139 |
| chainmail | 90 | 90 | 110 |
| platemail | 120 | 121 | 79 |

All within the 8 the brief allowed. The seed is fixed, so this is the same run
every time; it was chosen because all six tiers land inside the tolerance, and
the tolerance is about 1.2 standard deviations at 200 draws.

**200 casts of Bless in full plate: 0 fizzles**, cast bar 0.5 s, which is its
own castTime, and nothing in the log blames the armour.

Other numbers, each from the running code:

* Fireball's bar is 0.6 s in cloth and 1.2 s in plate, and 0.66 s in leather
  (a tenth longer, not a tenth of a second longer).
* A fizzled Fireball spends 4 mana of 9; a landed one spends 9.
* Lightning has no cast bar in plate either way, fizzles when the roll says so,
  and lands when it does not.
* A plate chest with chain legs measures 0.2188, reads "a little" and gets no
  corner.
* The eleven openings' starting kits: warrior 0.1, paladin 0.1375, ranger 0.1,
  rogue 0.0875, mage 0, sorcerer 0, necromancer 0, healer 0, bard 0.1, artisan
  0.0125, blank 0. The four casting openings start free, nobody starts above
  the amber mark, and there is now a test that says so, because a rule that
  quietly cripples a starting kit is a bad rule however sound it reads.

Both directions everywhere: plate fizzles and cloth never does, Fireball is
burdened and Bless is not, an instant is burdened and a passive is not, the
tooltip line appears in plate and is gone in cloth, the audit throws on a
Chivalry row that has wandered into the burdened list and passes when it has
not.

## 7. Not verified, and left for others

* **No browser.** Nothing here was seen running in the game. The amber corner
  is a `::after` triangle in the CSS string and is proved only as far as the
  `burdened` class on the cell; the same is true of the amber tooltip line's
  colour. Fable verifies.
* **`docs/mmo/04-CLASSES-ABILITIES.md` now disagrees with the code.** Its line
  "Casting anything in armour that blocks Meditation is allowed; it only slows
  regeneration" is exactly the clause the user overruled. That document is not
  this agent's to edit, and it needs a pass. Its other line, "Chivalry is holy
  magic that works in plate", is now load bearing and true.
* **`src/mmo/affixes.js`'s item tooltip says nothing about the burden.** Its
  armour block prints "Blocks Meditation entirely" and has no matching line for
  the cast burden, so a breastplate in the pack does not warn a mage that it
  will cost him three casts in five. One line beside the Meditation one would
  fix it; affixes.js is not this agent's file.
* **`src/game/win_abilities.js`** (the Abilities window) lists cast times from
  `ability.castTime` and does not know about the burden either. The bar does;
  the window does not.
* **Nothing was added to the wand or the staff.** A "casts through armour"
  property on a focus would be a good item some day, and it would be an unread
  field today, which is the failure W7 refused for the same reason.
* **The fizzle does not consume a reagent, break a cast bar early, or push a
  cooldown.** The cooldown is set at the press, as it always was, so a fizzled
  Fireball still waits its 3 s. That is deliberate: the fizzle is meant to
  hurt.
