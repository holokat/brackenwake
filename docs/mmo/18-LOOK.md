# The look book: what a zone has to feel like

The user's brief, 2026-09-06, given as screenshots of World of Warcraft and
one sentence: "they look like actual themed zones and have totally different
feel to them, this is what I'm after, not a generic looking simulator. Needs
to feel like a game and follows our storyline." Every builder that touches the
world reads this before building. The realms sheet in `src/mmo/realms.js`
says what each place is; this says how a place has to read.

## The rules the screenshots teach

1. **A zone is a bowl.** Every WoW zone is ringed by mountains, with passes
   where the roads leave. From anywhere inside you see a silhouette of ridges
   against the sky. The horizon is never flat. Ours: rim ridges per realm,
   irregular borders, passes at the roads, coast where the sea is.
2. **The sky belongs to the zone.** Purple over the snow fortress, ochre haze
   over the desert, blue fog in the graveyard, warm gold over the farmland.
   The sky, fog and light change at the border and the change is felt.
3. **Something authored every forty metres.** A fence, a signpost with a
   lantern, a fallen column, a rib cage, a cart, a well, a shrine. Open
   country is never empty; it is the space between things.
4. **Roads are the spine.** Cobbled or worn, with lamp posts at dusk, signs
   at forks naming where they go, a bridge at every river (rope, timber,
   stone, per realm), a carved gate at the zone line, a shrine at the
   crossroads.
5. **Structures are large and few, and they have a purpose in the story.**
   A temple with a colonnade and steps. A tower with a wizard at the door.
   A titan fortress on a crag. A ruined colonnade with glowing lamp posts
   in a purple wood. A castle with a keep. Each one is somebody's, and the
   sheet says whose.
6. **Camps are lived in.** A tent, a campfire burning, a weapons rack, a
   target dummy, crates, a bedroll, a lookout post, a palisade of sharpened
   logs, a log cabin with a lit doorway. Bandits sit at the fire until you
   come.
7. **Graveyards are places.** Rows of headstones in long grass, an iron
   fence with a gate, a mausoleum, a dead tree, fog, one great tree over it
   all.
8. **Farmland reads as farmland.** A farmhouse with a windmill, a fenced
   field, a haystack, a cart, a scarecrow, a dirt track to the road, smoke
   from the chimney.
9. **Towns are dense, tall and lit.** Timber framed houses two and three
   storeys, steep roofs, lanterns on brackets, barrels and crates by the
   doors, cobbles, a tower or a spire above the roofline, banners on lines
   across the street, windows glowing at night.
10. **Underground has structure.** A mine is trestle bridges, rails, carts,
    props, ledges and a shaft of light from above; a cavern has height, a
    lit chamber and a way down.
11. **Trees are silhouettes.** A pine is a spire with layered boughs, an oak
    is a round crown on a thick trunk, and forests are stands with clearings
    and edges, not a spray.
12. **Ore glows.** A vein is a crystal cluster with a sparkle you see from
    thirty metres. Things worth walking to announce themselves.
13. **The map is a painting.** Parchment, hatched ridges for the borders,
    stippled canopy, blue water, dashed roads, every place named, a banner
    with the zone's name.

## The one test

Stand anywhere in a realm and ask: could this be any other realm? If yes,
the realm is not done.
