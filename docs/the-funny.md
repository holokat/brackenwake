# Making people laugh

## What actually gets shared

Nobody screenshots a joke. They screenshot **a game that appears to have an
opinion**. The laugh comes from realising something was noticed, remembered, or
committed to far past the point of reason, and that the game never once winks
about it.

So: no gag text, no comedy fonts, no self-aware humour. The valley plays it
completely straight. That is the joke.

Three rules that make the difference between funny and try-hard:

**Persistence beats punchline.** A one-off gag is spent the first time. A goat
that has eaten eleven specific things over four seasons is a story the player
tells other people.

**Never acknowledge the bit.** The moment the game says "haha, that goat!" it
dies. The goat eats a fence post. The fence post is gone. That is the whole
event.

**Comedy sits next to consequence.** The funniest version of anything here also
costs or gives something real. A joke that is only a joke feels like filler.

# The big five

If only a handful ship, these. Each is a MECHANISM, not a gag, so it keeps
producing new moments after the first laugh.

## 1. Gary

One chicken is wrong. The game picks a chicken, quietly names it, and from then
on that chicken is where it should not be.

On the barn roof. Inside a closed pen you never opened. On the far side of the
river. Standing on the market stand at 3am. Once, and only once, on top of the
windmill, turning.

Rules that make it work rather than be irritating:
- Gary is never in danger and never dies. Foxes ignore Gary. Wolves ignore
  Gary. If Gary is somehow taken, Gary is back the next morning, unbothered.
- Gary never blocks anything or costs you anything.
- **The game never mentions Gary.** No toast, no card, no achievement. The name
  appears in exactly one place: hover over that chicken and the tooltip says
  `Gary` instead of `Chicken`.
- Gary appears roughly once every couple of hours of play. Rarity is the
  entire point.

The payoff is the player screenshotting a chicken on a roof and captioning it
themselves. That is the shareable unit.

*Cost: small. Pick one chicken record, add a teleport-on-a-long-timer to a
curated list of implausible spots, exempt it from predator targeting.*

## 2. The scarecrow gets worse over time

Buy a scarecrow. It works. Birds keep away.

Then one bird lands on it. Then two. After a season there is a bird on each arm
and one on the hat. After two seasons there is a nest. By the end there are
visibly more birds in that field than in any field without a scarecrow.

Never explained. Never fixed. The scarecrow's tooltip still reads "keeps birds
off your crops" the entire time.

If the player removes it, one card from Bram, and only if his reputation is
high enough that he would bother:
> "Saw you took the scarecrow down. Probably for the best."
> "It had become a sort of hotel."

*Cost: small. Bird count on a scarecrow scales with its placed-age. The joke is
entirely in not commenting.*

## 3. The dog brings you things

A dog on the farm occasionally leaves something at your gate. The list
escalates on a fixed order, so everyone who plays long enough sees the same
arc and can compare notes:

1. A stick. Just a stick.
2. A better stick.
3. One boot. Never the pair.
4. A fish. Wet. Recently alive.
5. A wheel. From something. There is no wheeled object on your farm.
6. Somebody else's dog's collar, name tag filed off.
7. A **live rabbit**, entirely unharmed, which then becomes a real bunny on
   your farm.
8. Sedge's hat. Sedge turns up two days later, hatless, and does not mention it.
9. The kettle, if you sold the kettle. It is the same kettle. It has been
   somewhere.

Each item is a real object placed at the gate, and the dog is visibly pleased.
The player only ever gets a toast reading, for example: `🐕 the dog has left you
a wheel.`

*Cost: medium. Needs a small gift queue on the dog and a handful of props. The
rabbit and the hat are the ones people will talk about.*

## 4. The goat eats your things

A goat on the farm will, occasionally and permanently, eat something.

Not crops. That would be a mechanic. It eats **decor**: a birdhouse, one plank
of a fence, the scarecrow's hat specifically, a flower bed, a lantern. The item
is gone. It comes back three days later, chewed, with a visibly worse model.

What makes this funny rather than infuriating:
- It only ever eats decor and never anything that produces or stores.
- It never eats the same thing twice.
- The goat is unmistakably the culprit: it is standing exactly where the item
  was, chewing, when you next look.
- The collection book quietly gains one page: **Eaten by the goat**, listing
  everything with the date. Nothing else in the game references that page.

If a player owns no goat, none of this happens. Someone will buy a goat
specifically because a friend showed them the page.

*Cost: medium. A timer, a decor picker, a swap to a chewed variant, one book
page.*

## 5. Stats you did not know were being kept

The collection book already counts sensible things. Add a second page that
counts things nobody asked for, presented with total sincerity, no framing text
at all:

```
Plots watered that were already wet          47
Longest unbroken stretch holding the hoe     11 minutes
Times you clicked the windmill               213
Fish that got away                           31
Times you chopped a stump                    18
Coins spent on things that do nothing        4,205
Nights your animals spent outside            9
Trees planted                                 2
Trees felled                                 96
```

The last pairing is the joke, and it is not labelled as one.

*Cost: small. The stats system already exists; most of these are one bumpStat
call. The restraint is in the presentation.*

# Animals with an interior life

## 6. Two animals who are inseparable

The game silently pairs two animals of different species. A cow and a duck. A
pig and a chicken. They stay within a couple of metres of each other for the
rest of their lives.

Sell one, and the other stops producing for a day. No message. No explanation.
The player works it out, and then has to tell someone.

## 7. The sheep all face the same way

Every sheep on the farm faces an identical direction. That direction rotates
about one degree a minute, all day, together.

It is never mentioned. It is never wrong. Occasionally, for around ten seconds,
one sheep faces the other way, and then quietly rejoins.

## 8. A cow that sits down

Not tired. Not hungry. It has decided. It sits in the most inconvenient
available spot, which is calculated, and it will not move for anything except
you clicking it, which produces a slow and clearly resentful stand.

## 9. The gate crowd

If a gate is closed and animals want in, they pile against it. Genuinely pile:
shoulder to shoulder, all facing the gate, in silence, until you open it.

This is just good simulation, and it will get screenshotted more than anything
deliberately written.

## 10. The pig has found something

The pig finds truffles. Occasionally the pig finds something else, digs it up,
and stands over it looking proud: a spoon, a coin from no currency in the game,
a very small key. The key does nothing. There is nothing it opens. It sells for
1 coin and appears in the collection book as **Small Key**, description:
"Fits nothing you own."

Somewhere much later in the game, a locked box comes up in the fishing junk
table. It is not the same key. It does not fit.

# Deadpan at exactly the wrong moment

The register break lands hardest when everything around it stays serious.

## 11. The windmill takes a lightning strike

During a storm, once ever, lightning hits the windmill. Full sound, full flash,
the works.

Nothing breaks. It just spins **backwards** now. Forever. No card, no toast, no
repair option. If the player clicks it, the usual windmill tooltip, unchanged.

## 12. Bram on catastrophe

Whatever the disaster, Bram's comment afterwards is one flat line, and it is
never about the disaster.

Bear charges the farm and wrecks a fence:
> "Fence was old anyway."

The barn burns down in a storm:
> "Well."

He is never unkind about it. He simply has a different sense of scale.

## 13. The market customers get specific

The order board already names customers. Give a handful of them a life that
shows only through what they order over time:

- **Old Willem** orders exactly three carrots. Every time. For the entire game.
  Never a fourth. Never anything else.
- **Beekeeper Ida** orders honey from you, which is strange, and the amounts
  slowly grow.
- One customer starts ordering ingredients that add up, over about ten orders,
  to a wedding cake. The last order is for the cake.

# Things the player did that the game noticed

## 14. Idle observations

Rare, harmless, and only ever when genuinely true:

Standing on the same spot with the watering can for a very long time:
> Everything within reach has been watered four times. The soil is as watered
> as soil gets.

Selling literally everything you own:
> Your stores are empty and your pockets are full. The barn echoes.

Owning fifty decor items and no storage:
> It is a beautiful farm. There is nowhere to put anything.

## 15. Naming your farm badly

Whatever the player writes on their sign, one customer misreads it once, and
uses the wrong name in an order. Only once. Never corrected.

# Running bits that pay off late

## 16. Sedge's cart

The cart is visible whenever Sedge is around. What is on it changes across the
game, and only ever gets stranger: sacks, then a chair, then a birdcage with no
bird, then a second cart wheel that is not attached to anything, then, very
late, a small mounted deer head that is looking at you.

He never sells any of it. It is not for sale. Ask and he changes the subject.

## 17. The bird that watches you fish

After roughly your twentieth cast, a single bird lands on the dock post. It is
there for every cast after that, for the rest of the game.

It never does anything. When you catch a big fish it is closer than it was.

## 18. One chicken survives everything

If a fox or wolf takes an animal, there is a chance it goes for Gary, fails, and
leaves. If you ever lose your entire flock, one chicken remains.

It is Gary.

# What to build first

**Gary**, and nothing else, in the first pass. He is cheap, he needs no art, he
generates screenshots, and he proves whether this register works at all before
anything expensive gets built on it.

Then **the stats page**, because it is nearly free and the trees planted vs
trees felled line will land immediately.

Then **the scarecrow**, which needs no new art, only a bird count that goes the
wrong way.

The goat and the dog are the expensive ones and should wait until the cheap
three have shown the tone is right.

## The line not to cross

None of this may cost the player something they earned. The goat eats decor,
never produce. Gary never blocks a plot. The windmill still works backwards.

Comedy that punishes stops being funny on the second occurrence, and the player
remembers it as a bug.
